# **Management of AI Products - Class Reflection**

**1. Class Overview** This session asked whether AI should be used at all, before asking whether it can be. We worked through three trigger products, an agricultural advisory for small farmers, a UPI dispute assistant and Netflix recommendations, and used them to discuss value, the cost of being wrong, timing, human oversight and why people resist switching.

# **2. Key Concepts and Discussions**

- **Gate 0, Social Legitimacy:** Before any technical question, check standing and consent, whether vulnerable groups like children are involved, and whether the product relies on manipulation or surveillance. A product that fails here is dropped however accurate it is.
- **The Other Gates:** Value needs pre-AI and post-AI baselines. Viability covers quality, latency, cost and support. Learning has to be bounded, with owners and hard stops.
- **Appropriateness Ladder:** Rules, then search and analytics, then predictive ML, then generative or agentic. Start at the lowest rung that does the job. The nuclear-powered pencil sharpener was the example of building something only because we can.
- **Actionable Time Window:** A correct prediction is useless if it arrives after the user can act. A rain warning one minute before the rain does nothing for a farmer.
- **Tail Risk:** Good average accuracy can hide a rare failure that ruins a farmer's only crop of the season. Guardrails and escalation belong in the product itself.
- **Adoption and Dark Patterns:** Resistance is usually a rational calculation of switching cost. Products that make sign-up easy and cancelling hard were called out.

# **3. Important Insights**

- **Rubber-Stamp Risk:** When people keep seeing routine AI recommendations, they stop checking. In the professor's lending product the human and the AI decided separately, and a disagreement made the human justify their call.
- **Measurement Drives Behaviour:** "Tell me how you measure me, I'll tell you how I behave." A claims bot measured only on profit will deny every claim.
- **Where I Differ:** The ladder is harder to apply from the bottom in a conversational product, because the conversation is the product. I think it still applies inside the product, one piece at a time.

# **4. Practical Learnings**

- **Gate 0 on Infi:** Infi is for Class 6 to 12 students, so most Gate 0 questions apply to us. Some of our early decisions were Gate 0 decisions without the name: parental consent for students under 18, no paid randomised rewards of any kind, and dropping the jokey tone to share helpline numbers when a student writes something that suggests self-harm.
- **Streaks and Notifications:** Our spec says a broken streak should never shame the student. I want to check our notification copy against the same rule.
- **The Ladder Inside Infi and InfiNotes:** Checking a Sparks balance, reading a student's class from the profile and finding a chapter in an InfiNotes PDF are jobs for rules and search, and they should never depend on what the model says.

**5. Conclusion -** Gate 0 gave a name to decisions we had already made for Infi by asking what could go wrong for a 12-year-old using the app alone at night. My open question comes from the measurement line. What main metric should a children's companion use, so the team is rewarded for helping students without quietly being rewarded for keeping them on the app?
