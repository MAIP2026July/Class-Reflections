# Reflection: Dynamic Product Navigation, Framework Pragmatism, and Human Team Realities

**Session Date:** August 18, 2026
**Session Topic:** Framework Adaptation, The Moving Target Dilemma, Scrum as Stability Negotiation, Pricing Surplus Dynamics, and Evaluation Structure

## Core Takeaways & Concepts

The session opened with student reflections on traceability matrices, progress over raw features, and PMs as custodians of trust. It then focused on operating under volatility and complex team dynamics.

- **Custodians of Trust, Accountability vs. Responsibility:** Even a CEO answers to the board and stakeholders. PMs can delegate responsibility but never accountability. Decisions need reasonable documented evidence (including decisions not to act on a competitor move), not paper for its own sake.
- **Qualify Your Personas:** The instructor dislikes bare "user", "customer" or "business user". Qualify them by name or type (student, parent, faculty), education level, time and conditions of use. If users wear gloves or glasses, interface elements must work for them (a typing "F" becomes "D"). Gautam added that segment preferences can be tested with conjoint analysis, and that mandatory features are the baseline.
- **Frameworks as Dials, Not the Steering Wheel:** JTBD, Double Diamond, Opportunity Solution Trees, Lean Canvas, Porter's Five Forces, RICE, and MoSCoW are decision-support tools. Stacking them adds ceremony without clarity. The PM owns the decision, its expiry, and its consequences. The AARRR customer lifecycle (acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue) and the short lifecycle within one session also matter.
- **Transformation vs. Incremental:** A caterpillar becoming a butterfly is a step change (the caterpillar-to-butterfly speed difference is orders of magnitude). Be honest if your product is only incremental, even if you brand it otherwise. The instructor prefers "transformation using AI" to "AI transformation".
- **How You're Measured Shapes Behavior:** "Tell me how you measure me, I'll tell you how I behave." Rational people hit 5-10% instead of 20% to preserve something for next year. Expectation-setting matters (the early birthday gift analogy).
- **The "Moving Target" Analogy:** A product is like a missile fired at a moving target. Competitors also attack the same target, and customers can build the solution themselves (AI lowers dev cost and democratizes domain knowledge). Even with a static target you course-correct. Competition can come from anywhere (a student startup). Crypto ASIC mining being repurposed for AI is an example of fast shifts.
- **Changing Your Mind Credibly:** Be humble, admit when sales forecasts change (for example, to one-tenth), and explain the environmental reason so you're rational rather than flaky. If the product fails, stand in front and take the blame.
- **Feasibility Sanity Checks:** Ask the submarine-depth and aircraft-oxygen questions early (can the physics work?).
- **Scrum as a "Labor Negotiation" for Stability:** PM pivots cause whiplash ("turn right, turn left"). A sprint is a negotiated contract: flexibility between cycles in exchange for stability within one. In more stable environments, longer sprints work, but truly stable environments are shrinking. Swaminathan noted Scrum can fail for proof-of-concept work where outputs aren't predictable. Choosing two vs. three vs. four weeks is a negotiation, and you will refuse four if you wanted one.
- **Product vs. Project Manager, and Team Trust:** Products have continuity across versions, whereas projects end. A vision (like Steve Jobs's) needs a team on the same wavelength. Take your team along, for example by fighting HR for flexible rules or sharing equity. Trust is earned (would your people work for you at lower pay?).
- **Generalizing Products vs. Bespoke Projects:** A product serves *N* unnamed customers. Build a generic core, add a domain/industry layer, then bespoke customization (the ERP analogy). If one customer demands non-reusable work, say no or fund it separately.
- **The 80/20 Value Surplus:** Customers won't give you 100% of value. The fair balance is not 50-50. Start with a share that does not exceed 20% and test how much customers will share (refined as 15-25% in Class 7).
- **One-Way vs. Two-Way Doors:** Reversible decisions are decentralized for speed. Irreversible ones need pre-mortems and executive scrutiny.
- **AI Techniques Q&A:** A question on encoder-only, decoder-only, and encoder-decoder transformer variants got the reply that variants exist, each proven in some areas and not others.
- **Ethics and AI Use:** Ethical practice isn't optional. Use AI for PM artifacts (traceability, PRDs), but stay in control and avoid "AI slop".

## Workplace Applicability & Contextual Translation

- **The Bespoke Trap in Candidate Screening Systems:** Hiring managers constantly request custom screening rules or knockout questions. The layering model (core > domain > bespoke edge) maps directly: keep the core evaluation engine generic and handle exceptions through modular scripts or configurable templates.
- **The 80/20 Rule for Recruiter Tooling Adoption:** Recruiters must feel the bulk of the savings (the 80%) as faster shortlists. Mandatory tagging, prompt logs, and cleanup consume the surplus and collapse adoption.
- **Managing Sprint Whiplash:** Priorities change weekly with requisitions. Two-week increments protect contributors from context-switching while letting leadership reset direction at sprint boundaries.
- **Measurement Shapes Recruiter Behavior:** If recruiters are measured only on submittal volume, quality will drop. Metrics must reflect placements and quality, not just activity.
