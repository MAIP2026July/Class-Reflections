Session 1 Reflection
Date: 25 July 2026

Topic: Course Introduction, PM Role & The 3 Pillars of AI

Today's session gave me a different perspective on what a Product Manager actually does. Earlier, I mostly associated the PM role with planning, coordinating with different teams, managing timelines and gathering requirements. But I understood that the role goes much beyond that. A PM needs to take responsibility for the overall product and make sure that it is actually solving the problem it was designed for.
Since I come from a software development background, I generally tend to look at things from a technical perspective. However, this session made me realise that having a technically good solution doesn't necessarily mean that it is a good product.

My key learnings from the session:

1. Ownership is more important than authority:

One thing I found interesting was that a Product Manager may not have direct control over the engineering team, budget or technical decisions. Still, they are expected to take ownership of the product. This means understanding the situation, making decisions with the available information and getting everyone aligned towards a common goal.

2. Choosing technology is not the same as making the right product decision:

The database example was quite relatable to me. We might choose a very advanced technology because it offers better performance or features, but if the team finds it difficult to maintain, it may create more problems in the long run. I could relate this to AI as well. Choosing the most powerful AI model is not always the best option if it increases cost, response time or complexity.

3. Understanding the three pillars of AI:

I learnt that AI can be connected to product management in three different ways.
- Using AI to develop products and improve engineering processes.
- Using AI to make a PM's day-to-day work easier, such as research, analysis and summarising information.
- Integrating AI directly into a product to provide new features and capabilities.
Earlier, I mostly thought about AI from the perspective of building AI-based features and integrating models into applications. I hadn't really considered how much AI could also help in the product development process itself or in the daily responsibilities of a Product Manager.

Overall Reflection:

I found this session useful because it helped me connect my existing software development experience with the product management perspective. My biggest takeaway is that building something technically impressive is only one part of the job. Understanding the actual problem, making practical decisions and delivering something valuable to users are equally important.
