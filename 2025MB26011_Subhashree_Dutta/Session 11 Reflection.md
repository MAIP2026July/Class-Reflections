# **Session 11 Reflection**

**Date:** 24 October 2026\
**Topic:** Responsible AI, Bias, Privacy, Hallucination, Accountability & Risk-Based Launch Decisions

This session helped me understand that responsible AI should be considered from the beginning of product development, not added after launch. A technically accurate AI system can still create unfair outcomes, misuse personal data or generate misleading information.

One important learning was that AI bias can come from historical data, even without intentional discrimination. This makes fairness checks especially important in areas like recruitment, lending and identity recognition. I also realised that collecting data simply because it is available can create unnecessary privacy risks. Data should be collected and used only when it serves a clear purpose.

The discussion on hallucinations highlighted that an AI system should be able to admit when it doesn't know something. Abstaining, asking for better information or escalating to a human can sometimes be better than providing a confident but incorrect answer.

I also found the discussion on human oversight and accountability valuable. Having a human in the loop is not enough; responsibilities, review mechanisms and escalation paths must be clearly defined. Even when third-party AI vendors are involved, the product team remains accountable for the customer experience.

**My key learning:** Responsible AI is about understanding and managing the consequences of failure. Fairness, privacy, transparency and accountability should be built into product decisions, with launch choices based on risk rather than simply aiming for perfect accuracy.
