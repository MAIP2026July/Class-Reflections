# **Session 12 Reflection**

**Date:** 31 October 2026\
**Topic:** AI Product Economics, Value-Based Pricing, Target Costing, Unit Economics & Platform Strategy

This session helped me understand that building a good product does not automatically make it a profitable business. A product must deliver customer value while maintaining sustainable costs as usage grows.

One of my key takeaways was **target costing**. Instead of calculating development costs first and adding a profit margin, we should start with the value delivered to customers, understand their willingness to pay and work backwards to determine the cost we can afford.

I also learned that customers pay for outcomes, not technical inputs like tokens, API calls or computing resources. This makes it important to align pricing with measurable value. Similarly, requirements such as accuracy, speed and efficiency should be clearly quantified so that product success can be evaluated objectively.

The discussions on workflow visualization and the C4 model highlighted the importance of understanding system components, dependencies and data flows. PMs don't need to write the architecture, but understanding it helps them make better decisions around cost, scalability and reliability.

Another important concept was unit economics. More customers do not always mean more profit, especially when every AI interaction adds inference costs. Contribution margin helps determine whether the product is actually profitable. I also found the discussion on downselling interesting, as retaining a customer on a smaller plan can sometimes be better than losing them by pushing an expensive upgrade.

**My key learning:** Sustainable AI products need to balance customer value with the cost of delivering it. Pricing, architecture and unit economics should be considered from the beginning, not after the product is built.
