# Reflection: Session 9

10 October 2026 | Gangrade Pranjal Jitendra | 2025MB26023

---

The loan example was arithmetic I could follow and a conclusion I have been avoiding. A bank makes two and a half percent on a good loan of one lakh, and loses the principal on a bad one. One miss wipes about forty wins. Accuracy of ninety percent can still be a loss. The faculty's request to MBA students was not to argue precision versus recall. Put rupees in every cell and then decide.

We have been selling Helm as five hours of senior time cut to ninety minutes. That is the profit cell, and it is small. The loss cell is a watermelon engagement ranked low, skipped, and then lost. Recovery staffing, fee concessions, a renewal that does not happen. One of those wipes a year of time savings. Our own ethics note already said recall on at risk engagements matters more than precision. This session gave me the reason in money rather than in principle. If I cannot put a number on that loss cell, I should stop quoting the time saving as if it were the case.

**Who pays.** For a payment fraud, the bank only spent energy after the law put the cost on them. Until then there was expense and no gain. For us the person who pays for a false negative is the firm, later, at renewal. The person who pays for a false positive is the engagement manager, this week, in an uncomfortable conversation. Those two people are not the same, and the second one can starve the product of honest writing. Session 6 already had that. Session 9 added that whoever bears the cost is who will fund the improvement. Right now we have designed the product as if the RDM's time is the scarce thing. The scarce thing is the firm's retained revenue, and the engagement manager's willingness to keep writing useful status reports.

**The guitar.** A helmet camera fined a rider because a guitar on the back looked like a pillion without a helmet. Nobody on the test team thought of a guitar. I cannot list the guitars in our data. I can admit that the first embarrassing miss will be something we did not think to test, and that the launch blockers have to survive a miss we have not imagined, not only the ones we have.

**A promise, not a feature list.** Everything before first use is a promise. Reputation is the value actually derived. If Helm's weekly briefing becomes the way the region is run, the promise is not a ranked list. It is that quiet trouble will not be skipped. I had been describing the product as a briefing. That is a feature. The promise is the thing I should be able to say in one sentence to a delivery director.
