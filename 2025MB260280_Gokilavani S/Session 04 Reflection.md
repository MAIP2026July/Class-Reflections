Session 4 Reflection

Date: 22 August 2026

Topic: Deciding Whether AI Is Appropriate


Before this session, I used to judge an AI idea by checking whether it was technically capable of doing the task. Session 4 showed me that this is not the right starting point. What matters more is whether AI adds enough value to justify its cost and its risk.

We studied a ladder of options, beginning with deterministic rules, then analytics or search, then predictive machine learning, and finally generative or agentic AI. The lesson I took from it is to start with the simplest solution that solves the problem. When a checklist or a rule-based system is enough, a complex AI model only brings extra cost and unpredictable behaviour. Coming from a records department where a simple, reliable checklist often works best, I found this very easy to accept.

The agricultural alert example stayed in my mind. A prediction can be accurate and still be useless if it reaches the farmer after the chance to act has gone. This taught me that accuracy alone does not make a product successful. Timing, latency and the window in which the user can act are equally important.

I also learned how a good average can hide a dangerous problem. A model may be right most of the time and still fail in a few critical cases. When one such failure can cause a large financial loss, a safety issue or damage to reputation, the overall accuracy percentage is not enough. The product needs guardrails, fallback options and a clear path for escalation.

The discussion on human-in-the-loop was the most thought-provoking part for me. I had assumed that human approval automatically makes a system safe. In reality, when people see routine AI recommendations again and again, they slowly begin to approve them without proper checking. This is called automation bias. Human involvement has to be meaningful, especially when the situation is uncertain or the impact is high.

Finally, I realised that adoption is also a design problem. Even a valuable AI feature may be rejected if it disturbs the way people are used to working or adds too many extra steps.

I leave this session with a more careful mindset. Sometimes the strongest AI decision is to use less AI, and to design with the people who will actually live with it in mind.
