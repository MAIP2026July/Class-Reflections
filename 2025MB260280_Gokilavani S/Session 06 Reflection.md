Session 6 Reflection

Date: 5 September 2026

Topic: Workflow Redesign, Requirements and Data

The biggest lesson I took from this session is that adding AI to a weak process does not fix it. It may only make the same wasteful steps run faster. Before bringing in AI, we should question the existing workflow and, where needed, redesign it.
The black-box approach helped me see this clearly. Instead of accepting every input, approval and hand-off as necessary, we first ask what outcome the process is really meant to deliver. Then we redesign what happens in between. This gives us a chance to remove steps, not just automate all of them. In my own work, I have seen steps that continue only because "that is how it has always been done," so this idea felt very real to me.
The discussion on PRDs connected well with our earlier talk on defining the user. Requirements should be written from the point of view of a specific stakeholder, because an approver and an end-user may want very different outcomes from the same feature. Technical constraints should be recorded separately, and not presented as if they were the stakeholder's own need.
One small but practical point stayed with me. We should not delete a section from a product template just because it looks irrelevant. Writing down why it does not apply shows that someone thought about it, and it may help the team catch an important dependency later.
We also looked at build, buy and API-based approaches. APIs let a team launch quickly, but they can bring vendor dependency, unpredictable usage costs and data privacy worries. The right choice depends on how important the capability is, what expertise the team has, how much control is needed and how sensitive the data is. Domain-specific data can become a real advantage for a product, so it needs to be governed with care.
The examples on biased data made me stop and think. Bias is not always easy to see. A model can look accurate and still treat certain cases unfairly again and again. So good product design must look beyond technical performance and consider data quality, governance, trust and human behaviour.
I leave this session believing that a better product often begins with a simpler, fairer and better-questioned process, and only then with AI.
