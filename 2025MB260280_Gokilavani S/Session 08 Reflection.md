Session 08 Reflection

Date: 3 October 2026

Topic: AI Reliability, RAG, Prototyping, Progressive Automation and Product Governance

It's convenient to think that if an AI solution is efficient during the development process, the hard part is done. The primary reason for this session is that I learned how wrong this assumption be can be. When I read about what an AI system can do, I wanted to know that it was operating in the same way as it did in tests, despite no changes on our end, but that wasn't the case. But, constant monitoring on After deployment is not a late thinking. Made as part of the product.

The enforced reality check that was the discussion on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) was welcome. If looking good, then RA is easy, you might want to put AI in every corner of a product.If it looks good, it must be RA easy, maybe you want to use AI technology into every corner of a product. In situations where a lot of information needs to be extracted from a large knowledge base, RAG suits the need. Calculations, validations and anything that needs to be an exact same outcome every time, however, are better suited to APIs or rule-based logic. I believe the best of AI products are those that marry AI and old-school software by leveraging the abilities of each.

I also now have a very better understanding of Prototypes, Pilots and Scaling. First, a prototype can provide information about if an idea is workable or not. An introduction to the process by which real customers and real deadlines can wreak havoc on a pilot. What a clean demo looks like to a lot of people – can say a lot about its capabilities on a presentable demo, but little to nothing about its growth capabilities in the real world. What I will say is that I have a reaction to demos, I love seeing them, and I love these guys.

One piece of advice that stuck with me was to "Learn—Don't Automate. A team can't pass over an entire process on the first day. Believing poor players in the initial stage is where unplanned flaws occur, such as "quirky" players no one elicited in the design stage. It is important to give automation more of a role in the work once trust is established. Manual overrides and fallback routes are the backup for when a model gets into a state of not performing well.

I got myself ordered in the governance discussion. All but the very last stage must have a clear purpose in mind, and the team must be well aware of the things proving when it passes to the next stage. Otherwise, it's a real simple case of getting busy on features and thinking they're progressing.

What stuck with me is that as you're building an AI product you're slowly decreasing the uncertainty, testing and learning, and validating. The ultimate objective of full automation from the outset is not the aim. It's something the product gradually does as it evolves, something that for me is humbling and hopeful.
