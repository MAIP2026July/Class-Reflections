Session 11 Reflection

Date: 24 October 2026

Topic: Responsible AI, Bias, Privacy, Hallucination, Accountability and Risk-Based Launch Decisions

It's easy for Responsible AI to be a checklist that is to be checked off right before you go live. This session gave me an insight into the reality that this isn't sufficient. A model might perform well on all the technical measures and yet still be unfair/unsafe/unexplainable to the people it impacts. It isn't about running after the ball, it's about feeding forward toward the ball.

The bias discussion had me in pain. So a model learns from all the data it is provided, and if any of that data has old prejudices, so will the model, with much confidence. In a recruitment scenario, an automated decision can make or break a person's job, in a lending situation an automated decision can decide whether they do or don't qualify for a loan, and of course an automated decision making a person who does or doesn't qualify for identity verification?

As much as that is true of privacy and data minimisation. Mobile access to a customer's data is fine, but there is a benefit to it. Before any data is gathered a team should be able to discuss in depth what it is used for, how it will be used in the system, and whether it exposes without providing real value. When you have less data carefully managed there will be fewer “things” that can go wrong.

I thought about one thing more than anything else – hallucination. Particularly there will be times when it is best for a system to say "I don't know" and a venturesome guess will do more harm than a sober pause. An ablity to obtain additional information or to have the case forwarded to an individual or follow a back-up plan, can make a product more reliable in real life, than in appearance.

In fact, I also got a more cautious attitude towards the oversight of man. It does not make a person (always…) taking his/her seat inside the workflow a system responsible. Real responsibility is evident in the details; a player of an awkward possession, a player embarrassed by a result, a person who suffers a bad result, someone who has to take the blame on a tough call. If there isn't a human in the loop, this person could amount to little more than a box to check on a form.

Leaving for the day, the most valuable lesson I learned is that Responsible AI requires thinking through the damage that a mistake might cause and planning that day ahead of time. As the product manager, I will have to consider the business value and model performance, then balance with fairness, privacy, transparency and risk in the decisions I make regarding the extent of the product's launch. It would seem a bit intimidating, but it is the right way to do it and that's what keeps people at the heart of the work.
