### 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways
* **Goodhart’s Law in AI Metrics:** When a proxy metric becomes the official target, it stops being an effective measure of success. Chasing superficial metrics like raw prompt count or daily token consumption can easily mask poor user experience or high frustration.
* **Automation Complacency vs Human Agency:** If an AI system is too automated without proper checkpoints, users either become overly reliant and miss critical errors, or they become distrustful and abandon the system entirely.
* **The Value of Meaningful Intervention Windows:** Rather than asking for confirmation on every trivial step, the product must smartly batch approvals and flag only high-impact anomalies where human judgment truly matters.
* **Measuring Time Saved Over Feature Usage:** The true measure of an assistant product is whether it saves the user time and cognitive energy, not how many minutes they spend clicking around inside the interface.

---

### 2. What Resonated Most With Me
Professor Vinaya's warning about vanity metrics really made me reflect on how software teams measure engagement. In traditional SaaS, high daily active usage and long session durations are considered positive signals. But in an AI assistant or delegation product, if a user spends 45 minutes tweaking prompts and reviewing every single draft, the product has actually failed them. 

The goal of our AI Twin should be minimal interaction with maximum trust—the user checks a short summary, approves two key items, and moves on with their day. Designing for "speed to value" rather than "time on screen" requires a very different product mindset, especially when presenting metrics to business leadership who are used to standard engagement funnels.
