### 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways
* **Beyond Feature Tracking:** In an AI-driven product, a PM's role cannot be reduced to managing Jira tickets or relaying stakeholder requests. We have to take direct accountability for the commercial viability and user adoption of what gets built.
* **Problem Definition Trumps Algorithmic Complexity:** The most sophisticated neural network is useless if it tackles a problem users are indifferent toward. The PM must spend significant energy validating the pain point before any modeling starts.
* **The Three Workstreams of Enterprise AI:**
  1. Internal productivity tooling (accelerating our own research and documentation).
  2. Software development workflows (code generation and test automation).
  3. External customer-facing value (the core product capability).
* **The Danger of the Middleman:** Falling into the habit of merely passing requirements between data scientists and business teams creates confusion and delays. As PMs, we must understand the core architectural trade-offs so we can make informed prioritization calls.

### 2. What Resonated Most With Me
Professor Vinaya's opening remarks on the "ownership mindset" immediately set the tone for the semester. When he challenged the class on why so many product managers act like passive coordinators rather than true product owners, it struck a chord with me. 

In my own career, I have often seen projects stall because nobody felt personally responsible for the end-to-end outcome—engineers blame vague requirements, business teams blame technical delays, and the PM sits in the middle acting like a courier. Professor Vinaya's insistence that an AI PM must act like an entrepreneur spending their own savings is a great mental model. With generative AI moving at a dizzying pace, waiting for someone else to tell you what to build is a recipe for irrelevance.

I also liked the emphasis on customer empathy. It is very easy to get seduced by the latest model release or impressive synthetic benchmarks, but if the end customer does not experience a tangible reduction in friction or cost, the product will fail in the market.
