## What the session covered

- **Pricing AI Products:** How value capture shapes product design
- **Cost of Inference:** When architecture becomes a finance problem
- **Unit Economics:** Contribution, retention and payback
- **Business Model Design:** Value capture logic
- **OpenAI API Pricing:** Pricing power, fairness and developer adoption
- **Monetization Risks:** Where revenue logic collides with trust

## Key ideas and cases

### Ideas that stayed with me

- **Inference cost is variable:** Unlike traditional software, every AI interaction costs money, so usage can grow faster than margin.
- **Pricing must match perceived value:** The pricing model should follow how customers see value and how costs actually accrue.
- **Elegant spreadsheets can fail:** Packaging that looks good on paper can damage trust, margin or adoption in practice.

### Cases that made it concrete

- **Midjourney subscriptions:** Simple subscription logic works when usage is predictable.
- **OpenAI API tiers:** Usage-based, tiered pricing aligns revenue with cost.
- **Klarna automation:** Support automation economics are judged by cost per resolved case.
- **Shopify AI bundling:** AI bundled into merchant workflows drives retention more than direct revenue.

## How my thinking shifted

| Before this session | After this session |
| --- | --- |
| AI features are priced like software | AI pricing must account for per-use inference cost |
| Charge for every AI feature | Some AI features earn more as retention drivers |
| Monetisation comes after launch | Monetisation is a product design decision from the start |

## Applying it to my work

- **Cost per transaction scored:** Calculate inference cost per risk decision and compare it to the fraud loss avoided.
- **Bundle vs charge decision:** Decide whether AI insights for merchants are a premium tier or a retention feature.
- **Margin stress test:** Model margins at 10x usage before committing to flat pricing.

## Key takeaway

> The best AI business models are legible to customers and sustainable for the operating team.

How do you price an AI feature whose cost per use keeps falling every quarter?
