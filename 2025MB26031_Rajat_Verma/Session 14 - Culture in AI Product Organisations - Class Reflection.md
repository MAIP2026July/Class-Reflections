Management of AI Products

## What the session covered

- **Culture as a Decision System:** Decision speed, challenge quality, escalation
- **Cross-Functional Trust:** Teams silently optimising different products
- **Open Innovation:** Learning from ecosystems without copying everything
- **Knowledge Loops:** Reusable learning that survives team churn
- **Leadership Levers:** Forums, incentives, resourcing and review quality
- **Measuring Culture:** Tracking how work moves, not only how people feel

## Key ideas and cases

### Ideas that stayed with me

- **Culture shows in decisions:** Culture is visible in decision speed, review quality and escalation behaviour, not in slogans.
- **Polite is not healthy:** Polite meetings can hide vetoes and late disagreement that slow the product down.
- **Learning must be written down:** If learning lives only in chat threads, the organisation repeats the same mistakes.

### Cases that made it concrete

- **Microsoft under Nadella:** A culture shift showed up in how product decisions were made and reviewed.
- **Open-source ecosystems:** Open innovation speeds learning but can create strategic dependency.
- **Postmortem rituals:** Blameless reviews turn incidents into reusable knowledge.
- **Review forums:** Leaders shape culture through the forums and incentives they design.

## How my thinking shifted

| Before this session | After this session |
| --- | --- |
| Culture is HR's responsibility | Culture is built through product operating mechanisms |
| Agreement in meetings means alignment | Real alignment shows in execution speed |
| Innovation culture is about inspiration | It is about forums, incentives and review quality |

## Applying it to my work

- **Decision memos:** Record major AI decisions, alternatives and dissent in a short memo.
- **Blameless postmortems:** Run a structured review after every model incident and share the learning.
- **Track decision cycle time:** Measure how long key product decisions take from proposal to commitment.

## Key takeaway

> PM leaders improve culture by redesigning operating mechanisms, not by waiting for inspiration.
