## What the session covered

- **Capstone Deliverables:** What makes a proposal worth funding
- **Product Strategy:** Evidence, market logic and differentiation
- **UX and Solution Design:** Workflow, UX and feasibility fit
- **Metrics and Feasibility:** Proving value and readiness
- **Risk and Governance:** Constraints and mitigations
- **Presentation and Defense:** What convinces evaluators

## Key ideas and cases

### Ideas that stayed with me

- **Why AI, why now:** A proposal must explain why AI is the right lever instead of a simpler product fix.
- **Three-way proof:** Success must be shown for the customer, the business and the operating team.
- **Legible beats exhaustive:** The PM is rewarded for making the proposal defensible, not for saying everything.

### Cases that made it concrete

- **Fintech AI feature proposals:** Must show risk controls as clearly as the upside.
- **B2B SaaS copilots:** Workflow fit decides whether the copilot gets used.
- **Support-assistant dashboards:** Quality and cost metrics together prove readiness.
- **Credit/hiring risk reviews:** High-stakes AI needs governance addressed up front.

## How my thinking shifted

| Before this session | After this session |
| --- | --- |
| A good pitch is a good story | A good pitch is a story backed by evidence |
| Put everything in the main deck | Main deck for the argument, appendix for support |
| Risks weaken a proposal | Openly addressed risks strengthen credibility |

## Applying it to my work

- **Capstone artifact stack:** Prepare the core deck, appendix, product brief, metric logic, feasibility and risk narrative.
- **Pressure-test with skeptics:** Rehearse the defence with colleagues from risk and engineering.
- **Reuse at work:** Use the same proposal structure for internal AI investment pitches.

## Key takeaway

> Turn a promising idea into a proposal that is legible, defensible and investment-worthy.
