# Session 2 Reflection

**Date:** 1 August 2026  
**Topic:** Product vs. Project, Product Lifecycle, Frameworks & Traceability  

The biggest takeaway today was the fundamental shift between a project and a product: *"A product is for $N$ unknown customers, while a project starts with one known customer."* A product might begin with a single specific problem, but if it isn't generalized for a broader user base, it’s still just a custom project.

**Key takeaways & thoughts:**

* **"Don't Pretend Certainty" & Deferring Decisions:** I loved the house construction analogy—structural decisions must be made early, but paint colors can wait. Deferring non-critical decisions until more information is available saves rework. However, the key is clear communication so stakeholders don't mistake an open decision for a final one.
* **Beta is Only Halfway:** The story about the credit card product launch (realizing settlement was missing right when they thought beta meant "done") was a great reality check. Pushing a team to hit a beta release as if it's the finish line burns everyone out before the hardest operational work even begins.
* **Traceability Over Paperwork:** We went through a firehose of PM frameworks (JTBD, RICE, Kano, QFD, Traceability Matrix). The core lesson was that frameworks are decision aids, not the decision itself. A decision made in a steering meeting that doesn't trace down to the architecture, sprint backlog, and testing is essentially lost.
* **AI-Enabled vs. AI-Native:** Bolting AI onto an existing workflow vs. building a product that wouldn't exist without AI are two completely different games. Also, an AI PM doesn't need to be a data science expert, but needs enough technical judgment to make trade-offs on latency, cost, and risk.
