# Session 3 Reflection

**Date:** 18 August 2026  
**Topic:** Qualifying Users, Progress vs. Features, Incremental vs. Transformation & AI Moats  

The biggest takeaway from today’s session was the core principle that *"progress is more important than features."* It’s easy to measure team productivity by feature velocity, but adding features doesn't automatically mean the product is moving forward. If a feature doesn't directly enable the target persona to achieve their intended outcome, it just adds unnecessary complexity.

**Key takeaways & thoughts:**

* **Qualifying "User" and "Customer":** We need to stop using "user" or "customer" as generic blanket terms. Different roles interacting with the same product (e.g., end-user vs. buyer vs. admin) have vastly different priorities. Failing to qualify exactly *who* we are designing for leads to solving one problem while creating three others.
* **Incremental vs. Transformation:** Loved the caterpillar-vs-butterfly metaphor. A 5% efficiency gain is valuable, but calling it a "digital transformation" is misleading. The discussion on corporate incentives was a reality check: teams often hold back incremental improvements across release cycles just to ensure they have deliverables to show in future appraisals.
* **Layering Core Products:** To prevent a scalable product from devolving into a custom project, software needs proper layering: a generic core at the bottom, a domain/industry layer in the middle, and minor customer-specific configurations at the top. Saying "no" to custom feature requests is essential to keep the core product intact.
* **The Shrinking AI Advantage:** With AI drastically lowering development costs and time-to-market, simply knowing *how* to build a feature is no longer a sustainable moat. If a customer can build a similar prototype using LLMs and API calls over a weekend, the PM must focus on deeper differentiators—like workflow integration, proprietary data, governance, and long-term defensibility.
* **Sprint Durations & Stability:** Sprint planning isn't just an agile ritual; it's a trade-off between team stability and organizational flexibility. Finding the right balance depends on how dynamic the product's external environment is.
