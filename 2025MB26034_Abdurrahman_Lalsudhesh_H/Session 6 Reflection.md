# Session 6 Reflection

**Date:** 5 September 2026  
**Topic:** Re-engineering Workflows, Stakeholder-Voice PRDs, Data Governance & Building "Category Killers"  

This session focused on moving from raw ideas to actual product design, highlighting a critical lesson: **AI should rethink and re-engineer workflows, not just accelerate bad existing processes.** If you place AI inside an inefficient process, you just get a faster inefficient process.

**Key takeaways & thoughts:**

* **Reimagining the "Black Box":** Instead of adding AI as an extra feature to a legacy pipeline, view the existing workflow as a black box. Question why inputs exist in their current form, strip away unnecessary steps, and redesign the internal logic completely.
* **Stakeholder-Voice PRDs:** Writing PRDs requires grounding requirements in the specific voice of the individual stakeholder (*"I, as a [specific role], want..."*) rather than using generic "user" labels. Technical constraints belong in system specifications, not in the stakeholder requirement itself.
* **Never Delete Template Sections:** When completing standard product templates, never simply remove non-applicable sections. Explicitly stating *why* a section is not applicable forces rigorous thinking and ensures critical dependencies aren't accidentally overlooked.
* **Build vs. Buy vs. API Trade-offs:** Utilizing external APIs offers rapid execution speed, but introduces token cost volatility, vendor lock-in, and strict data governance risks under regulations like the DPDP Act. Proprietary domain data should always be protected internally—it forms the primary defensible moat against competitors.
* **Data Bias & Hidden Emotions:** As seen in real-world failures like biased credit algorithms, dataset bias doesn't announce itself—it silently distorts outcomes and creates massive institutional risk. Furthermore, products fail when they ignore subtle human friction, emotional trust, and operational resistance.
