Session 10 Reflection:

1. AI Cost Is Part of My Job

My first learning from this session is that the cost of AI is no longer small, and as product managers we are responsible for it. I had honestly never thought of token usage as something I need to track. Even the AI we use during development has to give a return on what we spend.

2. Scaling Is About Keeping the Promise

I always thought of scaling as handling more users and more servers. What I understood here is that it is also about the promise we make to customers. Once people start depending on a product, their expectations go up very fast, often faster than the technology can keep up. A disclaimer in the terms and conditions will not protect us if customers expect more.

I also understood that the servers can be working fine while the customer promise is still broken. I think the SLAs we write at work are mostly technical, and they should say what we are promising the customer.

3. Quality, Speed and Cost

The most important idea for me was the trade-off between quality, speed and cost. If the product manager does not set limits, the team keeps switching between a cheap model and an expensive one depending on who complains loudest. I understood that the product manager should give a clear range for each, and the team can decide freely within it.

I also learned that faster is not always better. Sometimes customers prefer a response time they can predict. And a feature that impresses people today will be expected tomorrow.

4. Running the Product Day to Day

I understood that the whole team should be able to explain what the product promises, not just the product manager. Alerts should be on business outcomes and not only on system health. I also liked the point that different people in a team work in different ways. Some work in short intense bursts and some are slow and steady, and we need both. I think I have sometimes expected the same thing from everyone in my team.

5. Versions and Queues

I understood that AI versioning is more than software versions. Data, models and even which customers use which version all need tracking. When usage grows, we will hit rate limits and need queues, and maybe priority for premium customers. I am still not fully clear on how to plan all of this early, but I understand it cannot be left until things break.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to write down the promise of any product I work on in simple words and make sure the team can repeat it. I also want to set a clear range for quality, speed and cost instead of reacting to complaints, and keep in mind that if the economics are broken, growth will only expose it faster.
