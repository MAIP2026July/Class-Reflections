Session 11 Reflection:

1. Bias Comes From What We Have Seen

My first learning from this session is that bias usually comes from limited exposure, not from bad intentions. AI trained on limited data behaves the same way. I honestly think most of us, including me, are biased in ways we do not notice. I also understood that bias can never be removed completely, but being aware of it is the first step.

2. Fairness Has to Be Defined for Our Product

An important learning for me was that there is no single standard for fairness. We should follow the law and industry rules as a minimum, but we also need to define what fairness means for our specific product. Writing "we will ensure fairness" in a document means nothing. For some products the risk is very low, and for others like hiring or lending it is serious.

I also understood that it is better not to promise something we cannot guarantee.

3. Collect Less Data

The most practical idea for me was to only collect data that we really need. If we do not collect something, we cannot misuse it or be biased on it. I understood that consent and a clear purpose are now expected, that some data like health and children's data needs more care, and that customer data is held in trust and has to be deleted when they ask.

At work, I have seen forms asking for details at a stage where they are not needed. After this session I want to question that more.

4. Saying "I Don't Know" Is Fine

I understood that it is perfectly fine for an AI product to say it cannot answer, or to pass the question to a human. That is much better than a confident wrong answer. Even when humans are not part of the main flow, someone should regularly review how the product is actually performing. I also had not thought before about people deliberately trying to fool an AI product.

5. Governance That Actually Works

I understood that governance should keep pace with the product and not become a bottleneck, otherwise people start hiding things from it. The idea I liked most was to keep two lists for a launch: the minimum we need to launch, and the things that would block it. Anything in between is a management call. I think this is a practical way to make launch decisions, and I want to try it at work.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to define fairness and privacy for the specific product I work on, collect only the data I can justify, and design products that can say "I am not sure" instead of guessing. For our team project, we need to write the privacy and risk sections properly, with real details.
