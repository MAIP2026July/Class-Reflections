Session 12 Reflection:

1. Write the Spec Like a Contract

My first learning from this session is that a product document should be written as if we are giving it to a vendor and paying with our own money. Words like "accurate" or "user friendly" mean different things to different people. We should use numbers and clear targets instead. Honestly, our own project draft has many such vague words, and I need to go back and fix them.

I also understood that every workflow should be defined now, even if it changes later, and that diagrams written as text are easy to update and track.

2. Price Backwards From the Customer

The most important idea for me was to start pricing from what the customer is willing to pay for the value they get, and work backwards to what we can afford to spend. I usually think forward, from cost plus profit. If we only find out later that we are more expensive than the competition, it is too late.

I also understood that with AI, a customer who uses the product heavily can become expensive for us, so pricing has to be checked against heavy users and not only the average user.

3. Customers Pay for Output, We Pay for Input

This idea was very clear for me. Customers care about the result they get, while our costs depend on what we put in, like tokens and effort. Charging for outcomes can win more business and earn more, but then we carry the risk if the outcome does not happen. I understood that we should only take that risk if we can deliver consistently.

I also understood that customers who pay a lot but use very little will eventually notice and leave, so sometimes it is better to offer them a cheaper plan.

4. Architecture and Money Are Connected

I had never connected architecture decisions to pricing before. How a vendor charges can decide how we design the system. The same applies to AI. How many times we call a model, and whether we combine requests, is a product decision because it affects cost. I also understood the importance of knowing the margin on each customer, and being careful if each new customer actually lowers it.

5. Ecosystems

I understood that competition today is often between ecosystems and not single companies, and unless we have deep pockets, it is safer to align with an existing one. We also need to decide whether our AI is a standalone product, an add-on, or part of a bigger product. I am still thinking about which one fits our project best.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to start pricing from the value to the customer and not from our cost. For our project, the immediate actions are to remove the vague words from our document, add clear numbers and workflows, and prepare a simple view of our unit economics.
