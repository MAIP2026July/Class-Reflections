Session 13 Reflection:

1. Learning From Other Teams

This session was for the project presentations. At first I thought logging every piece of feedback, even feedback given to other teams, was extra work. But while listening, I realised most of the feedback applied to everyone, including us. I ended up with a long list of things to fix in our own project just from hearing the others.

2. Be Clear About Where AI Is

An important learning for me was that we should clearly show where AI is used in the product, where a human checks its output, and where it goes straight to the user. Where there is no human check, the product should show its confidence clearly, because nobody reads disclaimers.

I also understood that a useful product is not enough on its own. People need a reason to change their behaviour, either because they gain something or because a regulator or competitor forces them. I think we have assumed in our own project that people will adopt it just because it is useful.

3. It Is a Pitch, Not a Demo

I realised that, like most teams, I would have spent the presentation explaining features. The real question to answer is why someone should invest in us. Features and bugs can be fixed later. What matters is whether the product is set up to succeed.

The most useful point for me was to keep two calculations separate: why the customer should buy, and how our business makes money. I also understood that customers expect a lot more value than what they pay, so just breaking even is not enough for them to switch. We also need to be clear about who pays for the AI cost, and include marketing and maintenance costs in our plan.

4. Product, Not Project, Again

The idea of a product versus a project came up again. If something is built for one organization, it is a project. I think the reason many of us keep slipping back into project thinking is that most of our jobs involve building for one internal team or client. It is a habit that will take real effort to change.

5. Questioning Our Own Idea

I also understood that we should find the weaknesses in our own product before someone else does. "AI based" sounds good in marketing, but internally we should talk about the value, because AI is only a way to deliver it. I also liked the reminder to collaborate rather than compete, because the learning matters more than the marks.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, our team needs to separate the customer's return from our own business plan, explain who pays for the AI and what happens when it is wrong, and show clearly where AI and humans each play a role. More generally, I want to practise explaining any idea as a short pitch about value, and keep asking myself whether I am building a product for many customers or a project for one.
