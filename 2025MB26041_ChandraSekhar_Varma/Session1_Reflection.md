Session 1 Reflection:

1. My Understanding of the Product Manager's Role:

My understanding from this session is that a Product Manager is a person who takes overall responsibility and ownership of the product without abandoning the outcomes, whether they are successful or unsuccessful. In many ways, the Product Manager needs to think like a CEO of the product.

A Product Manager coordinates and works with different teams, aligns them toward a common goal, and makes decisions by balancing customer needs, business goals, and engineering capabilities. The ultimate focus should be the success of the product rather than individual team priorities.

Another important learning for me was that a Product Manager may not always have formal authority over people. Therefore, they need to be humble, influence people, build alignment, and avoid a dictatorship-style approach.

2. How This Session Broadened My Thinking

I feel that my thinking has broadened after this session. Earlier, I was thinking more from a requirement-delivery perspective: the business asks for something, the team builds it, and we deliver it.

This session helped me understand that Product Management requires much broader thinking. I should look beyond the stated requirement and consider the customer's actual need, understand the real pain behind the problem, evaluate the business value, and think about the sustainability of the product.

3. Decision-Making and Trade-offs

I also understood that Product Management is fundamentally about making decisions and trade-offs under uncertainty. There may not always be a perfect answer, so I need to understand the users, market, competition, and business model before making decisions.

I should learn to balance different priorities and constraints and make the best possible decision for the product at that particular point in time.

4. Handling Challenges and Moving Forward

Another important learning for me is that challenges, failures, ambiguity, and disagreements are part of this role. Instead of getting stuck or complaining about problems, I should understand the situation, make the best possible decision with the available information and resources, learn from the outcome, and keep moving forward.

5. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to consciously practice this broader product mindset in my work by looking beyond the requirement, questioning the real problem, considering customer and business value, making better trade-offs, and taking greater ownership of the overall product outcome.
