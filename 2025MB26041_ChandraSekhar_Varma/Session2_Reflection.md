Session 2 Reflection:

1. The Product Life Cycle Is a Loop

My main learning from this session is that the product life cycle is not a straight line. I always pictured it as stages that finish one after another, but it is more of a learning loop, and we will go around it many times before the product is right.

I had also never really thought about the end of a product. At work we only talk about launching and enhancing. Now I understand that retiring a product also needs planning, so that customers and their data are taken care of.

2. Thinking Like a Trustee

The idea I connected with most was that a product manager is a trustee. We are spending someone else's money, time and effort, so our decisions need some evidence behind them and not just a feeling.

This made me think about my own work. Many times a decision is taken in a meeting and a few months later nobody remembers why. I want to get into the habit of recording what was decided, why, and who owned it.

3. Not Every Decision Has to Be Made Now

Another important learning for me was that decisions which are costly to change should be taken early, and the others can wait until we have more information. I also liked the idea of one way and two way doors. If a decision can be reversed, we can move fast. If it cannot, we should slow down and be careful.

Linked to this, I understood that a product only needs to be good enough to launch, not perfect. Deciding what "good enough" means is still a hard call, and I do not think I am fully comfortable with it yet.

4. Frameworks Help, but They Are Not the Job

Many frameworks were mentioned, and honestly I could not keep track of all of them. What I took away is that they are only aids for making a decision. We should use the one that helps with the decision in front of us and not try to apply all of them.

5. Where AI Fits

I understood the difference between adding AI to an existing product and building something that was not possible before AI. I was also relieved that an AI product manager does not need to be an expert in the technology, just fluent enough to understand the experts and ask the right questions. Even when we take help from consultants or vendors, the accountability for the product stays with us.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to act more like a trustee of the product, write down my decisions with the reasons behind them, and ask whether a decision is a one way or a two way door before spending too much time on it.
