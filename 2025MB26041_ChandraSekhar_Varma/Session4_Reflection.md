Session 4 Reflection:

1. AI Only Where It Adds Value

My main learning from this session is that we should not add AI to a product just because AI is popular. We need to show that it adds value over what already exists, and to do that we need to measure things before and after AI.

I also understood that before asking whether we can build something, we should ask whether we should build it at all. Is it legitimate, do we have consent, and could it harm someone? I usually jump straight to "can we build it" and skip this question.

2. Start Simple

The most practical idea for me was to start with the simplest option. If a simple rule can solve the problem, use the rule. If not, try search or analytics, then a prediction model, and only then generative AI.

I can relate to this at work. There is a lot of pressure to use GenAI for everything, even when a lookup or a simple rule would do. I also agree that a process has to be stable before it is worth automating.

3. Rare Failures and Late Answers

Two ideas were new for me. The first is that averages can hide serious problems. A product can look fine on average and still cause real harm in a few rare cases, and those rare cases are what we should worry about.

The second is that information is only useful if it comes in time to act on it. A correct answer that comes too late has no value. I think a lot of the reports we get at work arrive after the decision has already been made.

4. Why People Resist

I understood better why people do not adopt a product even when it is useful. Switching has a cost, and if people feel automation is a hidden way to cut jobs, they will resist it. Also, the same person can be an early adopter of one thing and a late adopter of another, so we should first find the people who are ready.

5. How Much Freedom to Give AI

We also discussed the different levels of autonomy, from AI only suggesting something to AI acting on its own within limits. Most tools I use today act only with my approval, and I now see that as a deliberate design choice. I have not fully worked out how a team should decide the right level for a given product, but I understand it should be a conscious decision.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, before suggesting AI for anything, I want to first check whether a simple rule can do the job, and make sure we have a before and after measure. I also want to think about rare but serious failures, and not only about the average case.
