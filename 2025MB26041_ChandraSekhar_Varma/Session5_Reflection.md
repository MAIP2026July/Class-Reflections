Session 5 Reflection:

1. Customers Pay for Value

My main learning from this session is that customers do not pay for AI. They pay for the value they get. Another thing I had not thought about before is that our job is to help the customer finish what they came to do and move on, not to keep them inside our product as long as possible.

I also understood that there are clear signs of where AI could help: friction in a process, tasks repeated very often, stressful decisions, and places with a lot of data. At the same time, AI can reduce trust if it gets things wrong, so it does not automatically make things better.

2. Is It Worth Doing?

An important learning for me was to ask whether a problem is big enough to be worth solving. At work, I have seen us spend weeks building screens for things that change once in a few years. I understood that something can be valuable but not possible today, and that this can change over time.

I also felt that I sometimes spend too long discussing an idea when a quick prototype would answer the question faster. With AI, trying something out has become very cheap, and I want to use that more.

3. Build, Buy or Use an API

I understood the trade-off between building our own AI, buying it, and using an API. An API is the fastest way to start, but we become dependent on the vendor. What we should own is the part that makes us different, not necessarily the model.

4. Designing for Uncertainty

The part I found most useful was about the user experience. A small disclaimer saying the answer could be wrong is not enough. The product should show when it is confident and when it is not, and have a fallback when it is unsure. I also understood that a product manager should know the product well enough to demo it alone.

5. A Human in the Loop Has to Be Real

I always assumed that having a human in the loop makes a system safe. Now I understand it is not that simple. If the AI is right most of the time, people get tired and just approve everything. The human has to be given the right information, be accountable, and be measured in the right way, otherwise the review is only for show.

6. What I Want to Carry Forward

Going forward, I want to check whether an AI idea is worth the effort before getting excited about it, and think about how the product behaves when it is unsure, not just when everything goes well. When I design an approval step, I want to make sure the person approving is really adding value.
