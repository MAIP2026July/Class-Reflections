Session 6 Reflection:

1. Imagining What Is Possible Now

The main thing I took from this session is that a product manager needs to know what is now possible with technology. If I do not know what AI can do today, I cannot imagine a better product. Many things that were too costly a few years back are cheap now.

I also realised that many of our processes were designed around the limits of older technology, and we keep following them even after those limits are gone. I do not practise this kind of questioning much. I usually accept "this is how it is done", and I want to change that.

2. Requirements in the Customer's Words

An important learning for me was that a requirements document should be written in the stakeholder's own language, like "I as a ride giver want...". A stakeholder will only ask for what matters to them. Technical needs like how many users the system must handle belong in a separate specification.

I also understood that the whole team should use the same terms throughout. Looking at our own project draft, we use "user", "customer" and "consumer" for the same person, so this is something we need to fix.

3. What Are We Really Optimising For?

This part made me think the most. When we turn a business problem into an AI problem, we need to be clear about what we are trying to achieve. If we pick the wrong target, the AI will do the wrong thing very well. I also understood that what customers say they need is not always what they really need, and that finding the real need takes effort.

I found the idea of treating the current process as a black box useful. We keep the inputs and outputs in mind and rethink what happens in between.

4. Data and Measurement

I understood that data can be incomplete or biased, and that we are only trustees of customer data, with consent and deletion rights to respect. I also had not thought before about measurement having a cost. Logging everything adds cost and risk, so deciding what to measure is part of the product manager's job.

5. Reading Customer Feedback Properly

On sentiment analysis, my learning was that simply labelling feedback as positive or negative is not enough for a product manager. We need to know which feature the customer is talking about, and also notice the emotion behind the words. Customers often give hidden signals about how they see the product, and a good product manager should pick them up.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to practise rethinking processes instead of just digitising the old way, write requirements in the user's own words, and be clear about what we are really optimising for before we talk about any model.
