Session 7 Reflection:

1. Thinking as the Product Manager

My learning from this session is that when we look at a problem, we should answer as the person who owns the product, not as an advisor giving general advice. Saying "the UI should be user friendly" does not help anyone. What will I actually do in this specific product?

I also understood that a product manager should be close to the product: able to run it without help, and willing to sit with customer support after launch. Honestly, I have never handled support tickets for the products I work on, and I think I would learn a lot from doing that.

2. Removing the Problem Instead of Handling It

The class exercise on customer support for an electricity company taught me something important. I was only thinking about how to make the call centre better. The bigger idea was to ask why the customer needs to call at all. If we can detect the problem and inform people before they call, the problem goes away.

I think this is what reimagining really means, and I want to ask this question more in my own work.

3. Pricing Depends on the Customer

I understood that pricing has to fit the customer's situation. A company running at a loss will not pay for a premium product, however good it is. Each pricing model also has side effects. For example, if we charge per seat and our product reduces the work, the customer will buy fewer seats next year.

The idea that a product built for one industry could later be used in similar industries was also useful for me.

4. Separating Facts From Recommendations

The most useful idea for me was to separate facts, inferences and recommendations in AI output. Facts that come from reliable data can be trusted much more. Recommendations need the most care and should show the evidence behind them. This gave me a much clearer way to think about hallucinations, as something with degrees rather than a simple right or wrong.

5. Launch Is Not the End

I also understood that the product's life does not end at launch. AI behaviour can change after launch, we may depend on outside vendors, and there are data and contract responsibilities until the product is retired. The point that the team needs energy left after the launch is something I can relate to from go-lives at my workplace.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to ask whether a problem can be removed instead of just handled better, keep facts and recommendations separate in anything AI produces, and plan for life after go-live instead of treating the launch as the finish line.
