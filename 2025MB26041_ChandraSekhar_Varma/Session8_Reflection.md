Session 8 Reflection:

1. AI Tools Are Not Always Consistent

My first learning from this session is that AI tools do not always behave the same way. I have seen this myself. Some days a coding assistant finishes the whole task, and other days it stops after one step. I understood the difference between a model that is wrong a small percentage of the time, which we can plan for, and a vendor quietly changing something behind the scenes, which we cannot easily see or control.

I also understood that these tools do not really learn from my feedback over time. They may improve within one conversation, but the next day they start from the same place.

2. Hidden Assumptions

An important learning for me was that AI can quietly build in assumptions during long tasks, and we only find them much later when something breaks. Linked to this, when AI makes everything faster, the product manager becomes the bottleneck. There is so much to review that it is tempting to just say "go ahead".

I have caught myself doing exactly this with AI generated documents and code reviews. Fixing a wrong decision later takes more effort than getting it right the first time, so I need to be more careful about what I approve without reading.

3. Earning the Right to Move Ahead

I understood that a product goes through stages, from concept to prototype, then a pilot with real customers, and then scale. Each stage needs a different mindset. Something can work in a demo and fail in a pilot, or work in a pilot and fail to scale. The idea I liked most was that a team should only move to the next stage after showing real evidence, not just a good demo.

4. Honest MVPs

Another learning was that it is fine for an MVP to have manual steps behind the scenes, as long as we are honest about it and do not present it to customers as automation. We should learn first and automate later, with a way to hand over to a human when the system is not confident.

On governance, I agree that it should be just enough. When the process becomes heavy, people start filling forms just to tick boxes, which defeats the purpose.

5. Going Back to Basics

The discussion on the midterm made me realise that in AI there will always be new terms. Instead of getting stuck on a term, it helps to go back to basics and ask what kind of problem it is: a classification, a recommendation, a decision or something generated. I think this is a good habit for work as well.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, I want to watch for hidden assumptions when I use AI for long tasks, and not approve things just because there is too much to read. For our project, I want us to agree upfront on what evidence we need before moving from prototype to the next stage.
