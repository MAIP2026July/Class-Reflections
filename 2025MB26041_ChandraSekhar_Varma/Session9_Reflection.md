Session 9 Reflection:

1. A Product Is a Promise

My first learning from this session is that every product is a promise of value, and the value only becomes real when the customer actually uses it and benefits. I also understood that a copy of an existing product has little chance of surviving. There has to be something new that gives customers a reason to trust a new player.

I was also relieved to understand that this course is about managing AI products and not necessarily building them ourselves.

2. Evaluation Does Not Stop at Launch

I understood that AI products do not stay stable after launch, so evaluation has to continue after release, and what we learn should drive the next version. AI metrics matter more than I thought, because they directly affect trust and margins. If quality drops, trust drops. If we need a more expensive model to fix it, our margin drops.

3. Averages Can Hide Risk

An important learning for me was that averages can hide the real risk. One rare but very bad event can end a product, even if things look fine on average. I also understood that metrics can look good while hiding a problem, and that our metrics should change as the product matures. If we have tracked the same numbers for a long time, we should ask what we are missing. I think this happens a lot with the dashboards we review at work.

4. Accuracy Is Not the Same as Value

This was the most useful part of the session for me. A model with high accuracy can still lose money if the mistakes it makes are expensive. A less accurate model can sometimes be more profitable. So instead of only looking at accuracy, we should ask what each type of mistake costs and who pays for it.

I had never thought about model metrics in terms of money before. I think this way of explaining it would be much easier for management to understand than precision and recall.

5. Experiments Are Harder With AI

I understood that testing with AI is not fully controlled, because if we use an outside model, the vendor might change things during our test. I also understood that if we keep tuning based on feedback from one group of customers, the product can get worse for everyone else. It is better to keep a generic core and add personalisation on top.

6. My Takeaway Going Forward

Going forward, whenever I see a metric like accuracy, I want to ask what each kind of error costs. I also want to look beyond averages and check whether an improvement is real before celebrating it. Honestly, there were many ideas in this session, and I think I need to go through it again to connect all of them.
