## 1. Key concepts
This session walked through the product lifecycle. A product goes through discovery, definition, build, launch, growth, renewal and sometimes retirement. It isn't a waterfall, and there are gates along the way where we decide whether to launch and what goes in.

One idea I noted was to delay decisions as long as you can, except the structural ones that are expensive to change later. The professor's example was choosing the paint colour after the foundation is done, not before. He also explained General Availability (GA). Marketing can start early, but you can't say you've launched until GA.

Retiring a product is also the PM's job. That means protecting customer data and honouring contracts, and asking whether it's ethical, not only whether it's legal. He kept reminding us that we spend someone else's money, so a roadmap should show our intent without pretending to be certain.

We went through many frameworks: Jobs to be Done, Double Diamond, opportunity solution trees, Lean Canvas, Porter's Five Forces, QFD, North Star, RACI and RAPID, and one-way versus two-way doors. His advice was to treat them as aids, not as management itself, and to pick one per decision instead of mixing them.

For AI products, every artifact changes. The PRD has to cover data, evaluation, uncertainty (including P50 and P95 cost and latency), and regulations like the RBI guidelines and the NIST framework. Evidence also gets old quickly because models keep changing. A vibe-coded prototype isn't production quality, and even with consultants involved, the PM still owns the product promise.

## 2. What changed in my thinking
The credit-card story stayed with me. The team built authorisation first, then realised that without settlement the product was worthless, and by then they had no energy left. I tend to start with the part I know best. I need to map the whole value chain before I spend effort on one piece.

## 3. How I will apply it
For VendorPulse, some decisions are structural and need to be made now: the delivery data model, and how we define a NEX vendor (no ASN). UI details can wait. I'll use Jobs to be Done for the receiving lead, who wants to know who is really coming this afternoon so the dock is staffed, and RACI for who confirms a schedule change. I also want to compare a version without AI (the agreed delivery window) against the ML model, as the professor suggested.
