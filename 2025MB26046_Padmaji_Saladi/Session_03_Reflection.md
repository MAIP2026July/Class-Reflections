## 1. Key concepts
The professor started with a rule he repeats often: never say "user" on its own. Say who it is, like a student, a parent or a receiving lead. Without that, personas mean nothing, and a PM should never be the one causing confusion.

He spoke about how mandatory and optional features change over time. What one generation sees as a nice extra, the next one expects as standard.

Then came the difference between a product and a project. A product takes one customer's problem and solves it for many customers you don't know yet, which spreads the cost. You can add industry variants on top, but the core has to stay protected. If you build only for one customer, it's a project. He called the market a moving target, especially now that AI makes it cheap for customers to build things themselves.

On frameworks, he said they are instruments, not the operating system. Customer journeys run from the first contact to the day the customer leaves, and every customer leaves eventually. In opportunity solution trees, features don't belong in the opportunity layer. Lean Canvas helps expose your riskiest assumption.

He was firm that we must follow the law and the regulator, even under pressure from our own company. He also said accountability can't be delegated, but responsibility can, and introduced "blast radius": how much damage a wrong decision can do.

On pricing, his starting point was that the vendor usually gets about 20% of the value created and the customer keeps about 80%. We should use that until we have real customer data.

He also covered logistics: teams of up to six (a team of one is fine), topic approval through a GitHub issue, presentations in sessions 13 and 14, and reflections logged early.

## 2. What changed in my thinking
The 20/80 split was new to me. I had been thinking of price as cost plus margin. Starting from what the customer gains and taking a fair share of it feels much easier to defend.

## 3. How I will apply it
For VendorPulse, I'll name my users clearly: the receiving lead who plans dock staffing, and the dock associate using a Zebra handheld. The core (arrival prediction and the reliability score) should stay the same for every chain and be configured, not custom-built, so it stays a product. I'll also check my price against the 20% rule by comparing it with the labour and shrink savings per store.
