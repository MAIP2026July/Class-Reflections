## 1. Key concepts
This was the first day of project presentations. Before starting, the professor asked every team to make sure the README lists each member's name, team number, GitHub ID, roll number and BITS email, because he plans to check the repos with scripts and AI. He also asked us to log any feedback given to other teams as an issue in our own repo if it applies to us.

Feedback I noted from the presentations:
- **Make AI steps and human checks visible** in the workflow, and show how AI risk is communicated on screen. For CarePulse he also asked them to separate what the patient sees from what the doctor sees.
- **Answer "why should I invest?"** He wanted this answered without slides or screen share. When PolicyGuard's demo hit a bug, he said to fix the bug and still tell the story.
- **Keep two sets of numbers apart.** The customer's numbers are their savings and ROI. Our own are build cost to MVP and GA, unit of sale, number of customers and seats, and revenue year by year in our own spreadsheet. He asked for stress tests on pricing, who pays for the AI, and a cost "kill switch", like falling back to a cheaper local model.
- **Customers expect 3–4 times the value of what they pay.** If they only break even, there's no reason to risk a new vendor. With HR Ally he pointed out that customers won't believe a full headcount cut and will apply their own safety margin.
- **Pick one unit of sale.** With Helm he asked: per seat, per manager, per project, or one big deal? And use one currency.
- **Plans should cover different outcomes,** not just success or failure, because a pilot usually leads to changes.
- **Make the product general enough for more than one market,** and use it yourself. IdeaHub AI got this feedback.

## 2. What changed in my thinking
Seeing several teams stopped at "why would the customer buy?" showed me how easily we slip into explaining features. The 3–4× rule is a simple test I can use on my own numbers.

## 3. How I will apply it
For VendorPulse, the customer value is about 4.3 times the price ($1,188 a year per store against about $5,100 saved), and 2.3 times in my stress case. I'll open my pitch with that instead of the architecture. I'll keep customer ROI and our startup plan (stores live, break-even in year 3, the $1.5M seed) in separate sheets. I'll check my README for all the required details, and add issues to my repo from other teams' feedback, like marking AI steps and human checks in the swimlane and stating the cost kill switch.

## 4. Open questions
- Is 2.3 times enough in the stress case if customers expect 3–4 times?
- How do I show the savings are really ours when shrink has so many causes?
