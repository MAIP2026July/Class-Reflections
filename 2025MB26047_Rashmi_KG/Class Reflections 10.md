Session 10 Reflection: Making the Right AI Trade-offs 17-Oct-2026

Session 10 made me think differently about what makes an AI product successful. Coming from a software development background, my instinct is often to focus on technical capability and performance. This session reminded me that the most powerful model is not necessarily the best choice. As a Product Manager, I also need to balance quality, speed, latency, cost, predictability, and user experience.

One point that particularly stayed with me was that customers may sometimes value predictability more than speed. A system that responds consistently can provide a better experience than one that is fast sometimes but unpredictable at other times. I could relate this to my experience with enterprise software, where reliability and predictable behaviour are often just as important as performance.

I also found the discussion on smaller versus larger AI models practical. Not every request needs the most capable model. A smaller model may be sufficient for simpler tasks, while complex requests may require a larger model. The session also discussed routing requests between models. This changed my perspective because I now see model selection not just as a technical decision, but also as a product and economic decision.

The cost discussion reinforced this further. An AI demo may look impressive, but if each interaction requires expensive API calls, the same solution may become difficult to sustain when usage grows. This made me realize that a successful prototype does not automatically mean a scalable product.

Another concept I connected with was versioning and rollback. In software development, these are familiar concepts, but AI adds another dimension because models and data also need to be managed. A newer model may perform better overall but worse for a particular customer segment. The ability to identify that change and reroute or roll back becomes important.

My Key Reflection

For me, the biggest learning from Session 10 is that there is rarely one perfect technical choice in an AI product. The Product Manager has to make conscious trade-offs based on what creates the best overall experience and business value.

Going forward, I would not simply ask,“Which model performs best?” I would also ask:

“Is the quality good enough? Is the experience predictable? Is the cost sustainable at scale? And can we recover quickly if the new solution performs worse?”

My biggest takeaway is that the best AI product is not necessarily built with the most powerful model. It is the one that makes the right trade-offs between quality, speed, cost, predictability, and reliability for its users.
