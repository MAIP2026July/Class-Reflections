Being a product manager can sometimes feel like a thankless job because it is impossible to make every single stakeholder or team member happy all the time. However, much like watching a child progress through school, product management is an enriching art of creation that allows you to transform a societal challenge into a valuable product. Rather than treating product creation as a rigid waterfall process, it should be viewed as a managed learning loop where products continuously evolve through multiple iterations.

When managing a product, you are handling someone else's time, trust, money, and resources, meaning you must act as a responsible trustee. To avoid unnecessary friction, you should strategically defer minor decisions, such as visual design details, until as late as possible without harming your core goals, while ensuring structural and foundational alignment happens right at the start. Products also go through essential phases like alpha and beta testing before hitting the market, culminating in General Availability (GA), which marks the true launch when the product becomes open to the public. Furthermore, when a product's useful life has passed, leaders must have the courage to cleanly retire it, handling shutdowns ethically and decently without leaving bitterness behind.

Transitioning into Artificial Intelligence product management requires looking beyond traditional backlogs and technical models to focus on a broader product thesis, data uncertainties, and regulatory compliance. AI product managers must navigate new layers of complexity, such as governance rules and changing model capabilities. Ultimately, while product managers can leverage outside help from consultants or professional services for specialized support, the ultimate accountability, liability, and decision-making authority always rest squarely with the product owner.
