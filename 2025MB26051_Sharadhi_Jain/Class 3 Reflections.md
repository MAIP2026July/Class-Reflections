Session 3 of the Management of AI Products course focused heavily on navigating product strategy, applying strategic frameworks, and managing the unique complexities of AI product management. The discussion began with student reflections emphasizing the importance of traceability matrices, prioritizing progress over raw features, and operating with the mindset that product managers are custodians of trust who manage others' time, money, and resources.

A significant portion of the lecture centered on how rapid technological advancements and AI are shifting traditional product advantages. The instructor noted that because AI lowers development costs and democratizes domain knowledge, competitors and customers alike can easily build solutions on their own, transforming product development into a dynamic race against moving targets. To handle this volatility, product managers must constantly perform course corrections, evaluate market trends, and choose the right mix of strategic tools—such as Jobs-to-Be-Done, Lean Canvas, Opportunity Solution Trees, and Porter's Five Forces—without falling into the trap of bureaucratic over-customization for single clients.

Furthermore, the session explored team dynamics and structural execution, drawing parallels between agile sprints and labor negotiations that balance human stability needs with the necessity for rapid adaptation. The instructor underscored that while product owners delegate responsibilities, ultimate accountability rests squarely with them. Finally, the class concluded with administrative briefings regarding the evaluation components (EC1 through EC3), team sizing limits of up to six members, and the upcoming deadlines for project proposal approvals and GitHub repository setup.
