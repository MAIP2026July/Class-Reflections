Summary for Session 6 of the Management of AI Products course, integrating the core themes discussed during the lecture:

**Focusing on Customer Value over Tech Novelty**: Product managers must prioritize solving real customer problems and jobs-to-be-done rather than building overly complex, unstable products just to use the word "AI". Customers ultimately care about the utility and friction-reduction they receive, not the underlying user interface.

**The Art of Reimagining Workflows**: Building next-generation products requires pushing past historical constraints and questioning long-standing organizational assumptions. By reimagining the internal process (or "black box") with AI/ML, products can achieve greater speed, lower cost, and higher trust.

**Granular Sentiment Analysis & Opinion Mining**: Basic thumbs-up/thumbs-down ratings or overall document-level sentiment fall short for product managers. Effective sentiment analysis requires breaking down reviews into quintuples (target objects, features, sentiment values, opinion holders, and time/context) to gather actionable feature-level insights.

**Decoding Hidden Human Context**: Beyond literal feature feedback, reviews often contain valuable human elements, emotional cues, and social expectations (such as family communication or notification habits) that influence product adoption and perception.

**Data Risks, Governance, and Accountability**: Building AI products demands strict attention to data compliance (such as India's DPDP Act or GDPR), consent management, data leakage, and algorithmic bias. Product managers are ultimately accountable for choosing what is worth measuring and ensuring data practices do not kill product trust on arrival.

**Resource Allocation and Strategic Decision-Making**: Feedback metrics and sentiment dashboards must directly inform investment decisions—guiding whether to invest further to pull ahead of competitors, catch up where the product lags, or reallocate scarce management bandwidth to weaker areas.
