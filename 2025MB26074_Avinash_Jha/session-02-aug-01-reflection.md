## 1. Core Theme & Executive Synthesis

Session 02 reframed the AI product lifecycle from a linear waterfall process into a **managed learning loop**. Because human creators rarely achieve perfection on the first iteration, AI products must evolve continuously through discovery, strategy, planning, execution, launch, and growth—or face disciplined retirement. A central theme was the PM's role as a **trustee of organizational capital and user trust**, responsible for proving *why* decisions were made through rigorous documentation and continuous traceability.

---

## 2. Key Concepts & Frameworks Dissected

* **Managed Learning Loop vs. Waterfall**: Product development is an iterative feedback loop where each release collects telemetry to inform the next cycle. Retirement is a valid, courageous stage in the lifecycle when a product no longer delivers net value.
* **Strategic Deferred Decisions**: Deferring non-architectural decisions (e.g., UI color palettes) as late as possible without impacting core goals prevents locking in unnecessary constraints early when uncertainty is highest.
* **Traceability Matrix**: A continuous record mapping customer pain points $\rightarrow$ product requirements $\rightarrow$ architectural decisions $\rightarrow$ test evidence $\rightarrow$ user documentation. Unbroken continuity prevents critical decisions from getting silently dropped.
* **Decision Frameworks as Aids, Not Substitutes**:
  * *Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)*: Focuses on the underlying progress a user seeks rather than static personas or feature bloat.
  * *Double Diamond & Opportunity Solution Trees*: Explores broad problem spaces before converging on defined solutions and mapping competing paths.
  * *Quality Function Deployment (QFD / House of Quality)*: Systematic mapping of user "whats" to engineering "hows" to manage trade-offs (e.g., structural strength vs. fuel efficiency).

---

## 3. Personal Reflection & Practical Application

The concept of **Technological Readiness Levels (TRL)** brought up during class discussions strongly mirrored how engineering-heavy industries manage gate decisions from TRL 1 (basic principles) to TRL 9 (field-proven systems). In my own domain, I realized we often suffer from "decision leakage"—taking an architectural decision during ideation but failing to carry its rationale into user testing or marketing disclaimers.

Prof. Sathyanarayana’s warning that **"Random Forest is a model, not a product strategy"** hit home. In my daily work, technical teams frequently pitch ML models as products. A model is merely a component in the stack; without operational readiness, pricing mechanics, and user alignment, a model creates no business value.

---

## 4. Critical Evaluation & Friction Points

* **Traceability vs. Bureaucracy**: While traceability matrices ensure accountability, over-engineered documentation can cripple agile development. The PM must strike a balance—documenting architectural and risk-bearing decisions while keeping routine updates lightweight.
* **Regulated AI Trajectory**: Sectoral regulators (RBI, EU AI Act, FDA) are rapidly instituting mandatory compliance rules. Products designed without governance disclaimers and audit trails risk immediate shutdown.
