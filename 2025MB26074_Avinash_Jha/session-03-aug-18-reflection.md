## 1. Core Theme & Executive Synthesis

Session 03 focused on the operational realities of product management: navigating organizational dynamics through **influence without authority** and guiding products toward **moving targets**. Because customer preferences, competitive moves, and AI capabilities evolve simultaneously, product managers must conduct dynamic course corrections—accelerating, dropping, or re-prioritizing features—while preserving team trust and moral alignment.

---

## 2. Key Concepts & Frameworks Dissected

* **Influence Without Authority**: PMs lead cross-functional teams (engineering, UX, legal, sales) without direct line reporting. Leadership is sustained by articulating a compelling vision, serving the team, and taking personal accountability during setbacks.
* **Progress Over Personas**: Static customer personas can mask actual user behavior. The PM must evaluate the *progress* a specific, named user segment seeks under concrete contextual conditions (e.g., cooking in a kitchen vs. operating under low light).
* **Dynamic Target Alignment (The Missile Metaphor)**: Product strategy is not shooting at a static bullseye; it is launching a guided missile toward an evolving, moving target. Continuous telemetry requires constant, small course corrections.
* **One-Way vs. Two-Way Door Decisions (Blast Radius)**:
  * *Two-Way Doors*: Reversible decisions (e.g., pricing tests, minor UI tweaks). Delegate broadly to increase velocity.
  * *One-Way Doors*: Irreversible decisions with high "blast radius" (e.g., core architectural changes, compliance models). Require rigorous pre-mortems and executive sign-off.
* **OODA Loop (Observe, Orient, Decide, Act)**: A continuous decision-making cycle to outpace market changes and competitive threats.

---

## 3. Personal Reflection & Practical Application

The discussion around **human resistance to change** was particularly insightful. As PMs, we often assume users will eagerly adopt superior technology. However, users face real switching costs, disruption to established workflows, and loss of familiar routines. In my workplace, introducing automated AI recommendations initially met resistance because team members feared being replaced or micromanaged. 

To overcome this, product managers must lower switching friction—offering seamless import/export formats, clear fallback mechanisms, and two-way trial periods ("no questions asked" return paths).

---

## 4. Critical Evaluation & Friction Points

* **Sprint Lengths vs. Dynamic Change**: Shorter sprints (1–2 weeks) reduce risk and enable rapid course correction in volatile AI environments, whereas longer sprints (3–4 weeks) provide engineering stability. Balancing team stability against market speed requires careful judgment.
* **Product vs. Services Mindset**: Services organizations prioritize billable hours and client specifications, whereas product organizations invest in generic, scalable solutions. Transitioning a team from a services mindset to a product mindset requires a fundamental cultural shift.
