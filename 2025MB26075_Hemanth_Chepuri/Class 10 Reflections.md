## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- AI economics cannot be left to finance or engineering alone. A PM needs to understand the economics of the product.
- Model calls, tokens, infrastructure, latency and vendor pricing all contribute to the cost of serving a customer.
- Increasing the number of model or agent calls may improve quality but can also increase latency and cost.
- Pricing needs to make sense against both customer value and the actual cost of delivery.
- At scale, a product needs repeatable economics rather than just a successful demonstration.
- Governance requirements increase as AI systems and agents become more numerous and autonomous.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

I found the token economics discussion particularly practical. It is easy to think of an AI product as a normal software product where the marginal cost of an additional user is relatively small. Generative AI can change that because every interaction may have a real inference cost.

The trade-off between quality, latency and cost also stood out. A PM may want the best possible answer, but the customer may not be willing to pay enough to support the most expensive architecture.

This made me see product economics as part of the product itself rather than something considered only after development.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

I would like to understand how early-stage PMs should estimate AI unit economics when model usage and vendor pricing are still changing rapidly.

## 4. Personal Action Item

For my project, I will maintain a simple unit-economics view covering **cost per interaction, expected usage, infrastructure/vendor cost, customer price and gross contribution** before assuming that the product can scale.
