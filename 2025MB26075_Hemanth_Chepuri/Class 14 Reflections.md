## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- The presentations reinforced that the quality of a product idea depends heavily on how clearly the team can explain the customer problem and value proposition.
- A strong PRD should be clear enough for another person to build against it without the PM constantly explaining what was intended.
- Customer economics matter. A product should be able to explain what the customer saves or gains, not just what the software costs to build.
- Build-versus-buy decisions should consider the customer's economics, internal capabilities, strategic importance and opportunity cost.
- User feedback and correction mechanisms are important for AI systems, especially when the output is probabilistic.
- A product can become much stronger when the team thinks beyond the initial feature and considers what it could become, how it could scale and whether customers would actually buy it.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

The strongest learning for me was the recurring question:

**Why should the customer buy this instead of building it themselves or continuing with their current process?**

That forces the PM to quantify the value instead of hiding behind technology. If the customer currently spends a certain amount of money or time on a manual process, the product should explain how much of that burden it can realistically reduce.

I also found the emphasis on ambition useful. A prototype should not become an excuse to build a small feature with no meaningful product vision. At the same time, ambition needs to remain grounded in customer value and feasibility.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

The presentation format naturally pushes teams to show the “happy path”. I think a useful next step would be to force every team to present one failure scenario, the resulting customer impact and the fallback mechanism.

## 4. Personal Action Item

For my future product work, I want to quantify both sides of the equation:

**What does it cost us to provide the product?**

and

**What measurable value does the customer receive by using it?**

If I cannot explain both clearly, I probably have more product work to do before calling the idea ready.
