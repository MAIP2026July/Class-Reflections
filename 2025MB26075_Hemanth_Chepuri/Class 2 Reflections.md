## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- The PM-as-CEO mental model stood out to me. The PM may not have direct authority over engineering, design or data teams, but is still accountable for the product outcome.
- A product is not built once and finished. The lifecycle is iterative, and learning from users should continuously influence what happens next.
- The distinction between buyer, user and other stakeholders is important because they may not always be the same person.
- Product work requires multiple artifacts such as PRDs, prototypes, user stories, acceptance criteria, architecture and measurement.
- I also found the “trustee” mindset important — a PM is effectively responsible for decisions involving somebody else's money, time and resources.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

The PM-as-CEO analogy changed the way I look at ownership. Influence without authority can initially sound like a limitation, but it can actually be the core of the PM role. I may not build the solution myself, but I still need to make sure the right problem is being solved and that the different teams are moving towards the same outcome.

The lifecycle discussion also made me rethink the word “launch”. A beta is not necessarily the finish line. The harder part can begin after the first real users start using the product and expose assumptions that were not visible earlier.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

I would like to understand more about how PMs decide what is “good enough” for a beta versus what absolutely must be solved before release, especially when AI introduces uncertainty.

## 4. Personal Action Item

For my own projects, I want to maintain a simple decision record: what decision was made, why it was made, what evidence supported it and what would make us revisit it.
