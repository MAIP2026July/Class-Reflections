## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- The most useful idea for me was **progress before features and personas**. Features only matter if they help the customer make progress.
- A persona should be qualified. “User” is too vague unless I understand who the person is, what they are trying to achieve and the context in which they use the product.
- JTBD helped me think about the functional job as well as the frustration, anxiety and uncertainty around that job.
- The distinction between a product and a project was important. A project may begin with one customer, but a product needs to become relevant to multiple future customers.
- I also learned that frameworks are tools, not answers. The PM still needs to decide which framework is appropriate for the situation.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

The idea of progress before features changed my thinking considerably. In many work situations, discussions quickly become a list of requested features. But a feature request does not necessarily explain what the customer is actually trying to accomplish.

The discussion around qualified personas also made sense to me. Saying “the user wants a simple interface” is almost meaningless without knowing who that user is and under what conditions the product is being used.

I also liked the trustee mindset. A PM is not simply spending company resources; there is an obligation to make decisions responsibly because other people's money, time and trust are involved.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

There were many frameworks discussed, and my concern is that it could become tempting to use frameworks mechanically. I think the harder PM skill is knowing when NOT to use a framework and when a simpler way of thinking is enough.

## 4. Personal Action Item

Before writing feature requirements, I will first document the progress the customer is trying to make, qualify the persona and write the underlying job.
