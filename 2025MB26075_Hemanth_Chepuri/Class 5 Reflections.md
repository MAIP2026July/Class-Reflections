## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- AI product design cannot stop at the model. The UX layer determines how the customer experiences AI.
- AI can be visible, invisible or reviewable depending on the situation and risk.
- Uncertainty needs to be designed into the experience. If the AI is only 60% confident, the interface should not make it appear 100% certain.
- Confidence, fallback behaviour and human-in-the-loop decisions are product decisions, not only technical decisions.
- Explainability should help the user act or make a decision. A generic disclaimer is not the same as useful explanation.
- Product-market fit still matters even when the technology works.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

The idea of designing for uncertainty was probably my biggest takeaway. In traditional software, users often expect a deterministic response. AI can be probabilistic, so the product experience has to communicate that reality.

I also liked the discussion around human-in-the-loop design. Simply saying “a human is involved” does not automatically make the system safer. If the AI communicates false confidence, the human may stop thinking critically and simply approve what the system suggests.

That made me see UX as part of AI governance rather than just presentation.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

I would like to explore examples where the same AI output is presented differently depending on confidence or risk level. The right UX for a low-risk recommendation would clearly be different from the UX for a high-impact decision.

## 4. Personal Action Item

Whenever I design an AI workflow, I will explicitly define three states: **high confidence, low confidence and failure/fallback**, and decide what the user should see and what action should happen in each case.
