## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- Data is not just an engineering input; data choices are product investment choices.
- A PM should translate a vague business problem into a measurable problem before asking a data science team to build a model.
- Forecasting quality depends on factors such as transaction history, seasonality, local context and data freshness.
- Model accuracy is not necessarily the business problem. The business outcome might actually be inventory availability, fraud reduction or better decision-making.
- Passive signals and explicit feedback have different advantages and costs.
- Collecting more data is not automatically better because data collection also creates privacy and governance responsibilities.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

The statement that **“data choices are product investment choices”** changed the way I think about data. Previously I would naturally classify data collection as something owned by engineering or analytics. But if the product does not capture the right signal today, no future model can magically recover that missing information.

I also liked the distinction between the model problem and the actual business problem. A team can spend weeks improving accuracy while still failing to improve the outcome that the customer actually cares about.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

The passive-versus-explicit feedback discussion raised a practical question for me: how do we identify the minimum useful data without collecting everything “just in case”? That seems especially important for AI products because data collection has both product and privacy implications.

## 4. Personal Action Item

For any AI product idea, I will write the **business outcome first, then the measurable ML target, then the required data**. I will avoid starting with the model.
