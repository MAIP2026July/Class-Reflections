## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- The electricity customer-support case showed me that AI product management is about reimagining the complete customer journey, not simply adding an AI chatbot.
- A known outage could potentially be detected and communicated proactively instead of waiting for the customer to complain.
- Planned and unplanned issues may need different flows, including automation for predictable cases and human intervention for ambiguous or higher-risk cases.
- Build, buy and partner decisions depend on whether the capability is strategically differentiating, the vendor ecosystem, speed, cost and governance.
- Pricing should be connected to customer value, willingness to pay and the motivation of the actual decision-maker.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

The biggest learning was to think in terms of the end-to-end journey. If the system already knows an outage exists, forcing a customer to call support just so the company can tell them about the outage is a poor experience. The better product might remove the need for the call altogether.

I also liked the build-versus-buy discussion because it moved the conversation away from technology preference. The decision depends on whether building the capability creates strategic value or whether buying/partnering allows the product to reach the market faster.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

I found the pricing discussion interesting because different stakeholders can perceive value differently. I would like to explore how a PM should identify the real economic benefit and the actual decision-maker's willingness to pay without relying on assumptions.

## 4. Personal Action Item

For my project work, I will map the full customer journey before proposing AI features. I will also explicitly document whether each capability should be **built, bought or partnered** and why.
