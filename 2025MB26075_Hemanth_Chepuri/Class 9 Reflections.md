## 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways

- AI product success is not only about whether the technology works; it is also about whether the market wants the product and whether the product can create repeatable value.
- A product promise only becomes real when customers actually use the product and realise the promised value.
- Metrics need to reflect the customer segment and should influence what gets built in the next release.
- Average performance can hide serious edge cases. Tail risk can be more important than average accuracy in high-impact products.
- AI metrics should eventually connect to business or economic value rather than remaining isolated technical measurements.
- The PM remains accountable even when specialised teams own the technical implementation.

## 2. What Resonated Most With Me

The discussion on tail risk changed the way I think about metrics. A model can perform extremely well on average and still be unacceptable if one rare failure can cause severe financial, legal or customer impact.

I also liked the distinction between a product being technically good and actually fitting the market. A product can work perfectly and still lose because another product creates more value for the same customer segment.

The idea that metrics should drive the next release also made sense to me. Metrics should not simply appear in a dashboard; they should influence product decisions.

## 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further

I would like to practise converting technical metrics such as accuracy, recall or latency into business impact. That seems to be one of the most important bridges between AI teams and product leadership.

## 4. Personal Action Item

For my future AI product work, I will define **one or two customer/business metrics first**, then identify the technical metrics that support them, rather than treating model metrics as the final goal.
