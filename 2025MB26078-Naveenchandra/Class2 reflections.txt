Class2 reflections, Aug 1

1.Project vs. Product Mindset: Transitioning from solving a bespoke problem for a single known client to building for countless unknown users is fundamental
Products require scalable abstractions, whereas projects merely satisfy immediate contract deliverables

2.The Continuous Product Lifecycle: The lifecycle doesn’t finish at launch; it spans discovery, planning, build, growth, and retirement or renewal
In AI, planning for decommissioning and model deprecation is just as critical as initial deployment

3.Insight by Naveenchandra.L (TRL and Tollgates): Naveenchandra.L connected the lifecycle to automotive Technology Readiness Levels (TRL 1–9)
Because hardware volumes hit millions, every gate requires strict multi-stakeholder tollgate reviews before committing massive capital and manufacturing resources

4.Decision Gates and Expectations: Decision gates protect teams from blind momentum when alpha or beta feedback disappoints
Agreeing on measurable kill, pause, or proceed criteria upfront prevents emotional misalignment across executive stakeholders when realities shift

5.Artifacts Anchor Accountability: Crisp documentation—from PRDs and PRFAQs to experimental briefs—separates disciplined product thinking from wishful conversations
Written artifacts create a shared audit trail for design choices, hypotheses, and intended trade-offs across functional teams

6.Clarifying Decision Rights (RACI): PMs own the product promise, but organizational ambiguity destroys execution
Establishing clear RACI matrices defines who drives, approves, contributes, and escalates, ensuring decisions don't stall when technical uncertainty triggers disagreements

7.AI-Native vs. AI-Assisted: Ground-up AI products rely on data flywheels where usage directly improves underlying model performance
Retrofitting AI into legacy workflows often adds superficial novelty without creating sustainable defensibility or solving genuine user pain

8.Probabilistic Thinking Over Determinism: Traditional software is deterministic, but AI outputs operate across probability distributions
Modern product managers must shift from binary bug tracking to managing statistical confidence thresholds, acceptable failure rates, and graceful fallbacks

9.Professional Services and Customer Risk: When deploying probabilistic tools, professional services must validate edge cases and recovery protocols
PMs safeguard the core product promise, but customers ultimately own how predictions get applied inside high-stakes legal workflows
