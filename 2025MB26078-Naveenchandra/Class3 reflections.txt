Class3 reflections, Aug 18


1.Caterpillar vs. Butterfly Transformation: Real transformation isn't making a caterpillar crawl 10% faster; it’s an order-of-magnitude leap into a butterfly
Slapping AI onto legacy workflows for minor efficiency gains is incremental optimization, not genuine product transformation

2.Moat Erosion via Democratized Knowledge: Domain expertise used to be our primary moat
Today, LLMs democratize domain knowledge, allowing customers and competitors to build bespoke tools quickly
PMs must deliver deeper workflow integration to remain defensible.

3.Course Correction in a Live Race: Product roadmaps aren't static flight plans; they operate like guided missiles tracking moving targets
When competitor moves or user behaviours shift, PMs must decisively drop, reprioritize, or accelerate features without hesitation

4.Technology as Disposable Utility: When discussing transformer architectures, the takeaway was clear: PMs have no religion except customer value Whether using encoder-decoder or decoder-only models, tech is a swap-in tool, not an untouchable sacred cow

5.Product vs. Project Continuity: Unlike project managers who disband after delivery, product managers maintain multi-version continuity
We must defend our team from mercenary leadership, rally morale through crunches, and champion long-term customer outcomes

6.Exposing the Riskiest Hypotheses: Lean Canvas isn't an academic checklist; it forces teams to surface and attack their most fragile assumptions
Identifying where value, user adoption, and regulatory compliance could fail saves months of misguided development

7.Traceable Decision Metrics: Every product trade-off requires documented evidence and traceable metrics
Capturing why features succeeded or failed creates an institutional audit trail, ensuring teams don't repeat expensive mistakes while managing stakeholder capital responsibly

8.Servant Leadership in Product Management: True product leadership demands taking tomatoes and eggs when things collapse while giving credit away during wins
PMs must serve their team, protect team culture, and align incentives over rigid corporate mandates
