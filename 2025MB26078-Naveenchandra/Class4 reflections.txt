1.The AI Value Hierarchy: Do not default to foundation models
Start with the least complex solution: deterministic if-then rules first, then retrieval analytics and search, predictive machine learning next, and generative or agentic systems only when necessary

2.Question by Naveenchandra.L (Evaluation Bias Dilemma): As an innovation manager at Autoliv, Naveenchandra.L raised a practical dilemma: his AI screening model had training bias, technical SMEs favoured feasibility, and sales prioritized viability, leaving trade-offs conflicted

3.Insight & Resolution for Naveenchandra.L: The instructor advised Naveenchandra.L to establish an agreed evaluation rubric across parameters, merge scores into a unified index, and accept that eliminating all human subjectivity is impossible

4.Solving the Right Job-to-be-Done (JTBD): AI must serve the user's specific progress rather than engineering curiosity
As illustrated in the Kisan Seva advisory scenario, framing the real workflow constraint matters far more than showcasing raw model intelligence

5.The Adoption and Inertia Hurdle: Superior product utility doesn't guarantee adoption
Product managers must navigate switching costs, user habits, and scepticism by providing two-way door recovery options, such as transparent refund policies and reversible workflows

6.When Automation Backfires: Automating tasks that users intrinsically value destroys product satisfaction
Efficiency isn't universal; saving transaction time degrades customer relationships if users derive enjoyment or social connection from the manual interaction

7.Auditable Reasoning Over Black Boxes: In regulated sectors like automotive or healthcare, unexplainable predictions fail governance audits
PMs must reject vendors hiding behind proprietary IP and demand documented data lineage and defensible reasoning

8.Focus on Outcomes, Not Tooling: Avoid pitching dissertations or roadmaps around specific algorithms like NLP or deep learning
Locking into a technical mechanism blinds teams; PMs must prioritize measurable customer outcomes and keep underlying models interchangeable

9.Continuous Steering Beyond Launch: Launching an AI product isn't a one-and-done missile launch; it requires continuous course correction
PMs must constantly collect post-launch signals, test pre- and post-AI baselines, and adjust models against silent performance drift

10.Ownership of the Decision Trail: Cross-functional partners own their respective functional silos, but the PM carries ultimate accountability for the core product hypothesis, human-in-the-loop oversight, fallback triggers, and the final go/no-go launch decision
