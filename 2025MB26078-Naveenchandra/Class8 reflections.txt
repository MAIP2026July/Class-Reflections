Class8 reflections, Oct03

1.Provider Instability vs. Model Probability: Cloud-hosted LLM harnesses like Claude and Open AI fluctuate unpredictably in performance
PMs must separate underlying probabilistic model variance from upstream provider instability, compute throttling, or silent background model switches

2.Insight by Naveenchandra.L (Automotive Safety companion): Naveenchandra.L pitched an AI assistant for crashworthiness safety engineers overwhelmed by hundreds of 200+ page regulatory documents, helping onboarding engineers query technical requirements before consulting senior SMEs

3.Market Scope vs. Internal Tool Trap: When Naveenchandra.L framed the companion for internal Autoliv workflows, the instructor pushed back: build for global OEMs and tier-1 suppliers, not just one employer, turning an internal project into a marketable product

4.The Single-Threaded Owner Mindset: Adopting Amazon's single-threaded owner model ensures PMs tie personal success directly to their product's outcome
Treating an AI product as merely one of several side tasks guarantees failure

5.Prototypes Answer Questions, Not Code: Never build the flashiest prototype first. Align fidelity to learning objectives: use UI mock-ups to test workflow adoption, data prototypes for inventory forecasting, and lightweight fakes to resolve the costliest operational unknown

6.The MVP Boundary (Learning vs. Value): Calling every internal test an MVP corrupts learning
Prototypes validate feasibility internally; an MVP forces real external users to change their behaviour and absorb operational costs in live production

7.Avoiding Staged Automation Illusions: Wizard-of-Oz or concierge setups test workflows quickly, but hidden manual labour doesn't scale
If manual back-office tasks hide behind automated demos, teams deceive themselves on operating margins and unit economics

8.RAG Boundaries & Deterministic Traps: Commercial insurance and crash simulations prove generic RAG fails on arithmetic and strict guidelines
PMs must decouple semantic search from deterministic rule engines instead of expecting LLMs to calculate risk math
