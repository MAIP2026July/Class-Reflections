Class11 reflections, Oct24

1.Exposure Bias Over Malice: Bias in models mirrors a child assuming all security guards are Gurkhas simply because that’s all they were exposed to
AI isn't inherently evil; it reflects incomplete historical datasets and unexamined societal prejudices

2.Moving Beyond Motherhood Statements: Saying “we ensure privacy and fairness” is an empty slogan
A product manager must define fairness, compliance thresholds, and data governance strictly within their specific customer and industry context

3.The Power of Data Minimization: You cannot leak or discriminate on data you never collected
Dropping gender, age, and name fields from recruitment or credit workflows prevents inferred algorithmic bias while reducing regulatory audit exposure

4.Mitigating Hallucination via Grounding: Generative models will hallucinate under open prompts
Product managers must enforce RAG grounding, constrain candidate response options, and implement human review gates before outputs hit high-stakes client touchpoints

5.Recourse as a Product Feature: If an algorithmic decision harms or rejects a user, they must have clear visibility into the logic
True product accountability requires offering an accessible avenue for appeal, re-evaluation, or human escalation

6.Incident Response Preparedness: When an AI model fails publicly, social media amplifies the blast radius into a PR nightmare overnight
PMs must design containment protocols, rollback switches, and customer communication plans before launching

7.Actionable Launch Gates: An ethics checklist cannot be a ceremonial rubber stamp
PMs must define concrete blocking criteria—specifying exact failure rates, disparate impact thresholds, and security flaws that definitively halt deployment
