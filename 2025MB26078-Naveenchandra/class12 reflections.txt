Class12 reflections, Oct31


1.Target Costing (Japanese Manufacturing Philosophy): Forward cost-plus pricing creates unviable products
Adopt backward target costing: determine customer willingness-to-pay based on derived value, subtract the required profit margin, and strictly design the product architecture to match

2.The Asymmetry of Value vs. Cost: Customers happily pay for business outcomes and delivered value, but vendors incur costs based on raw inputs like compute and tokens
Product managers must bridge this fundamental commercial asymmetry

3.Inference Margin Traps: When customers fall in love with an unoptimized generative AI feature, soaring API consumption decimates gross margins
If adoption growth accelerates operational losses, the underlying business model is fundamentally broken

4.Downselling for Cohort Retention: When enterprise clients realize they are overpaying for unneeded compute tiers, proactively suggest downgrades
Churn is far more expensive than lower contract values; preserving account retention safeguards long-term customer lifetime value

5.Architecture as a Finance Problem: Model routing, context-window sizing, and caching are financial decisions, not just technical choices
Orchestrating micro-models and small language models directly dictates unit economics, latency trade-offs, and operating margins

6.Contribution Margin by Workflow Variant: Software products feature varied usage paths, from automated classifications to expensive generative loops
PMs must track contribution margins per sub-workflow to aggressively promote profitable features while throttling margin-draining capabilities
