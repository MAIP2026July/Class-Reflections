# Session 1 Reflection
**Date:** 25 July 2026

**Topic:** Introduction to Product Management and AI 

 The first session helped me understand the course structure and what the role of a Product Manager actually involves. Earlier, I thought that a PM mostly coordinates between different teams, follows timelines and ensures that the work is completed. After the discussion, I understood that the PM is responsible not only for coordinating the work but also for ensuring that the right problem is being solved.
 
One point that stayed with me was that a PM may own the outcome without having direct authority over everyone involved. The engineering team, budget and technical decisions may not be completely under the PM’s control. Therefore, the PM needs to influence people, communicate the purpose clearly and make decisions even when all the information is not available. I could relate this to project management because coordination alone does not guarantee that the final solution will create value.
 
The database example also helped me understand the difference between a good technical decision and a good product decision. The most advanced technology is not always the best option. We also need to think about whether the team can support it, how much it costs and whether it improves the customer’s experience.
 
We also discussed three ways in which AI can be connected to product management: using AI to build products, using AI in the PM’s own work and including AI as part of the product. Before this class, I mainly thought about AI as a feature provided to customers. This session gave me a broader understanding of how AI can support the complete product development process.
