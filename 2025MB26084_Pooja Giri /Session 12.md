# Session 12 Reflection

**Date: 31 October 2026**

**Topic: AI Product Economics, Value\-Based Pricing, Target Costing, Unit Economics & Platform Strategy**

This session made me realise that building a useful AI product does not automatically mean that it will become a successful business. Even if customers see value in the product, we still need to understand whether the cost of delivering that value is sustainable as usage increases.

The discussion on **customer value and target costing** was particularly useful. Instead of first building the product, calculating the cost and then deciding the price, we can start by understanding how much value the product creates for the customer and what they may be willing to pay. From there, we can work backwards to understand how much the product should cost us to deliver.

Another point that stood out was that **customers pay for outcomes, not our technical costs**. Our AI solution may incur costs based on tokens, API calls or model usage, but these are not necessarily meaningful to the customer. Customers are more interested in outcomes such as saving time, reducing effort, improving decisions or completing tasks more efficiently.

I also understood why **unit economics** is especially important for AI products. More customers and higher usage do not always mean more profit because every additional AI interaction can also increase our costs. Choosing the right model and architecture therefore becomes both a technical and a business decision.

The discussion on **measurable requirements** was also useful. Requirements such as “fast,” “accurate” or “user\-friendly” can mean different things to different people. Defining clear and measurable expectations makes it easier to determine whether the product has actually achieved its objective.

Overall, my biggest learning from this session was that **AI product economics needs to be considered while designing the product, not after it is built**. A sustainable product needs to balance customer value, pricing, cost, architecture and scalability as it grows.
