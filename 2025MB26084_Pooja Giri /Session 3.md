# Session 3 Reflection
 
**Date:** 18 August 2026


**Topic:** Users, Progress and Sustainable Advantage

 
The main thought I took from this class was that releasing more features does not always mean that the product is progressing. A team may complete many features, but if those features do not help the intended user achieve a meaningful outcome, they may only make the product more complicated.
 
We also discussed the importance of clearly identifying the user. Terms such as "user" and "customer" can be too broad. For the same product, the person using the system, the person approving the purchase and the administrator managing it may all have different expectations. A feature that is convenient for one group may create additional work for another. Therefore, the PM should specify exactly whose problem is being addressed.
 
The caterpillar and butterfly example helped me differentiate between incremental improvement and transformation. Making an existing process slightly faster is an improvement, but transformation means that the way the work is performed has changed significantly. I also found the discussion about organisational incentives realistic. Sometimes teams distribute small improvements across multiple releases because they need visible deliverables for future performance cycles. This may help internal reporting but may not create the best outcome for the product.
 
Another important idea was product layering. A scalable product can have a common core, an industry or domain layer and limited customer-specific configuration. If every customer request is added directly into the core, the product can gradually become a collection of customised projects. This also means that saying no to certain requests is part of responsible product management.
 
AI has also reduced the effort required to create prototypes and basic features. Because of this, the ability to build a feature may no longer be a strong competitive advantage. A more sustainable advantage can come from proprietary data, integration into customer workflows, trust, governance and the difficulty involved in replacing the product.
