# Session 4 Reflection
 
**Date:** 22 August 2026

**Topic:** Deciding Whether AI Is Appropriate

 
This session made me think beyond the question of whether AI is technically capable of performing a task. The more relevant question is whether using AI creates enough additional value to justify its cost and risk.
 
We discussed a progression starting from deterministic rules, followed by analytics or search, predictive machine learning and finally generative or agentic AI. I understood that we should begin with the simplest solution that can solve the problem. If a checklist or rule-based system is sufficient, using a complex AI model may introduce unnecessary cost and unpredictable behaviour.
 
The agricultural alert example showed that accuracy alone cannot define the success of an AI product. Even an accurate prediction is not useful if it reaches the user after the opportunity to act has passed. Therefore, timing, latency and the actionable window should be considered along with accuracy.
 
I also understood why average performance can sometimes hide serious risks. A model may perform correctly in most situations but fail in a small number of critical cases. If one such failure can cause a large financial, safety or reputational impact, the product cannot rely only on its overall accuracy percentage. Guardrails, fallback options and escalation mechanisms should be included in the product design.
 
The human-in-the-loop discussion was interesting because human approval does not automatically make a system safe. If users repeatedly see routine AI recommendations, they may begin approving them without proper review. This creates automation bias. Human involvement should therefore be meaningful, particularly in uncertain or high-impact situations.
 
Finally, I understood that adoption is also a product design issue. Even when an AI feature provides value, users may resist it if it interrupts their familiar way of working or requires too many additional steps.
