# Session 6 Reflection
 
**Date:** 5 September 2026

**Topic:** Workflow Redesign, Requirements and Data


 
My biggest learning from this session was that adding AI to an inefficient process does not automatically improve the process. It may only make the same inefficient steps happen faster. Before introducing AI, the existing workflow should be questioned and, where necessary, redesigned.
 
The black-box approach was useful here. Instead of accepting every existing input, approval and hand-off as necessary, we should first understand the actual outcome expected from the process. We can then redesign what happens between the input and output. This creates an opportunity to remove steps rather than simply automate all of them.
 
The discussion on PRDs also connected with the earlier conversation about defining the user. Requirements should be written from the perspective of a specific stakeholder. For example, an approver and an end-user may require different outcomes from the same feature. Technical constraints should be documented separately rather than being presented as the stakeholder’s need.
 
One practical point was not to delete sections from a product template simply because they appear irrelevant. Writing why a section is not applicable shows that it was considered. It can also prevent the team from missing an important dependency.
 
We also discussed build, buy and API-based approaches. APIs can help a team launch quickly, but they may create vendor dependency, unpredictable usage costs and data privacy concerns. The decision should depend on the importance of the capability, available internal expertise, required control and sensitivity of the data. Domain-specific data can become an important product advantage and should be governed carefully.
 
The examples related to biased data also showed that bias may not be easily visible. A model can appear accurate while consistently producing unfair outcomes for certain cases. Product design should therefore consider data quality, governance, trust and human behaviour, not only technical performance.
