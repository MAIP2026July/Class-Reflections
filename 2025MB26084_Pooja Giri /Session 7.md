# Session 7 Reflection
 
**Date:** 12 September 2026

**Topic:** Proactive Products, Pricing and Continuous Risk


 
This session connected several areas of product management, including customer experience, pricing, AI uncertainty and post-launch risk.
 
The electricity support example helped me understand the difference between reactive and proactive product design. A basic approach would be to use AI to answer customer complaints more quickly. A better approach would be to use operational data to identify the outage before the customer calls and proactively communicate the issue and expected resolution. In this case, the best support experience may be one where the customer does not need to contact support at all.
 
At the same time, proactive systems should not communicate uncertain information as if it is confirmed. If the system has low confidence, the interface should show that uncertainty, ask for verification or avoid triggering an unnecessary alert. Simply displaying a general warning that AI can make mistakes does not solve the design problem.
 
The discussion on value-based pricing was also new for me. Pricing should not depend only on the cost of building or operating the product. It should also consider the economic value created for the customer. The class discussed capturing a portion of the value created, with around 20% as a possible starting assumption. However, willingness to pay also depends on the buyer’s targets, incentives and risk perception.
 
The PFIR method gave me a structured way to analyse information. First, we identify the facts or features that are actually available. Next, we make inferences based on those facts. Finally, we provide recommendations. Keeping these stages separate can prevent assumptions from being presented as facts.
 
The final learning was that the launch of an AI product is not the end of product management. Model behaviour may change because of data drift, changes made by external vendors, API updates or pricing revisions. Therefore, monitoring, fallback planning and vendor management should continue throughout the product’s lifecycle.
