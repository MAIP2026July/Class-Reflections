# Session 11: Governance has to outrun the agents

24 October 2026 | Neetesh Jain | 2025MB26095

---

Governance must scale faster than the count of agents you have, or you have let loose a swarm you no longer control. At the same time, if governance becomes theatre, people hide work from the board or bundle it so it looks smaller.

Those two warnings pull in opposite directions, and both apply to the ethics note I wrote. We have launch blockers and a review step. We do not have a rule for how the review stays real when there are four subagents and twenty five engagements every Monday. A committee that meets monthly is already slower than the product. A click through approval on every item is the free labour trap. The faculty's test was: would you, as the highest paid person in the room, spend that time, or would you fake it. I would fake some of it by week six. So the design cannot assume I would not.

**Human review is not free.** If you want serious attention, pay for it, time box it, and do a share of the reviews yourself so you know the effort. We currently treat the RDM's ninety minutes as a cost we are reducing. We should also treat it as the control we are buying. If we squeeze it further because the agent looks confident, we have sold the control to improve the margin. Session 7 said if the product works the customer needs less of it. The internal version is: if the agent looks good, we will fund less review, which is how the control dies.

**There is no universal fairness test.** The industry standard can itself be biased. Follow the law as a minimum, then decide what fairness means for this product, and keep deciding. I wanted a checklist I could tick. That desire is the motherhood statement the faculty banned. For Helm, the live question is whose voice counts as evidence. A risk written by the person being assessed is cheaper and less reliable than the same risk written by someone with no stake. Weighting those the same is a policy bias, not a model bug. It belongs in the risk register as a design choice we made, not as a residual ML risk.

**Launch is never bug free.** Known issues, and still go, is a management decision. The split I needed was already close to what we wrote: a minimum bar to launch, a set of blockers that stop you, and a middle where judgement sits. I will add detectability and reversibility to each blocker. A leak we cannot see is different from a leak we can roll back. Session 9 said the dead cannot keep playing. This session said: know, before launch, which events you can undo.

**What I will not do.** Add another committee. I will put a monthly sample of approved items back in front of the people who approved them, with last week's outcome attached. If that feels like extra work, that is the point. If nobody has time for it, we are already past the speed of our own governance.
