# Session 9: The dead cannot keep playing

10 October 2026 | Neetesh Jain | 2025MB26095

---

Probability says that if you toss a coin long enough you neither win nor lose. The catch is that the model assumes infinite money. Hit a million losses first and you are out of the game. The dead cannot keep playing. Casinos charge a fee, so even a fair game is not fair once you include survival.

I have been scoring our risks as probability times impact. Session 4 already made me pull the tail cases out of that arithmetic because a tiny number times a huge one produces a middling score. This session named why the arithmetic is wrong, not just inconvenient. If one fabricated quote reaches a client, or one cross account leak happens, there is no average to recover into. The product is finished. My register still has rate based blockers, which assume we get to keep playing. I need a second column that asks only: if this happens once, are we still here.

**Averages hide the thing that kills you.** Revenue up eleven percent while new customer signups have dropped. The metric you asked for last year still looks good. The faculty's rule was: if the metrics have stayed the same as the product matured, you are probably blind to something. Our success list is still the one we wrote at topic approval. Right engagements in the top five. Time shifted. Actions used. Those were the right questions for a pilot. They will hide a later failure where the RDM is faster and less careful, or where status reports get vaguer because people feel watched. I will add one metric that is allowed to look worse while the others look better, specifically honesty of the written input, otherwise we will celebrate a dashboard that is feeding on thinner data.

**Do not let the ML number replace the business number.** A participant argued for recall as the parameter that saves you. The faculty would not preach precision or recall. Put profit and loss in each cell, because those numbers are harder to game. I own the economic model and I had a recall threshold in it, eighty percent of at risk engagements in the top five, without the rupee value of a miss. That is still a technical score wearing a business badge. I can keep the recall number for the engineers. The document I show a buyer has to be the cell that hurts.

**What I will change.** Sensitivity analysis on survival, not on margin. The question is not how much profit we make at twenty managers. It is which single event ends the product, and whether we would know in time to stop.
