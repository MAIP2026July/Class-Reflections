# Reflection: Session 1

**25 July 2026 | Jodhdeep Singh | 2025MB26112**

### The story that landed

* The idea of the **PM as the "CEO of the product"** clarified that a PM owns outcomes end to end, not just a backlog. The same ownership mindset applies to any role.
* The professor's **career journey in cards and early internet banking payments** showed how a product can scale to a huge user base and stay relevant for many years.
* His **continued upskilling in AI and NLP** was a reminder that a PM must keep learning as technology shifts.
* **No job is thankless** unless you stop working on it. Even routine pain points can be turned into customer or business problems worth solving.

### Where I disagree

None. I would only add that the "PM as CEO" framing works best when PMs are given real decision rights, otherwise ownership stays on paper.

### What I will do

* Align more closely with **product goals and priorities** instead of treating delivery as the finish line.
* Stay involved across the full lifecycle: **discovery → define → strategize → plan → build → launch → grow → retire**.
* Clearly **record assumptions, owners and timelines** for every initiative.
* Go beyond "development complete" by checking tests, code quality and readiness for QA before calling work done.

### Open questions

1. How does a product manager learn to influence stakeholders without formal authority? Is there a repeatable approach?
2. How should teams ship and deliver in markets where a product may not sustain beyond 18 months?
