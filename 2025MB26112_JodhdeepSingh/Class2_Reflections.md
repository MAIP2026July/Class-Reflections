### The story that landed

* A **roadmap is not a fixed commitment**. It is a set of bets that should change as evidence arrives, and some decisions are better deferred than made blindly.
* **Beta is closer to the halfway point** than the end. Much of the real learning happens after release.
* Product development is **continuous**, because customer needs, technology and markets keep changing.
* **PM artifacts** (PRDs, PRFAQs, decision logs, assumption trackers) exist to capture decisions, assumptions and learning, and I want to use them more deliberately.
* The Apple iPhone example contrasted **asking customers what they want** with **discovering needs they cannot yet articulate**.
* The Tata Motors example showed that a **capability can be reused in another market**, but it must be re-validated for the new context.

### Where I disagree

Planning and roadmaps matter, but in practice leadership changes often reset priorities and scope no matter how many iterations came before. A roadmap needs to be resilient to that, not just well reasoned.

### What I will do

* **Design with scalability in mind** instead of solving only the immediate requirement.
* Keep a **learning journal** of lessons from projects.
* Use retrospectives to record **what worked, what didn't and what to change**.
* Keep my MBA notes as a long-term reference rather than exam-only material.

### Open questions

2. How are **PRFAQs** actually used in product planning, and at what point?
