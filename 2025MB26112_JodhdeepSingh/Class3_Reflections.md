# Reflection: Session 3

**18 August 2026 | Jodhdeep Singh | 2025MB26112**

### The story that landed

* **Traceability over gut feel.** PMs work with "someone else's money" and act as custodians of trust, so decisions should trace back to evidence.
* **Customer progress over feature count.** Value comes from helping customers get a job done, not from stacking features.
* **Influence without authority.** Product leaders rally design, engineering, data and business teams around shared goals without relying on hierarchy.
* **Qualify the "user".** Generic terms hide differences. Being explicit about who the persona is changes what we build.
* **Scrum as a "labor contract".** Sprints protect the team from constant mid-sprint changes while still allowing course correction between sprints.

### Where I disagree

Short sprints work well for many teams, but where releases depend on security reviews, integrations and approvals, a two-week cycle can force artificial slicing of work. Sprint length should follow the real delivery constraints.

### What I will do

* Replace vague words like "user" in requirements with **specific personas and contexts**.
* Maintain a **decision log** linking each significant choice to the evidence behind it.
* **Protect sprint scope** and route new requests to the next planning cycle.

### Open questions

1. How do we balance fast experimentation (A/B tests) with compliance and sign-off requirements?
2. What is a good approach to influencing senior stakeholders who prefer opinion over evidence?
