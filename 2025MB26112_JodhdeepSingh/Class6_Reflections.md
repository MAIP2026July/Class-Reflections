# Reflection: Session 6

**5 September 2026 | Jodhdeep Singh | 2025MB26112**

### The story that landed

* **Start with value, not technology.** Customers pay for solved problems and outcomes, not for "AI" or fancy interfaces.
* **Standardized stakeholder language in PRDs.** Role-based statements such as *"I as a [Role] want [Feature] so that [Outcome]"* keep requirements clear and keep jargon out.
* **Re-imagining legacy workflows.** The "art of the possible" asks us to question old constraints and remove friction that existed only because of past technology limits.

### Where I disagree

Value-first is the right ideal, but some work, such as infrastructure upgrades or mandatory compliance changes, is technology-led and cannot be tied to direct customer value up front. It still needs a clear business justification.

### What I will do

* Open every initiative with the **customer problem and expected outcome** before discussing solutions.
* Write requirements as **role-based user stories** with explicit personas.
* Pick one existing workflow and **map which steps are legacy constraints** versus real needs.

### Open questions

1. How do we balance value-first thinking when compliance forces immediate technical change with no visible customer benefit?
2. How do we write PRD requirements for a feature that serves two very different personas?
