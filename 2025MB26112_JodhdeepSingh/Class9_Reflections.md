### The story that landed

* **Deterministic vs. generative systems.** Non-deterministic outputs need different evaluation approaches than traditional software testing.
* **Economic Payoff Matrix over pure accuracy.** Mapping the confusion matrix to profit, loss and risk shows the real business impact of a model.
* **Tail risk.** Averages can hide rare, extreme failures that cause outsized damage.
* **A/B testing pitfalls.** Seasonality and concurrent changes can distort results unless the experiment is carefully controlled.
* **Uncontrolled feedback loops.** Optimizing for the loudest or most active users can quietly degrade the experience for everyone else.

### Where I disagree

Payoff matrices are useful, but assigning a number to soft costs like trust or brand damage is difficult and can give a false sense of precision. The assumptions should be stated openly.

### What I will do

* Evaluate models by **net economic impact**, not just accuracy.
* Add **tail-risk reviews** to launch checklists.
* Design experiments with **proper controls** and account for external factors.
* Watch for **feedback loops** that favor power users over the wider base.

### Open questions

1. How can monetary values be assigned to indirect costs like frustration or abandonment?
2. Which statistical methods best isolate a product change when external seasonality is heavy?
