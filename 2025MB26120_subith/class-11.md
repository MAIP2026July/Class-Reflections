Session 11 – Class Observations
- AI bias: Bias can arise unintentionally from historical data, societal prejudices and labelling practices. Product Managers must actively identify and address hidden biases.

- Context-specific fairness: Fairness, privacy and safety must be defined according to the product, its users and its potential risks.

- Data privacy: Protecting user information, obtaining appropriate consent and minimising data exposure are essential responsibilities, particularly in healthcare.

- AI hallucinations and verification: AI-generated insights may be inaccurate. Techniques such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can help ground responses in reliable information, but verification remains important.

- Human accountability: AI should support decision-making, not replace human judgement or transfer accountability away from people.

- Continuous governance: Regular monitoring, audits and corrective actions are necessary to maintain an AI product's safety, reliability and usefulness after launch.

Key takeaway: Responsible AI product management requires proactive risk management, privacy protection, human oversight and continuous improvement throughout the product lifecycle.
