### Session 9 – Class Observations

- **Beyond accuracy:** A model's accuracy alone does not determine its business value. The financial, reputational and safety consequences of errors must also be considered.

- **Cost of errors:** False positives and false negatives have different impacts. Model evaluation thresholds should reflect the cost of each type of mistake.

- **Tail risks:** Rare but serious errors can be hidden by average performance metrics. Some risks require acceptable error limits, while critical harms may justify stopping a launch even after a single occurrence.

- **Reliable experimentation:** A/B testing must account for external factors such as seasonal demand, campaigns and service outages to ensure that results reflect the product change rather than unrelated events.

- **Feedback loops and bias:** AI systems trained primarily on feedback from highly active users may overlook quieter customer groups, potentially reducing overall customer satisfaction.

**Key takeaway:** Product Managers must evaluate AI models based on business impact, error costs, critical risks and the representativeness of feedback—not accuracy alone. A higher accuracy score is not sufficient justification for launching a product.
