**Class 1 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 25 July 2026 | Role of a product manager

The first class was mostly about what a product manager actually is. We went around the class giving definitions like bridge between business and tech, owner of the lifecycle, optimiser. The professor said all of these were fine but they missed the main point, which is ownership. His frame for the whole course is that the PM is the CEO of the product.

The cricket captain example made it clear for me. If the captain scores a duck and the team wins, the captain still collects the trophy. If he scores a century and the team loses, he still lost. You can't blame engineering or marketing, because they are also yours. You take feedback, but the decision and the blame stay with you.

He also said a PM mostly works with influence and not authority, and that the job is about trade-offs. His database story stuck with me. He picked the second-best database because talent for the better one was hard to find and he didn't want his architect stuck on it forever.

On the 'thankless job' question, he said it depends on how you look at it, like appraisals where someone is always unhappy, or kids changing classes. What I'd like to use is the habit of asking whether I'm acting like the owner or just passing messages along.
