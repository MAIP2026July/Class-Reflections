**Class 2 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 1 August 2026 | Product lifecycle, artefacts and frameworks

This session covered the product lifecycle: discover, define, strategise, plan, build, launch, grow, and then retire or renew. The professor said it is a learning loop and not a waterfall. You go round it many times.

The point I liked most was that we are trustees of someone else's money. Even if it is your own startup, it is your family's money and time. That is why decisions need evidence and traceability. He wants us to keep decision records in GitHub so one decision can be followed through architecture, testing and even user manuals.

The house-building example on deferring decisions was useful. You fix the rooms and the structure early because they are costly to change, and leave paint colours for later. But big mismatches in stakeholder expectations should be sorted out at the start.

From his 1992 card-processing product he said beta is only halfway and post-beta is the other half, so don't burn out the team in a sprint. He also explained general availability as the real launch, and why shutting a product down cleanly matters.

He listed a lot of frameworks: JTBD, double diamond, opportunity solution trees, lean canvas, QFD, RICE, Kano, MoSCoW and RACI. He was clear they are decision aids and not the management itself. The difference between AI enablement of an existing product and an AI-native product was also new to me.
