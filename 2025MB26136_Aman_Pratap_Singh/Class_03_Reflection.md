**Class 3 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 18 August 2026 | Frameworks in practice, product vs project, Scrum

We started by recapping and then got into why traceability matters. The answer again was that we are custodians of someone else's trust and money. He also said never to use the word 'user' unqualified. Name it: student, parent, farmer. Then you can build a persona around it.

The caterpillar and butterfly analogy was about transformation. Making the caterpillar crawl twice as fast is incremental. Becoming a butterfly is a different order of change. He asked us to be honest about which one we are really doing.

A product is for N unknown customers. If you build only for one, it's a project. You can layer a domain pack and a customer customisation on top of a generic core, and sometimes you have to say no to a customer who is pulling you down a rabbit hole.

The missile and moving target analogy explained why course correction never stops: customers move, competitors move, and the product doesn't go exactly as planned. That led into Scrum, which I hadn't seen framed this way before. A sprint is like a labour agreement with the team: no changes for a fixed period, then anything can change. He said that the more dynamic the environment, the shorter the sprint should be.

On pricing, he said the vendor can usually take only around 20% of the value created, and the customer keeps the rest.
