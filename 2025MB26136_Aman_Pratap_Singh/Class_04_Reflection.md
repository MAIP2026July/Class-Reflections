**Class 4 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 22 August 2026 | When AI actually creates product value

This class was about checking whether AI really adds value before building it in. He used examples like Kisan Sahayak, a UPI dispute assistant and Netflix recommendations. The first gate is social legitimacy: consent, vulnerable groups, no hidden surveillance or harm. Value and feasibility only come after that.

The nuclear-powered pencil sharpener line was funny but it made the point. You can build it, but should you? He said to start with the simplest option: rules first, then search and analytics, then predictive ML, and only then generative or agentic AI.

The actionable time window example stayed with me. Telling a farmer it will rain one minute before it rains is useless. The same goes for the autonomous braking example, where you have to add up detection time, alert time, human reaction and braking distance.

We also discussed adoption. People resist change because of switching cost and workflow disruption, so early adopters are the first target. For Naveen's question on biased idea scoring, he suggested one common rubric and merge formula for both the AI and the human experts. That reduces subjectivity but won't remove it.

He also said to always measure before and after AI, and to focus on the problem and not on 'deep learning' as the goal.
