**Class 5 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 29 August 2026 | Finding AI opportunities, build vs buy, UX for uncertainty

The class opened with the idea that customers don't pay extra just for the word AI. He compared it to the old 'double bearing fan' ads. AI has to help the customer finish their job faster, cheaper or better and get out. He used Google keeping ads off its homepage as an example of putting the user's job first.

Opportunity signals were friction, trust, frequency, decision intensity and data-rich workflows. His intranet example showed that something changing once in two or three years doesn't need a UI at all.

On build, buy or API, he said owning the capability matters more than owning the model. API dependence brings strategic risk. He linked it to Porter's forces and suggested doing a Porter's analysis now and again 18 months out. The Casio versus Sony story was about sometimes funding learning, and not just ROI.

The UX part was important. Every AI stack has a data layer, a model layer, an API layer and a UX layer. The UX has to show confidence and uncertainty honestly. A one-line disclaimer isn't enough. On human in the loop, he warned about reviewer fatigue, where people just keep clicking yes if nothing makes them accountable.

He also said a PM should be able to give a demo alone, and that a prompt should cover role, context, task and constraints.
