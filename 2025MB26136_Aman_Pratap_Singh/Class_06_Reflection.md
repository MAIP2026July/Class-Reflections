**Class 6 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 5 September 2026 | Reimagining workflows, translating problems to AI, sentiment analysis

The first part was about GitHub submissions and the assignment template. After that, the main theme was the art of the possible. If you don't know what AI can do, you can't reimagine a product. The audiobook and text-to-speech example showed how something uneconomic 25 years ago is easy now.

He said a PRD should be written in the stakeholder's own words, like 'I as a ride giver want…', and terminology must stay the same across the team. The cooked-dal-in-the-fridge story was about questioning assumptions. So was the print, sign, scan, email chain that could just be an email approval.

Translating a business problem into an AI target was the core idea. You treat the process as a black box with inputs and outputs, then ask which inputs can be dropped or added now. He warned that if the modelling is wrong, ML just optimises the wrong thing beautifully. Data risks like leakage, bias and missing data, plus consent under the DPDP Act, all came up.

The sentiment analysis walkthrough with the iPhone review was eye-opening. An opinion is a quintuple: object, feature, sentiment, holder and time. Plain positive or negative scoring isn't enough for a PM. The line about the mother being upset she wasn't told before the purchase showed the human signal behind a product, and you can't measure that as a feature.
