**Class 7 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 12 September 2026 | Case thinking with the DISCOM example, pricing, lifecycle

Most of this class was one long discussion on a customer support product for electricity distribution companies. People suggested escalation, empathy scripts and AI voice bots. The professor kept pushing us to think as the product company selling to the DISCOM, and not as the DISCOM.

The reimagining part was the best bit. If you can detect an outage from consumption patterns, why should the customer even need to call? Use the phone number or GPS to know their location, push planned-maintenance alerts, and send a field person before the complaint comes in. He also pointed out that power that was bought but couldn't be delivered is a direct loss, so early fault detection has real economic value.

On pricing, he said to understand the motivations of the decision makers, including politicians, and to read DISCOM balance sheets. You can buy power at 3–5 rupees and sell it at 9–12, so there's a lot of inefficiency in the middle. The 15–25% value share came up again.

He shared his framework for hallucination: truth, facts, inferences and recommendations, with the risk increasing as you go down that list. He also talked about transferability, meaning the same product for water and broadband, and land and expand.

For exams, his advice was to write 'I as a product manager will…', and not to advise someone else.
