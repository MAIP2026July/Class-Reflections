**Class 8 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 3 October 2026 | Prototypes, MVPs, staged automation

He started with his own experience of agentic coding tools. Claude and Codex are at the top, but their performance goes up and down for no visible reason, which he thinks is on the provider's side and not just model probability. The 'beat the donkey, it takes one step' line described the agent that stops even when told to keep going.

His three-banks story showed assumption drift. He said use three banks for testing, and the AI made three banks a permanent rule. His point was that the PM becomes the bottleneck because there are too many proposals to read, and saying 'go ahead' without reading creates rework.

The main content was prototype vs pilot vs scale. Each stage has a gate that has to be earned. A prototype can answer the wrong question. An MVP has to show real value to the customer even if some steps are manual. Faking automation with hidden human labour is fine for learning, but you have to be honest about it.

He warned about PMs who keep adding requirements until a customer shows up and then ask why the product isn't ready. Governance should be right-sized, not bureaucratic.

The midterm feedback was also useful. Answers should use the case context, and he plans to add short definitions of terms in future papers.
