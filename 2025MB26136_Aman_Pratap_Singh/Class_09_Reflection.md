**Class 9 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 10 October 2026 | Metrics, payoff matrix, A/B testing

This class was about metrics. The main message was that accuracy alone can mislead. The loan example worked it out in numbers. Each good loan earns 2,500 on one lakh, and each bad loan loses the full lakh. So one default wipes out about 40 good loans, and you can have 90% accuracy and still lose money. He wants us to put a business value in every cell of the confusion matrix.

Joydev argued for recall. The professor agreed it matters, but said he prefers the payoff view because it keeps business judgement in front and makes it harder to game the numbers.

Tail risk also came up, with Joydev's LA wildfire story where insurers went bankrupt. Averages hide edge cases, and the Vegas coin-toss point was that you have to survive long enough for probability to work.

On metrics in general: they should change as the product matures, and 'tell me how you measure me and I'll tell you how I behave' applies to testers, agents and staff. He suggested a small balanced set of around three metrics.

For A/B testing, he said always check the standard error before celebrating something like a 4.5 to 4.8 CSAT jump. LLM providers may be running their own experiments on us, so even controlled tests aren't fully controlled.
