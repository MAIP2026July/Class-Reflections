**Class 10 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 17 October 2026 | Scaling AI products

He began with the warning that marks will follow a strict bell curve this term and that late submissions won't be accepted. Then he moved to scaling.

Scaling is not only more users. It's also more requests, data, workflow complexity and organisational dependence. The line I wrote down was that user expectations harden faster than infrastructure can catch up. Once a feature becomes mission critical, the promise changes, whatever the terms and conditions say. His example was people tweeting when free WhatsApp goes down.

He said teams talk about servers when they should talk about promises. If Uber promises a cab in three minutes, that's what has to be monitored. Hygiene, expected and wow features move over time, like rear cameras in cars.

There's a trade-off between quality, latency and cost, and the PM has to give the team a range, not just say faster or better. He also mentioned routing simple queries to small models, caching, tiering, rate limits, queues for premium customers, and versioning data and models together, not only code.

The commando versus regular army comparison was about matching people to the kind of work. Commandos can't do sentry duty for long, so don't depend only on heroics.
