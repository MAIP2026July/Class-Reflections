**Class 11 Reflection**

Management of AI Products | 24 October 2026 | Ethics, bias, privacy and responsible AI

This class was about ethics and risk. The Gurkha example explained bias well. A small child who has only seen Gurkha security guards thinks every guard is a Gurkha and every Gurkha is a guard. AI trained on limited data does the same thing. He gave other examples too, like 'pilot' generating a man, the Apple Card case, and rules like no bachelors.

When Gautam asked about a standard framework for fairness, the professor said there isn't one universal answer. Follow the law and industry guidelines as the minimum, and try to do better. Being aware is the first step.

On privacy: collect only what you need, get consent, state the purpose and respect retention limits under DPDP and GDPR. Children's and health data are more sensitive. His point that blind reviews remove names because names can reveal gender made sense to me. He also shared a case where a team member misused a candidate's phone number, which is why need-to-know matters.

For hallucination, the product should know when to answer, when to abstain and when to escalate. Governance should keep up with the product and not turn into bureaucracy, and it has to scale faster than the number of agents.

He asked that our project reports include a data and privacy section specific to our own product, not general statements.
