**Class 1 Reflection**

25 July 2026 - Role of a product manager

First class, and the line that actually landed was "you are the CEO of the product." Not the usual bridge-between-business-and-tech definition everyone gives in interviews - full ownership, including the parts that aren't your fault.

The cricket captain bit made it concrete: score a duck and win, you still take the trophy; score a century and lose, you still lost. I've sat in enough post-mortems where the finger-pointing starts with "engineering missed the date" or "support didn't flag it in time" to know how easy that is to slide into, and how little it actually helps.

What I want to try out: before I hand off a decision to "the team," ask myself whether I'm actually deciding or just relaying. The database story he told - picking the worse-but-more-hireable option so one architect doesn't get stuck forever - is the kind of trade-off I'd have called a mistake a year ago. Now it reads as a pretty deliberate call.
