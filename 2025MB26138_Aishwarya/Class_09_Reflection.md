**Class 9 Reflection**

10 October 2026 - Metrics, payoff matrix, A/B testing

This one mapped onto my own work more directly than most. The loan example: each good loan nets 2,500 on a lakh, each bad one loses the full lakh, so a single default wipes out roughly 40 good loans - and a model can sit at 90% accuracy and still be losing money. That's close to how I'd actually want to argue about model quality in a treasury context rather than quoting an F1 score at someone.

Joydev's pushback for recall was fair, but I liked the professor's answer more - the payoff view keeps the business judgement visible in a way that's harder to quietly game than a precision/recall number nobody outside the data team can sanity-check.

Tail risk and the LA-wildfire insurer story are a good reminder that averages hide exactly the cases that actually bankrupt someone, and the standard-error point on A/B testing (don't celebrate a CSAT move from 4.5 to 4.8 without checking the noise) is one I'll actually use the next time someone shows me a dashboard with a confident-looking uptick.
