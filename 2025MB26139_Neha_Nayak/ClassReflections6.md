# Management of AI Products: Class 6

The session covered how to turn real business problems into AI/ML problems, starting from customer value rather than technology. The second half used customer sentiment analysis as a worked example of how product managers can extract decisions from unstructured feedback. 

## Core Concepts
- **Art of the possible and reimagining workflows:** AI product thinking starts by questioning old assumptions and constraints. Many processes were designed around limits that technology has since removed.
- **Translating business problems into AI/ML targets:** Treat the solution as a "black box" between inputs and outcomes. Decide which inputs to keep, cut, or add, what exactly to predict, and how to evaluate it. A wrong target means optimising the wrong thing very well.
- **Data risks and governance:** Missing or biased data, leakage, consent, and bias amplification can make a product "dead on arrival". Organisations are custodians of customer data, not owners, and must comply with laws like DPDP and GDPR. Teams should also think about "blast radius" and choose carefully what is worth measuring.
- **Sentiment analysis for product decisions:** An opinion has structure: object, feature, sentiment, opinion holder, and time. Analysing at the feature level is far more useful than a simple positive/negative score. It is not one problem but several (entity recognition, coreference, comparisons, spam detection), and accuracy is still imperfect.

## Reflections and Insights
- **Requirements belong in the stakeholder's voice.** A PRD should be written as "I, as a [role], want…", with consistent terminology across the team. Technical constraints like scale belong in the separate product specification, because a user cares about their own experience, not the system's capacity.
- **Build category killers, not clones.** A product manager adds value only by creating something that beats the reference product, not by copying it.
- **Low adoption cost is part of the MVP.** If users must juggle several platforms to get value, the product has failed even if the features are good.
- **Measurement changes the system, as in the uncertainty principle.** Measuring more adds cost, slows things down, and creates data risk, so deciding what to measure is a product decision.
- **Emotion is a hidden signal.** A review that mentions the buyer's family reacting is not a feature, but it shows the human context around a purchase that feature-by-feature analysis misses.
- **Data choices are product investment choices.** In demand forecasting, the true goal is having the right stock in the right place at the right time, not model accuracy.
- **Marketing cannot rescue a flawed product.** The course focuses on building a great product, and awareness-building is left to marketing courses.
