## 1. Class Context

The session focused on what changes when a successful AI product begins to scale. It covered rising customer expectations, latency, quality and cost trade-offs, stress testing, model routing, rate limits, queues and the meaning of service-level commitments as product promises.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Expectations rise with success:** What delights customers today can become a basic expectation tomorrow.
- **Latency is a UX problem:** Users do not experience infrastructure metrics. They experience waiting, uncertainty and interruption.
- **Stress test quality, not just availability:** A system can respond under peak load while the usefulness of its answers deteriorates.
- **Quality, speed and cost trade off:** Moving from a smaller model to a frontier model may improve quality but increase cost and response time.
- **The PM defines the sweet spot:** Technology teams can optimise different extremes, but Product must define what trade-off suits the customer segment.
- **Rate limits require product choices:** Queues, throttling and priority lanes determine who gets speed when capacity is constrained.
- **SLAs are business promises:** Capacity, failure recovery and service levels should be discussed as customer commitments, not merely technical targets.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **Latency should be part of the product requirement.** In healthcare workflows, an accurate answer that arrives after the operational decision is no longer useful. Response time and actionable time therefore belong beside quality metrics.
- **Peak behaviour matters more than demo behaviour.** Products usually look clean in controlled conditions. I want load testing to examine whether the answer remains useful when demand spikes, not only whether the service stays online.
- **Routing can become a product strategy.** Different workflows may justify different models based on urgency, complexity and risk. One model for every request is simple, but not necessarily economical.
- **SLAs should be written in customer language.** A client cares whether the workflow can be completed when needed, not which internal component met its uptime target.
- **Where I differ:** Priority lanes make commercial sense, but in healthcare I would not allow pricing alone to decide priority where a safety-critical or time-sensitive workflow is involved.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Define latency targets from the decision window of the workflow.
- Stress test answer quality during peak load.
- Route workloads based on value, risk, cost and urgency.
- Design graceful throttling before provider rate limits force it.
- Express service levels as customer and workflow promises.

## 5. Closing Reflection

The class reinforced that scale is not simply more infrastructure. My key takeaway is that scaling an AI product means actively managing the trade-offs between quality, speed, cost and customer expectations without breaking the product promise.
