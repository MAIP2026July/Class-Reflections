## 1. Class Context

The session focused on responsibility after AI becomes part of a product. It explored how bias can enter through exposure, data and training, how product teams should look for unequal outcomes, the need for concrete data privacy decisions, and how governance must grow without becoming bureaucracy.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Bias can be unintentional:** Awareness is the first step because incomplete or selective data can carry assumptions into the product.
- **Ask who benefits and who is harmed:** Aggregate performance can hide segments receiving an unfair advantage or disadvantage.
- **Investigate by segment:** Complaint patterns and reviewer disagreements can reveal where bias is appearing.
- **Practise data minimisation:** Specify what data is collected, why it is needed, consent, retention and privacy implications rather than using generic statements.
- **Audit to learn:** Audits can review design, fallback mechanisms and privacy choices, not only assign blame.
- **Agent governance must scale:** Increasing the number of AI agents without stronger controls creates operational risk.
- **Governance must earn its cost:** If the value of a governance step cannot be explained, it risks becoming paperwork and a bottleneck.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **Bias is a product outcome, not only a model property.** In healthcare, an overall metric can look strong while one specialty, hospital type or workflow experiences poorer results. I would therefore review performance by meaningful operating segments.
- **Data minimisation needs specificity.** Saying that we collect only necessary data is not enough. The PM should be able to explain the purpose and retention of each important data category.
- **Auditing can improve the product.** I like the framing of audits as learning mechanisms. Review findings should feed back into guardrails, workflow design and monitoring rather than exist only as compliance evidence.
- **Governance should enable responsible speed.** Large organisations need controls, but controls that add no decision value eventually encourage people to bypass them.
- **Where I differ:** Governance does not always need to scale simply with agent count. I would scale it primarily with autonomy, impact and reversibility, because ten low-risk assistants may need less control than one agent authorised to take consequential actions.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Review AI outcomes by relevant customer and workflow segments.
- Document purpose, consent and retention for collected data.
- Turn audit findings into product improvements.
- Scale controls according to autonomy and potential harm.
- Remove governance steps that cannot demonstrate decision value.

## 5. Closing Reflection

This class connected responsible AI directly to the Product Manager's role. My key takeaway is that fairness, privacy and governance cannot sit outside the product lifecycle. They need to be designed into the product while still allowing the team to move with purpose and speed.
