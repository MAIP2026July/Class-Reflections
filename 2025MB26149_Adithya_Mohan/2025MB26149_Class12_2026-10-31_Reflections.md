## 1. Class Context

The session brought product thinking together around the project work. It covered the distinction between a first-version delivery plan and a multi-version roadmap, the need to start with a target workflow, unit economics, extensibility, platform thinking and communicating an AI product's value to an investor or decision-maker.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Roadmap is not a project plan:** The first version needs detail, while later versions should show how the product evolves across versions two and three.
- **Start with a target workflow:** The workflow can improve after pilot feedback, but the team needs an explicit starting point rather than postponing the decision.
- **Design for extension:** Hard-coded choices can limit the product when new options or use cases appear later.
- **Unit economics must survive growth:** More customers are not automatically good if each additional customer reduces margin. AI input and token costs must create corresponding value.
- **Communicate value quickly:** A product presentation should convince a decision-maker why the product deserves investment, not simply narrate how it was built.
- **Platform thinking changes value:** Interoperability, compatibility, bundling and reuse can increase the strategic value of a product beyond a single use case.
- **Choose AI's role deliberately:** AI may be the product, a premium layer or an embedded capability. The business model should make that choice clear.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **The roadmap distinction is important for me.** Enterprise roadmaps can easily become implementation plans. I want a roadmap to show how product value evolves, while keeping delivery detail for the current version separately.
- **Extensibility is a product decision.** In healthcare, specialties, workflows and customer configurations change. Designing configurable boundaries early can prevent a scalable product from turning into repeated custom projects.
- **Unit economics belong beside customer value.** A feature can be loved and still be a poor business if usage increases variable AI cost faster than revenue or savings.
- **The investor-pitch framing is useful even inside an enterprise.** Leadership time is limited. A PM should explain the problem, differentiated value, economics and next evidence needed without relying on a long feature walkthrough.
- **Where I differ:** Platform ambition can be attractive too early. I would first prove a strong core workflow and reusable boundaries before investing heavily in platform capabilities.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Separate the detailed version-one plan from the multi-version roadmap.
- Define the target workflow early and refine it with pilot evidence.
- Prefer configurable design where future variation is predictable.
- Track variable AI cost as usage increases.
- Explain unit economics and differentiated value in a concise leadership narrative.
- Earn the right to become a platform by proving the core product first.

## 5. Closing Reflection

This session connected product design, scale and commercial thinking. My strongest takeaway is that a PM must design not only what the product does today, but how it evolves, how its economics hold up and how its value can be explained clearly enough for others to invest in it.
