## 1. Class Context

The session reframed product management from coordination to ownership. It also introduced three ways AI connects with product work: building products with AI, using AI in PM activities, and embedding AI into the customer experience.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Ownership over coordination:** A PM owns the problem and outcome, even when execution is distributed across teams.
- **Accountability without authority:** Product leadership depends heavily on influence, alignment and making dependencies visible.
- **Trade-offs matter:** The technically strongest option may not be the best product decision when talent, maintainability, cost and scalability are considered.
- **AI has three distinct roles:** AI can support product development, improve PM productivity, or become part of the customer-facing product. Each should be evaluated differently.
- **Ownership includes endings:** Sunsetting a feature, version or product is also part of responsible product management.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **The CEO-of-the-product mindset resonated with me.** In my role, Product, Engineering, Operations and implementation responsibilities often sit across different teams. I may not control every dependency, but I still need to drive clarity on the problem, decisions and expected outcome.
- **The database example reinforced practical product judgement.** A technically superior choice can become a weaker product decision if it creates talent, maintenance or scaling constraints. Product decisions therefore need operational realism, not technical optimisation alone.
- **Delivery is not the final outcome.** Shipping a feature matters only if it improves customer or business value. I want success measures to be clear before development rather than something defined after launch.
- **AI should earn its place in the product.** The three-pillar framing helps me distinguish AI used for productivity from AI that directly creates customer value. Customer-facing AI should justify the additional complexity, cost, risk and trust considerations.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Define success through customer and business outcomes, not only delivery milestones.
- Make trade-offs, assumptions and dependencies explicit when driving alignment.
- Treat influence and transparency as core PM capabilities when formal authority is limited.
- Evaluate whether AI genuinely improves the customer problem before making it part of the product.
- Be willing to reshape or stop investments when evidence no longer supports them.

## 5. Closing Reflection

My strongest takeaway is that product management is ultimately about judgement and ownership under uncertainty. I may not control every team or decision, but I am responsible for creating the clarity and alignment needed to move the product towards measurable value.
