## 1. Class Context

The session asked whether AI should be used before asking whether it can be. Using trigger products such as a multilingual agricultural advisory, a UPI dispute assistant and Netflix recommendations, the class discussed value, legitimacy, timing, human judgement and adoption.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Gate 0 is social legitimacy:** Before technical fit, check standing, consent, impact on vulnerable groups and whether the product relies on manipulation, surveillance or harm.
- **Value, viability and bounded learning:** AI must show measurable value against a pre-AI baseline, be viable on quality, latency, cost and support, and improve within defined scope, owners and hard stops.
- **Appropriateness ladder:** Start with deterministic rules, then search and analytics, then predictive ML, and use generative or agentic AI only if lower rungs cannot solve the problem.
- **Actionable time window:** A correct prediction is useless if it arrives too late to act, like a rain warning one minute before it rains.
- **Tail risk over averages:** Good average accuracy can hide rare but serious failures, such as wrong pesticide guidance.
- **Human judgement and accountability:** Decision makers may stop thinking when AI support feels reliable, yet accountability stays with people and the product owner.
- **Adoption has a switching cost:** Resistance is often rational, and dark patterns that make leaving hard erode trust.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **The appropriateness ladder is a practical discipline.** In healthcare operations, many problems can be solved well with rules, analytics or simple predictive models. Starting at the lowest rung reduces cost, risk and explainability concerns, which matters for clinical and operational trust.
- **Timing is part of the value proposition.** An insight that reaches a scheduler or clinician after the decision window has closed adds no value, however accurate it is. I want "when will this be actionable" to sit beside accuracy in every AI requirement.
- **Rubber-stamping is a design risk, not a user problem.** If the product makes it easy to accept AI suggestions without thought, the risk returns to the product owner. Override options with reasons, and clear explanations, help keep human judgement active.
- **Pre and post AI measurement is essential.** Claims of AI value are only credible with a baseline, and the value may differ by customer segment.
- **Where I differ:** Gate 0 is framed as a first gate, but in practice legitimacy concerns surface again as products scale into new segments. I see it as a recurring check, not a one-time approval.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Ask whether AI is needed before deciding which AI to use.
- Start with the lowest rung of the ladder that solves the problem.
- Define the actionable time window alongside accuracy targets.
- Design for tail risks with guardrails and escalation paths.
- Measure pre and post AI performance by customer segment.
- Avoid adoption tactics that trap customers.

## 5. Closing Reflection

This class reframed AI from a capability to showcase into a responsibility to justify. My key takeaway is that AI creates product value only when it is legitimate, timely, measurable and trusted by the people who act on it.
