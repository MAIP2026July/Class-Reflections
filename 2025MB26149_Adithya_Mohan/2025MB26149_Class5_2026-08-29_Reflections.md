## 1. Class Context

The session focused on how a PM sources and operates AI capability responsibly. It covered build vs buy choices, the PM's ownership of economics, how to design human-in-the-loop workflows, designing for uncertainty and treating prompts as a product asset. The midterm format was also explained.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Sourcing is a strategic choice:** Build, buy, open-weight models or APIs each trade speed against control. API dependence is an early start but also a strategic risk if access, price or availability changes.
- **Own the capability that is your moat:** Capabilities that create competitive advantage should not sit entirely with a vendor.
- **The PM owns the economics:** Unit cost and pricing can be calculated with help, but the final call stays with the PM.
- **Portfolio manager vs product manager:** The portfolio manager oversees many products, while the PM remains the owner of one product.
- **Human-in-the-loop must be designed:** Decide whether review is always on, exception-based or staged through escalation. Tell reviewers why a case reached them and disclose stale or unreliable data.
- **Human fatigue is real:** When AI looks confident, reviewers start approving without thinking, and the value of the review is lost.
- **Prompts are product:** Structure them with role, context, task and constraints, version them and include them in launch readiness.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **Review fatigue is the risk I relate to most.** In healthcare operations, a reviewer who sees many correct suggestions will naturally start trusting them. I now see that recording approvals, corrections and escalations is not admin work. It is how the product proves the human review is real and not ceremonial.
- **Escalation needs context to be useful.** Sending a case to a human without explaining what was uncertain or missing simply moves work around. Surfacing the reason and the evidence makes the human faster and more accountable.
- **Vendor dependence belongs in the business case.** I would expect architecture to cover this, but the class made it clear that model pricing, availability and change risk directly affect product margins and commitments, so the PM must own it.
- **Prompts deserve the same discipline as code.** If prompts shape customer experience, they need owners, versions and testing before release.
- **Where I differ:** Autonomy is often framed as something earned over time. In regulated settings, some steps may need permanent human review regardless of confidence, so I see autonomy as a deliberate design boundary, not only a maturity stage.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Include vendor and model risk in every AI business case.
- Define when human review is always on, exception-based or escalated.
- Record review decisions and corrections as product evidence.
- Treat prompts as versioned, tested product assets.
- Keep the core differentiating capability under our control.

## 5. Closing Reflection

This class showed me that operating AI responsibly is as much about economics and workflow design as about model quality. My key takeaway is that a human in the loop only adds value when the product is designed to keep that human genuinely engaged.
