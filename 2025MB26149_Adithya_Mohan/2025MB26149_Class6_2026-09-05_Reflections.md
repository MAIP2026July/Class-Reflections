## 1. Class Context

The session challenged us to reimagine workflows instead of automating existing steps. It then covered translating business questions into the right AI target, data risks such as bias and missing data, the cost of measurement, and sentiment analysis at the feature level.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Know the art of the possible:** A PM cannot design a next-generation product without knowing what current technology can do.
- **Reimagine, do not just automate:** The print, sign, scan and email example showed steps that exist only out of habit.
- **The black box approach:** Keep the inputs and outcomes fixed, then rethink the middle. Some inputs may no longer be needed.
- **Optimise the right target:** If the business question is translated wrongly, AI will optimise the wrong thing very efficiently.
- **Data risks are product risks:** Missing data, biased samples and consent failures can make the whole system fragile, as the Apple Card case showed.
- **Measurement has a cost:** Measuring everything adds cost and data risk. The PM decides what is sensible to measure.
- **Feature-level sentiment:** Useful feedback captures the feature, the sentiment, the opinion holder, the time and the context, not just a thumbs up or down.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **Reimagining is harder than it sounds.** In enterprise healthcare products, many workflow steps exist because of past system limits or approvals that no one has questioned. The black box framing gives me a practical way to challenge them: start from the outcome the clinician or operator needs and ask which inputs still matter.
- **Wrong targets are a quiet failure.** A model can look accurate while solving the wrong problem. I want the target definition to be reviewed with operations, not only with data science.
- **Feature-level feedback is directly useful.** Client feedback often mixes several messages, such as liking an insight but struggling with access or timing. Separating feedback by feature, holder and product version would make prioritisation far more evidence-based.
- **Context changes meaning.** The camera example showed that one feature can be good in one situation and poor in another. In healthcare, the same report may work for one specialty and not another.
- **Where I differ:** Minimal measurement suits mature products. For a new AI capability, I would measure slightly more early on and prune once we know which signals actually inform decisions.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Start redesigns from outcomes, not from existing steps.
- Validate that the AI target reflects the real business question.
- Check data for gaps, bias and consent before modelling.
- Tag feedback by feature, holder, time and context.
- Measure what informs decisions and drop the rest.

## 5. Closing Reflection

This class reminded me that AI amplifies whatever process and data it is given. My key takeaway is that the biggest gains come from questioning the workflow and the target first, and only then applying the technology.
