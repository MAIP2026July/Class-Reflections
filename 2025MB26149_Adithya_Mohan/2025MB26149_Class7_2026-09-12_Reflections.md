## 1. Class Context

The last class before the midterm was built around a live case on an AI customer service product for an electricity distribution company (DISCOM). The discussion moved from first answers to sharper problem framing, buyer economics and how AI outputs should be presented. The case-based exam format was also explained.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Frame the problem before solving it:** Most first responses improved call handling. The stronger question was what the utility already knows, such as which feeder or transformer serves an affected locality.
- **Systems thinking adds value:** Linking outages to lost revenue for the DISCOM turned a support problem into a business problem.
- **Understand the buyer's economics:** Reading DISCOM balance sheets shows that power purchase cost is a major driver, which shapes what the buyer values.
- **Facts, inferences and recommendations:** AI output should separate reliable facts, what is inferred, and what is recommended, and be honest when data is missing.
- **Data collection has limits:** Collect only what makes sense and keep regulations such as the DPDP Act in mind.
- **Profit is legitimate:** Pricing should be sensible and sustainable for the team and organisation.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **Reframing was the biggest lesson.** I would probably have started with a better triage or routing solution too. The case showed that the best product often removes the need for the interaction altogether by using data the organisation already holds.
- **Separating facts from recommendations builds trust.** In healthcare, a schedule or a case count is a fact, a predicted delay is an inference, and a suggested action is a recommendation. Showing these differently would help clinicians and operators judge how much to rely on each.
- **B2B buyers think in their own economics.** Understanding the client's cost structure, not just their stated pain, makes value conversations far more credible.
- **Where I differ:** Proactive communication is powerful, but it can create noise if not tuned. I would want clear thresholds on when the product reaches out, so users do not start ignoring it.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Ask what the system already knows before designing a new interaction.
- Connect operational problems to the client's financial outcomes.
- Present AI output as facts, inferences and recommendations.
- Be explicit when data is missing instead of guessing.
- Collect only the data the product genuinely needs.

## 5. Closing Reflection

This case showed me that the first problem presented is often not the real one. My key takeaway is that strong AI products come from reframing the problem through data and economics, and then presenting outputs in a way users can trust.
