## 1. Class Context

The session shifted AI evaluation from model metrics to business consequences. It covered payoff matrices, imbalanced errors, tail risk, experimentation and feedback loops, with the core message that a technically strong model can still be a poor product decision.

## 2. Key Takeaways

- **Accuracy is not economic value:** Precision, recall and related metrics do not tell a PM what errors cost the business.
- **Use a payoff matrix:** Map each prediction outcome to its business impact. One costly error can outweigh gains from many correct predictions.
- **Tail risk matters:** Rare edge cases can threaten the product or even the business, so averages are insufficient.
- **Experiment carefully:** Test and control groups can help, but external events and awareness of being measured can distort results.
- **Feedback is a signal, not the whole population:** Optimising repeatedly for one vocal segment can make the product worse for others.
- **Metric design is product design:** What we choose to optimise, and what we deliberately ignore, shapes product behaviour.

## 3. My Reflection as a Product Manager

- **The payoff matrix is more useful to me than accuracy alone.** In healthcare operations, a false alert may create extra work, while a missed high-risk event may have a much larger consequence. The product threshold should reflect that asymmetry rather than chase a single model score.
- **Tail risk changes how I think about AI quality.** A model may look strong on average and still be unsuitable if a rare failure is unacceptable. I would make high-impact edge cases explicit in acceptance criteria.
- **Feedback loops require governance.** Enterprise products often hear most from the most active clients. I need to separate strong feedback from representative feedback before moving the roadmap.
- **Where I differ:** Not every consequence should be reduced to money. In healthcare, safety and trust may need hard limits even when a financial payoff matrix suggests accepting more risk.

## 4. What I Will Carry Forward

- Pair model metrics with the business cost of each error type.
- Test rare but severe failure scenarios explicitly.
- Segment feedback before changing product behaviour.
- State what each metric optimises and what it ignores.
- Continue evaluation after launch rather than treating validation as complete.

## 5. Closing Reflection

This class made AI evaluation much more concrete for me. My key takeaway is that the best model is not the one with the highest headline accuracy, but the one whose errors, economics and risks fit the product decision we are asking it to support.
