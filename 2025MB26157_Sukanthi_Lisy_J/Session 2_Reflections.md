# Session 2 - Personal Reflections 

This session broadened my understanding of product management, particularly in the context of AI. My biggest takeaway was the Professor’s description of a product manager as both a creator and a trustee. The role goes beyond coordinating teams and delivering features; it involves making evidence-based decisions, managing risks, and taking ownership of outcomes.

### 1. Evidence vs. Product Intuition

I realized the importance of balancing evidence with intuition. While decisions should be supported by facts, customers cannot always articulate needs they have never experienced. The real skill lies in knowing when to rely on evidence and when to trust informed product intuition.

### 2. Strategic Deferral of Decisions

The concept of strategic deferral helped me understand that decisions that are expensive to reverse should be made early, while reversible decisions can wait for better information. The key is to defer strategically, not procrastinate.

### 3. Product Lifecycle and Launch Readiness

I previously viewed product launch as the point by which most of the difficult work should be complete. The session reinforced that launch, particularly beta testing, is the beginning of significant learning. However, “good enough” depends on the product’s risk. AI applications in healthcare or financial services require a much higher threshold for reliability and safety than low-risk productivity tools.

### 4. AI Is Not the Product Strategy

One of my strongest takeaways was to start with the customer problem and business outcome, rather than the AI technology. AI should be adopted because it creates meaningful value, not simply because it is available. I also found the distinction between enhancing existing products with AI and creating AI-native products useful, as each presents different opportunities and challenges.

### 5. Rapid Prototyping and Experimentation

AI makes it easier to develop prototypes, test assumptions, and gather customer feedback before committing significant resources. However, technical feasibility does not automatically establish commercial viability, operational feasibility, or genuine customer value. Experimentation is valuable when it leads to better decisions.

### 6. Artifacts and Traceability

The discussion on PRDs, roadmaps, user stories, and acceptance criteria changed my perception of documentation. These artifacts connect business intent with design, development, testing, and customer outcomes. For me, traceability is not about creating more documents; it is about ensuring consistency between intent and execution.

### 7. Frameworks as Decision-Making Tools

Frameworks such as Jobs to Be Done, Design Thinking, RICE, Kano, and MoSCoW can be useful, but applying too many can create analysis paralysis. I believe frameworks should be selected based on the problem rather than applied mechanically. They are a toolbox, not a checklist.

### 8. AI Governance and Accountability

AI introduces risks involving data, model behavior, misuse, regulation, and accountability. The Professor’s point that involving experts does not transfer the product manager’s responsibility resonated with me. Even when I am not the technical or legal expert, I must ask the right questions and ensure appropriate safeguards are considered.

### 9. Ownership and Influence

The idea that a product manager is the owner, not merely the coordinator, particularly resonated with me. Product managers often have responsibility without direct authority over every team involved. This makes communication, negotiation, stakeholder alignment, and sound judgment essential to delivering successful outcomes.

### My Key Takeaway

This session helped me see AI product management as less about managing technology and more about managing value, uncertainty, decisions, people, risks, and learning.

My biggest personal shift is that I now see a product manager not simply as someone responsible for delivering a product, but as someone accountable for ensuring that the right product is built for the right problem and the right users, with a clear rationale behind important decisions. This is the mindset I want to carry forward from the course.
