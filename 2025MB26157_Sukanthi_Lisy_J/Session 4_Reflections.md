# Session 4 - Personal Reflections 

### 1. Rethinking the Value of AI

This session reinforced my understanding that incorporating AI does not automatically make a product better. What matters is whether AI solves a genuine customer problem more effectively than existing alternatives. I realized that product managers should focus on customer needs rather than adopting AI simply because it is gaining popularity. Sometimes, a simpler solution may deliver greater value at a lower cost.

### 2. Measuring Value and Driving Adoption

One of my key takeaways was the importance of measuring AI's actual impact. Whether the goal is to save time, improve accuracy, or support better decisions, the benefits should be measurable.

I also realized that building a useful AI product is only part of the challenge. Customers must trust it, understand its benefits, and be willing to integrate it into their workflows. Product success depends not just on what we build, but on whether customers adopt it and derive value from it.

### 3. Balancing AI Autonomy with Human Oversight

I agree with Professor's emphasis on responsible AI and human oversight. AI outputs should not automatically be treated as correct, especially when errors can have serious consequences.

However, I believe human involvement should depend on the level of risk. Low-risk, routine tasks may benefit from greater automation, while high-impact decisions require stronger supervision and clear escalation mechanisms. The challenge is to balance efficiency with accountability.

### 4. Balancing Customer Value and Business Viability

This session also reminded me that technical innovation alone does not guarantee product success. An AI product must deliver customer value, justify its cost, and operate reliably and responsibly.

Going forward, I want to evaluate AI features based on their expected benefits, adoption challenges, implementation costs, and potential risks. If AI adds unnecessary complexity without meaningful value, a simpler solution may be the better choice.

### My Key Takeaway

My biggest takeaway is a change in the question I ask as a product manager. Instead of starting with “Where can I use AI?”, I should ask, “What customer problem am I solving, and is AI the best way to solve it?”

I want to approach AI product decisions with a stronger focus on measurable outcomes, customer adoption, and responsible implementation. Ultimately, effective AI product management is not about using the most advanced technology; it is about creating meaningful and sustainable value for customers and the business.
