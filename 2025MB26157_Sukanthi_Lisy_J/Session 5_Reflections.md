# Session 5 - Personal Reflections

### 1. Customer Value Over AI Features

This session reinforced my belief that AI should solve a genuine customer problem rather than be adopted simply because it is available. As a product manager, I want to start with the customer’s needs and determine whether AI offers a better solution than existing alternatives.

### 2. Business Value and Feasibility

I realized that a technically impressive AI product may not be commercially viable if its operating costs are too high or customers are unwilling to pay for it. I would therefore evaluate customer value, implementation costs, technical feasibility, and long-term maintenance before committing resources.

### 3. Measuring Product-Market Fit

Initial excitement does not necessarily indicate product-market fit. I would look beyond trial sign-ups and early engagement to understand whether customers continue using the product and experience lasting value. Identifying the right customer segment is equally important.

### 4. Designing for Uncertainty and Trust

AI can produce incorrect answers confidently, making reliability and transparency essential. I believe products should communicate uncertainty appropriately, provide supporting evidence where possible, and offer human review when needed. However, warnings should be proportionate to the risk to avoid overwhelming users.

### 5. Human Oversight and Automation

I do not believe greater automation always means better outcomes. Low-risk tasks may be automated, while high-impact decisions require stronger safeguards. Human oversight must also involve independent judgment rather than simply approving AI recommendations.

### 6. Build, Buy, or Use External AI

The discussion on building internal AI capabilities versus using external models made me think about long-term strategy. External solutions can accelerate development, while internal capabilities may offer greater control and differentiation. I would base this decision on customer value, cost, strategic importance, and vendor dependency rather than assuming that building everything internally is better.

### 7. Experimentation Before Scaling

I agree that rapid experimentation helps teams validate ideas before making significant investments. However, a successful prototype does not guarantee reliability, security, or commercial viability. My preferred approach is to experiment quickly, validate thoroughly, and scale only when the evidence supports it.

### 8. My Key Takeaway

This session helped me understand that AI product management is not about identifying where AI can be used, but where it should be used to create meaningful business and customer value.

Going forward, I want to focus on validating customer needs, measuring outcomes, understanding product economics, designing for uncertainty, and building customer trust. For me, successful AI product management means combining technological possibilities with sound business judgment and responsible decision-making.
