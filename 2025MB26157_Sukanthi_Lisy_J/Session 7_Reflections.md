# Session 7 - Personal Reflections

## 1. Start with the Problem, Not the Technology

My key takeaway from this session is that AI product management begins with identifying a real problem, not looking for ways to use AI. The electricity distribution example helped me understand that an AI-powered support system creates value only if it addresses actual customer concerns, such as delayed responses and uncertainty about power restoration.

Going forward, I want to define the problem clearly before choosing a solution. I also recognize the importance of staying open to new opportunities without losing sight of the original objective.

## 2. Take Ownership of the Product Experience

The discussion on product ownership made me realize that a product manager must understand the actual customer experience, not just rely on reports from technical teams. A product may work as intended technically but still frustrate customers if its responses are inaccurate or its process is complicated.

I would make a conscious effort to observe customer interactions, understand complaints, and identify gaps in the experience. At the same time, ownership does not mean doing everything personally; it means understanding the product well enough to guide teams and make informed decisions.

## 3. Connect Product Strategy with Business Value

The discussion on pricing reinforced the importance of understanding the business context. A customer may need an AI solution but may not be willing to pay a premium unless its benefits are clear and measurable.

For an electricity distribution company, value could come from reducing support costs, improving response times, or handling complaints more efficiently. I would therefore evaluate both the operational benefits and the financial case before making product or pricing decisions. I also learned that understanding the priorities of decision-makers is important because the people approving an investment may have different concerns from the end users.

## 4. Think Beyond the Immediate Problem

I found the idea of looking at the entire system particularly useful. A power outage is not just a customer support issue; it can also involve operational monitoring, engineer allocation, and communication across teams.

This reinforced the importance of understanding the complete customer journey rather than solving one isolated problem. However, I would be careful about expanding the product scope too quickly. Exploring adjacent opportunities, such as power consumption forecasting, may create value, but each opportunity must be assessed against the product’s strategy, resources, and business objectives.

## 5. Set Clear Boundaries for AI

The discussion on planned and unplanned outages highlighted that not every situation should be handled in the same way. AI may be effective for routine queries supported by reliable data, while complex or urgent situations may require human intervention.

My learning is that automation should depend on the risk involved and the reliability of the system. I would establish clear performance measures, monitor results after launch, and define when a query should be escalated to a human. I would rather have an AI product that handles routine tasks reliably and recognizes its limitations than one that attempts to answer everything.

## 6. Build Trust Beyond the Launch

One point that stood out to me was that launching a product is only the beginning. AI performance, data quality, and external service availability can change over time, so continuous monitoring is essential.

I also realized that managing customer expectations is part of product management. If a product makes promises it cannot consistently deliver, customer trust will suffer. Data consent, appropriate usage, retention, and deletion must also be considered throughout the product lifecycle.

I would therefore include monitoring, customer communication, data governance, and contingency planning in the product roadmap rather than treating them as activities to address after launch.

## 7. My Key Takeaway

This session helped me understand that effective AI product management requires a balance of customer understanding, business judgment, technical awareness, and long-term responsibility.

My biggest takeaway is to validate assumptions with evidence rather than rely on general expectations. Whether I am evaluating customer needs, pricing, or model performance, I need to understand the specific context before making decisions.

Going forward, I want to approach AI opportunities with curiosity and commercial discipline. My goal would be to build products that solve meaningful problems, deliver measurable business value, and earn customer trust over time—not simply products that demonstrate what AI can do.
