# Session 9 - Personal Reflections

## 1. Business Value Over Technical Accuracy

My biggest takeaway from this session is that AI products should be evaluated by the business value they create, not just their technical accuracy. A model may perform well statistically but still cause significant losses if its mistakes are costly.

The loan approval example helped me understand why the consequences of errors matter. As a product manager, I would evaluate expected savings, revenue, error costs, and potential losses before deciding whether an AI solution is worth the investment.

## 2. Look Beyond Average Performance

An AI product may perform well overall but fail in critical situations. I learned that evaluating average accuracy alone can hide risks that affect customers and the business.

I would assess worst-case scenarios and consider the financial, legal, operational, and reputational consequences of failure. However, safeguards should be proportionate to the use case. A low-risk recommendation does not require the same level of oversight as a financial or healthcare decision.

## 3. Monitor Performance After Launch

This session reinforced that launching an AI product is only the beginning. Model performance, customer expectations, technology costs, and market conditions can change over time.

I would establish clear success metrics before launch and review them regularly to identify areas for improvement. For me, AI product management is a continuous process of monitoring, learning, and adapting rather than a one-time implementation.

## 4. Experiment and Learn from Evidence

The discussion on A/B testing made me realize the importance of validating assumptions before drawing conclusions. An apparent improvement may be misleading if the sample is too small or other factors influence the results.

I would define a clear hypothesis, use appropriate control groups, and examine results across customer segments. I also believe an inconclusive experiment can provide useful learning if it helps refine the next test.

## 5. Choose Technology Based on Business Needs

I found the discussion on emerging AI technologies particularly useful. More advanced or expensive models are not necessarily the best choice for every problem.

I would evaluate technologies based on their effectiveness, cost, reliability, and associated risks rather than popularity alone. Regularly reviewing customer needs and competitor developments would also help me identify opportunities and prepare for changes in the market.

## 6. Balance Profitability with Responsibility

While I agree that economic value is essential, I believe AI decisions should not be judged by financial returns alone. Fairness, trust, customer well-being, and accountability are equally important in sensitive applications.

As a product manager, I would balance technical performance and commercial viability with stakeholder impact and ethical considerations. I may not need to build the model myself, but I must understand it well enough to challenge assumptions, assess risks, and make informed decisions.

## 7. My Key Takeaway

This session helped me see AI product management as a business leadership responsibility rather than purely a technological exercise.

Going forward, I want to focus on three priorities: measuring business outcomes rather than accuracy alone, monitoring performance after launch, and balancing commercial value with customer impact.

Ultimately, I believe a successful AI product is not the one with the most sophisticated technology, but the one that solves a meaningful problem reliably, responsibly, and sustainably.
