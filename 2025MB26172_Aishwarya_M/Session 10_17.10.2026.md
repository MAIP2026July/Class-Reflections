In this session we discussed scaling of AI products. What is important is to provide the promised product to the users. In a hospital, a small delay in response may lead to poor outcomes for the patient. Just like that, if product fails to perform well when it has to, users will lose trust immediately. We also discussed the trade-offs between quality, latency, and cost. As a product manager, our decisions carry so much weight. We have to balance several aspects and choose between say technical advancements and cost/budget. So, it is always a thin line and knowing that the decision is the most appropriate gives satisfaction to us more than anything. Also, a good leader must know how to balance and protect their team. Tracking changes and versioning is equally important for having a reliable rollback plan if an AI model starts performing poorly. This, I found very similar to how we maintain strict audit trails and protocols in hospital quality management. We have to track every metric not to prove anything, but to make ourselves better. In the same way, for a product. Having good documentation helps us revisit decisions and changes quickly and helps in governance and control.
