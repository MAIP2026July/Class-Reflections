Finance metrics and monetization of a product or service (when considering healthcare programs) cannot be only the responsibility of the finance team. The PM must take ownership of this component too to ensure that the product is useful, and also profitable to the firm. I used to think that pricing was simple. Calculate the cost incurred and add desired margin to make the final price. This discussion made me understand that pricing decisions have multiple layers. What is the value that one derives from a product and how much a “user” is willing to pay is as important as our production and operating costs. Another important point was to note that users are not much interested in the computational input or infrastructure effort required to generate value that a product provides. This I found to be very similar to healthcare wherein we are cognizant of clinical outcomes and quality improvement  gains, not the underlying systems deployed. As PMs, we must carefully test our costing models and try to balance the trade-off between quality, cost, and reliability.
We also discussed the importance of prioritizing user retention over rapid user growth. Yes, even in the hospital, patient trust and long term retention is more important than just increasing one time foot falls. Long-term adoption of an AI product and value add it gives should become a priority not the initial hype it creates. I think one example I can give is the in-trend Chat GPT images. They work for sometime till the social media trends peak but what value they can provide long term must be looked into. I wish to use some of the learnings from this when we are asked to evaluate an AI product proposal for my hospital.
