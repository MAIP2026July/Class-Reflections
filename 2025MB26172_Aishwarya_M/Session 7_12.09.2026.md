We discussed that PMs cannot remain detached from the products they are responsible for. Even when technical teams, vendors, customer support teams, or external partners are involved, ownership of the overall outcome is with the PM. This is a recurring learning point that I think any PM must embed. 
One part of the discussion that resonated with me was the emphasis on staying close to users rather than managing entirely through reports and dashboards. I have got very useful insights from directly interacting with clinicians, patients, and operational teams and not through reviewing BI dashboards at work. Many issues become obvious only when one spends time understanding how work is actually being performed on the ground.
We had a productive conversation around a sample case - customer support issue in electricity distribution wherein each of us pitched in with our view of what could be the ideal solution for the problem given. It was a useful reminder that many business problems are interconnected. Solving one visible issue often requires understanding several underlying processes. We also discussed the PFIR evaluation model: facts/features, inferences, and recommendations.
A product must also demonstrate its value in a way that customers are willing to pay for. I have faced this often at workplace. For any initiative, value must be measurable and visible. Then only we will be able to get management buy in. The same principle appears to apply to products.
There are a lot of assumptions in AI systems. Data quality, consent, bias, uncertainty, and explainability are often labelled as technical issues. These ultimately affect the product. A technically strong model may still fail if the underlying assumptions are incorrect or if stakeholders do not trust its outputs. 
Also, we always connotate launch with “success” or the end point. Launch of a product is just the beginning of another phase where monitoring, customer feedback, trust building, and course corrections become equally important. Delivering a solution and ensuring its sustained success are two different challenges.
