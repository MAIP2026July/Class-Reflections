## Class 1 -- Understanding Product Management as a Role of Ownership (25 July 2026)

The first lecture was not what I had expected. I assumed that we would
begin by exploring Artificial Intelligence and its practical
applications. Instead, the discussion centred on the actual
responsibilities of a Product Manager and what the role truly involves.

At the beginning, several students described a Product Manager as a
bridge between business and technology teams, someone who collects
requirements or coordinates stakeholders. Our professor encouraged us to
move beyond these descriptions and look at the role through the lens of
**ownership**. He compared a Product Manager to the CEO of a product,
which gave me a different way of looking at the position.

The cricket examples helped explain this idea. A captain is appreciated
when the team wins, even if the captain has not scored any runs. In the
same way, an excellent individual performance does not change the fact
that the team has lost if the overall result is poor. I understood that
a Product Manager must be answerable for the product's overall
performance and cannot simply attribute failures to engineering,
marketing or another department. This degree of accountability may be
demanding, but it is central to the role.

A classmate also raised the question of whether Product Management can
become a responsibility for which a person receives little appreciation.
I found the question relatable because similar situations occur in
workplaces. Our professor explained that every role comes with its own
frustrations and difficult moments, both at work and in personal life.
That does not make the effort worthless. However, if someone repeatedly
feels that their contribution is not valued, they may need to assess
whether the role is suitable for them instead of continuing with
resentment.

**Key Learnings:**

-   **Accountability:** Work can be delegated across a team, but
    responsibility for the product's overall result remains with the
    Product Manager.
-   **Influence Without Direct Authority:** Product Managers often have
    to bring people together and influence decisions even when those
    people do not report to them. I understood that this ability is
    fundamental to the role.
-   **Understanding the Domain:** The job is not limited to transferring
    information between departments. Sound decisions require knowledge
    of the product, its operating environment and the people it is
    intended to serve.

**My Perspective:** I agree that ownership is essential, but putting it
into practice can be difficult in large organisations. A Product Manager
may be held responsible for results while having limited control over
engineering capacity, budgets or wider organisational choices. For
ownership to be meaningful, the organisation should also provide
appropriate authority and room to make decisions.

**How I Intend to Use This at Work:** In my current role, I have
sometimes treated platform-related responsibilities mainly as
coordination activities. Following this discussion, I want to pay closer
attention to the decisions I can own and the outcomes for which I am
accountable. I also want to avoid escalating matters unnecessarily when
I have enough information and authority to make a decision myself. This
should help me build a stronger ownership mindset and approach my work
more like a product professional.
