## Class 13 -- Presenting an AI Solution as a Sustainable Business (14 November 2026)

This was the first day of project presentations. Our professor treated
the sessions as pitches to potential venture capital investors rather
than as simple product demonstrations. He also told us that feedback
shared with one team should be considered by every team. We were
expected to create a GitHub issue for each relevant point in our own
repository and close it with a comment if it was not applicable.

The main areas of feedback that I noted, and that I also need to
consider for our project, were:

-   **Make Swimlane Diagrams Clear:** Each role should have its own
    lane. AI activities should be labelled separately as AI1, AI2 and so
    on, and human involvement should be clearly marked. The diagram
    should also show the points where AI-generated results are checked
    or approved before they reach the final recipient.
-   **Explain AI Across Three Areas:** Teams should state whether AI is
    being used to create Product Management artefacts, assist with the
    development process and/or operate as part of the product itself.
-   **Be Explicit About Who Pays for AI Usage:** The business model
    should explain who covers API consumption. The customer may use and
    pay for their own API key, or the provider may include those costs
    in the product price.
-   **Separate the Provider's Business Case from Customer ROI:** These
    are two different financial views. The startup's plan should show
    development expenses, expected customer numbers, revenue and
    profitability over time to explain the investment opportunity to a
    VC. Separately, the customer's ROI should compare the product's
    price with the savings or benefits it delivers. Some teams,
    including teams with promising ideas, had mixed these two
    calculations together.
-   **Use a Defensible Value-Sharing Assumption:** A customer may share
    roughly 18--25% of the value created with the product provider. On
    that basis, the customer would generally expect to receive savings
    worth around three to four times the product's price. If dependable
    market benchmarks are not available, this can be used as an initial
    assumption for planning.
-   **Challenge Financial Estimates:** We should test our calculations
    under different scenarios. For example, if the same work could be
    outsourced to a country where labour is cheaper, would buying our
    solution still be financially attractive?
-   **Allow for Conditional Pilot Outcomes:** A pilot should not always
    end with a simple yes or no. A conditional approval may be
    appropriate when the product can proceed after specific changes or
    requirements have been completed.
-   **Know Whether We Are Building a Product or a Project:** Our
    professor strongly emphasised that a solution created only for one
    organisation should not automatically be described as a product. A
    genuine product should be capable of serving a wider customer base
    rather than remaining a one-off customised delivery.
-   **Assess All Four Dimensions of Viability:** Teams should examine
    domain understanding, technical feasibility, regulatory requirements
    and customer behaviour. The last area includes both the positive
    reasons people may adopt a solution and the concerns that may
    prevent them from doing so.

One particularly interesting moment was the professor's decision to use
the Idea Hub team's product to evaluate all the GitHub submissions. He
described the approach as "dog eat dog food". The team could make
changes to meet the course's requirements, but it could not create a
separate customised version for every team. That would turn the solution
into a collection of projects instead of a reusable product. I thought
this was a practical way of demonstrating product generalisation.

**My Personal Reflection:** The discussions on pricing and financial
modelling made me aware of a gap in my own skills. I am fairly
comfortable estimating technical expenses, including token usage and
infrastructure, but I have less experience presenting a convincing
calculation of customer ROI. I need to become better at linking a
product's price to measurable savings and business outcomes. This is an
important capability to develop as I build my understanding of Product
Management.
