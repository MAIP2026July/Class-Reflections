# Reflection: Session 2

**1 August 2026 | Usha Talatala | 2025MB26177**

### The story that landed

* I understood that a **roadmap is not a fixed commitment**. Priorities may change based on new evidence and uncertainty, so some decisions can be deferred rather than taking them blindly.
* The **beta is not the end of the product**. It is more like halfway, because a lot of learning and improvement happens after beta as well.
* I liked the idea that product development is a **continuous process**. Customer needs, technology and market conditions keep changing, so the product also needs to evolve.
* I had not worked much with **product management artifacts** earlier. I would like to use them more consciously in my future projects to capture decisions, assumptions and learning.
* The Apple iPhone example made me think about the difference between **asking customers what they want and identifying something they may not yet be able to ask for**.
* The Tata Motors example also showed me that an existing capability can sometimes be **reused in another market**, but it still needs to be evaluated for the new requirement.

### Where I disagree

Even after multiple iterations, I have seen leadership changes bring new priorities and changes in scope. So, although good planning and roadmaps are important, in real projects priorities can keep changing because of changes at the leadership level.

### What I will do

* I will try to **build with scalability in mind**, instead of only solving the immediate requirement.
* I will maintain a **learning journal** and capture lessons from my projects so that I don't repeat the same mistakes.
* I already participate in sprint/release retrospectives, but I want to use them more effectively to record **what worked, what did not work and what we should change next time**.
* I would also like to preserve my MBA notes and learning for future reference instead of treating each topic as something only for the exam.

### Open questions

1. I would like to understand **decision gates** better — what exactly are the criteria used to decide whether a product should move to the next stage?
2. I would like to understand **PRFAQs** better and how they are actually used during product planning.
