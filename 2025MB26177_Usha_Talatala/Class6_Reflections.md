# Reflection: Session 6

**5 September 2026 | Usha Talatala | 2025MB26177**

### The story that landed

* I understood that product development must **start with value and solving customer problems rather than pushing complex technology**. Customers pay for solved problems and outcomes, not fancy UI or AI labels.
* Standardizing **stakeholder terminology in PRDs** (e.g., using explicit role statements like *"I as a [Role] want [Feature]"*) is essential to keep teams aligned and prevent requirements from turning into technical jargon.
* The emphasis on **re-imagining legacy workflows** and exploring the "art of the possible" showed me that product managers must challenge past technology constraints to eliminate friction and streamline operations.
* Data privacy laws such as **DPDP in India and GDPR in Europe** mean organizations are only trustees of user data. Neglecting consent, data leakage, and cybersecurity risks will leave products "dead on arrival".
* Simple positive/negative/neutral sentiment analysis is insufficient for PMs. **Feature-level and aspect-based sentiment extraction (quintuple extraction: target, feature, sentiment value, opinion holder, time/context)** is required to capture actionable insights and human emotions behind customer feedback.

### Where I disagree

While starting with customer value rather than technology is ideal, in high-throughput enterprise platforms like web payment gateways, underlying infrastructure upgrades or mandatory compliance changes often require tech-led investments before direct end-user value can be explicitly demonstrated.

### What I will do

* I will apply the "art of the possible" to **re-imagine merchant onboarding and payment checkout flows**, eliminating unnecessary input fields and reducing setup friction.
* I will standardize PRD persona descriptions across web payment platforms (e.g., *"I as a Merchant Developer want API key management..."* vs. *"I as a Shopper want 1-click offer redemption..."*).
* I will partner closely with security teams to ensure strict **data privacy governance and consent management** are integrated into web transaction architectures by design.
* I will move beyond overall CSAT scores by implementing **aspect-based feedback tracking** on specific merchant dashboard tools, UI widgets, and API integrations.

### Open questions

1. How do product teams balance value-first product design when regulatory compliance mandates immediate back-end architectural changes with no visible front-end benefits?
2. What are the best methods to capture aspect-based sentiment on developer-facing web portal APIs where feedback is mostly technical logs rather than textual reviews?
