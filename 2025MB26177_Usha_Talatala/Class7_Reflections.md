# Reflection: Session 6

**12 September 2026 | Usha Talatala | 2025MB26177**

### The story that landed

* I realized that product development must always **start with customer value and business problems** rather than pushing technology for its own sake. Customers pay for solved problems and outcomes, not fancy tech labels.
* The importance of **standardized stakeholder language in PRDs** resonated strongly. Writing explicit requirements from specific user perspectives keeps technical teams focused on actual needs rather than technical jargon.
* I liked the concept of **re-imagining legacy workflows**. Product managers need to challenge old constraints, eliminate unnecessary friction, and streamline operations using modern capabilities.
* The emphasis on **data consent and privacy regulations (such as DPDP and GDPR)** was a critical takeaway. Organizations are merely trustees of user data, and ignoring data governance introduces major risks.
* The discussion on **aspect-based sentiment analysis** showed why surface-level sentiment metrics are insufficient. PMs need feature-level feedback to extract actionable insights.

### Where I disagree

While starting with value rather than technology is ideal, in enterprise platform environments like web payment portals, infrastructure shifts or compliance upgrades often force technology-led changes before direct customer value can be articulated.

### What I will do

* I will apply the "art of the possible" lens to **re-imagine merchant onboarding and checkout workflows** in payment portals to reduce friction and eliminate unnecessary form fields.
* I will standardize PRD user stories across payment personas (e.g., merchant developers vs. end shoppers) to ensure clear requirements.
* I will work closely with security and compliance teams to ensure **data privacy and PCI-DSS/DPDP governance** are built into web platform features by design.
* I will move beyond aggregate CSAT scores and collect **aspect-based feedback** on specific payment gateway and dashboard features.

### Open questions

1. How do you balance value-first product design when regulatory compliance demands immediate architectural changes with no direct user-facing benefit?
2. What are the best practices for structuring PRD requirements when a single feature serves both B2B merchant developers and B2C end consumers?
