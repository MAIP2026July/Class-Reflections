# Class 1 Reflection

**Date:** 25 July 2026  
**Topic:** Course introduction and the role of the product manager

The idea I carried away from the first class was that a product manager cannot stop at being a “bridge” between business and technology. A bridge only passes things from one side to another. The product manager has to make decisions and own what finally happens.

The cricket example made this very easy to understand. A captain may play well and still lose, or personally perform badly while the team wins. In both cases, the team result matters. It also made me think about how easy it is to say that engineering missed a date or marketing did not support the launch. Those reasons may be true, but they do not improve the product.

I also liked the database example, where the most advanced option was rejected because it depended on a rare skill. Earlier, I might have seen that as settling for a weaker technology. Now I can see it as a practical product decision about people, maintenance and future flexibility.

For this course, I want to keep checking whether I am actually making a decision or only forwarding someone else's opinion. That feels like a useful test of ownership.
