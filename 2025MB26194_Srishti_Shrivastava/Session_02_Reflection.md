# Class 2 Reflection

**Date:** 1 August 2026  
**Topic:** Product lifecycle, decision gates, artifacts and frameworks

Before this class, I pictured the product lifecycle as a neat sequence: discover, define, build, launch and grow. The discussion made it clear that real products rarely behave so neatly. A problem found during development can take the team back to discovery, and customer feedback after launch can change what the team thought it had already settled.

The decision-gate idea was useful because a gate is supposed to ask whether the evidence is good enough for the next investment. It should not become an approval meeting that everyone attends because the calendar says so. Stopping or changing direction can also be a valid outcome.

I had also not thought enough about keeping a record of why a decision was taken. Requirements and designs change, and after a few months people may remember the decision but forget the reason behind it. A short decision record could prevent the team from repeating an old discussion without context.

There were many frameworks in the session, and I am unlikely to remember all of them during actual work. My takeaway is simpler: use a framework when it sharpens a decision, but do not let the framework replace judgment.
