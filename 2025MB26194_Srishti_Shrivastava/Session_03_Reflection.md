# Class 3 Reflection

**Date:** 18 August 2026  
**Topic:** Frameworks in practice, product versus project, decision rights and pricing

One small point from this class stayed with me: do not keep saying “the user” without naming who that person actually is. A frontline worker, an L&D manager and a subject-matter expert may all use Wayfinder AI, but they come with completely different goals. Calling all of them users makes a requirement sound complete when it is still vague.

The difference between a product and a project was also clearer than I expected. A solution can work very well for one organization and still be a project. It becomes a product only when the underlying problem, design and delivery can be repeated for other customers. That is a useful warning for our project because the first customer's SOP structure should not become a hard-coded rule for everyone else.

I also liked the sprint discussion. Treating the sprint as a short agreement feels more practical than saying that nothing can ever change. The team protects the current commitment, then renegotiates when the boundary arrives.

Finally, the pricing discussion reminded me that customers do not pay for our effort. They pay because the product creates value for them. We need to show that value in a way they can measure.
