# Class 4 Reflection

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Topic:** When AI is appropriate, legitimate and valuable

The most useful change in my thinking today was the order of questions. Before asking whether an AI idea is valuable or technically possible, we should ask whether it is legitimate. If it depends on hidden monitoring, weak consent or unfair treatment, then a good business case does not make it a good product.

I also realized how quickly I jump from a problem to an LLM. The class gave a better order: first check whether a rule can solve it, then consider search or analytics, then predictive models, and only use generative or agentic AI when it genuinely adds something. The most advanced solution can easily become the most expensive and least dependable one.

The point about timing sounded obvious, but I had not framed it clearly before. An accurate recommendation has no value if it reaches the person after the moment to act has passed. For a frontline worker, the answer has to arrive during the task, not later in a report.

For Wayfinder AI, this means AI should help with drafting and finding knowledge, while permissions and approvals remain explicit. The system also needs a sensible response when approved information is missing instead of making up an answer.
