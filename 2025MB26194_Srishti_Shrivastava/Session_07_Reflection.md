# Class 7 Reflection

**Date:** 12 September 2026  
**Topic:** DISCOM case, value based pricing, FIR framework and lifecycle

The DISCOM case was useful because it forced me to decide whose perspective I was taking. The utility, the end customer and the product company do not have the same problem. If that distinction is not clear, the buyer, user and value proposition all become mixed together.

The shift from reacting to complaints toward noticing a likely outage earlier was the most interesting part. Instead of waiting for a customer to call, the product could identify an unusual consumption pattern, confirm the situation and help the utility respond. The value comes from changing the process, not only from making complaint handling faster.

I also liked the distinction between facts, inferences and recommendations. Repeating an observed fact carries less risk than inferring what it means, and recommending an action carries even more responsibility. The further the product moves from evidence toward action, the stronger the review should be.

That applies directly to Wayfinder AI. Quoting an approved SOP is different from summarizing it, and both are different from telling a worker what to do in a new situation. We should be clear about those boundaries instead of treating every answer as equally reliable.
