# Class 11 Reflection

**Date:** 24 October 2026  
**Topic:** Ethics, bias, privacy and responsible AI

This class made responsible AI feel much more practical. Writing that a product will be fair or protect privacy is easy. The difficult part is explaining what fairness means for that decision, who could be harmed and what happens when someone believes the system treated them wrongly.

The examples of biased associations showed how a model can learn a narrow pattern without anyone deliberately programming it to discriminate. That made me think about representation in the data, but also about monitoring the outcome after launch. A dataset can look acceptable while a particular group still receives worse results.

The privacy discussion gave me a simple question to use: can we explain why every piece of information is needed? Collecting data because it may be useful later creates risk without a clear product benefit. Access, retention and deletion also need actual rules, not only a privacy statement.

For Wayfinder AI, workers should see only content they are authorized to access. Their questions should not quietly become a way to judge competence or performance. If the system lacks reliable support for a consequential answer, it should abstain and send the question to a responsible person. That is a product feature, not a failure.
