# Class 12 Reflection

**Date:** 31 October 2026  
**Topic:** Buildable PRDs, diagrams, pricing and unit economics

The strongest lesson from this class was how expensive vague language can become. Words such as accurate, fast, easy or reduced sound sensible, but different people can interpret them differently. Saying that consultation time will be reduced could technically mean half a second. A useful requirement needs a baseline and a target.

I also understood the difference between a delivery plan and a roadmap more clearly. The first version needs enough detail for people to build and launch it. Later versions are still assumptions and should not be presented with the same certainty. A swimlane diagram can expose missing decisions because it shows who does what and where the AI or human review enters the workflow.

The pricing discussion was the other part that stayed with me. Customers would rather pay for an outcome, while suppliers prefer charging for inputs because the risk is lower. The chosen model changes who carries the risk when usage or results differ from expectations.

For Wayfinder AI, we need measurable targets for content-update time, answer resolution and faithfulness to approved sources. We also need to watch model and review costs. A useful product can still become a weak business if serving each additional customer costs too much.
