# Class 13 Reflection

**Date:** 14 November 2026  
**Topic:** Project presentations day 1

The first presentation session showed how quickly a promising idea can become weak when the assumptions underneath it are questioned. The repeated questions were not really about slide design. They were about who would pay, what problem was important enough to solve, and whether the solution could work beyond one organization.

I found the difference between the investor case and the customer case particularly useful. Investors want to understand growth, revenue and defensibility. Customers want to know whether the benefit is worth the price and the effort of adoption. Using the same calculation for both audiences can make the argument confusing.

The feedback on labour-cost estimates was also a good warning. It is easy to select a high hourly cost and produce an attractive ROI. A buyer may compare the product with a cheaper employee, an offshore team or the current process instead. The assumption has to survive that comparison.

For our Wayfinder AI pitch, I want the market and savings figures to be easy to trace. If a number is still an estimate, we should say so and explain what a pilot would need to validate. That sounds more credible than presenting every number as settled.
