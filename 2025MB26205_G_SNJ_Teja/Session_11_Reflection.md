# Session 11 Reflection

**Date:** 24 October 2026

This class was on bias, privacy and governance. The Gurkha example explained bias simply: a child who has only seen Gurkha security guards assumes every guard is one, not out of bad intent but from limited exposure. Models trained on narrow data do the same. When a classmate asked for one framework to handle bias, the answer was that there isn't one; follow the law and guidelines and keep improving, because even guidelines can carry old bias.

The privacy advice was very direct: collect only what you could justify to a regulator, because data you never collect can never be misused. "Data is a drug" is a good way to describe how teams keep wanting more. This applies to my project, since AdmitAssist deals with parents' phone numbers and children's details, so I need a clear section on what is collected, how consent is taken, how long it is kept and which rules apply. For hallucinations, the answer, abstain or escalate option was the most practical. A system that says "I don't know, let me pass this to the office" is better than one that guesses. On governance, I noted that committees advise but the product owner stays accountable, and that reviewers who get no time for reviews will just approve. For risk, detectability and reversibility matter along with probability and severity.
