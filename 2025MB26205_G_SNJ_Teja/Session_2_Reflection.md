# Session 2 Reflection

**Date:** 1 August 2026

The line I kept coming back to after this class was that a product is built for lots of customers you don't know yet, while a project can start with just one. I had never really thought about it that way. It's easy to build something for one client, call it a product, and only later realise it doesn't work for anyone else. The lifecycle discussion added to this for me, because I used to see launch as the finish line, and the class showed it's more of a checkpoint in a loop where you keep learning and adjusting. The credit card story made that real: the team thought beta meant they were nearly done, but a whole piece of the work was still waiting. "Beta is only halfway" is something I'm going to remember, especially because a team pushed hard to reach beta is already tired when the harder part starts.

I found the "don't pretend certainty" idea useful but also a bit tricky. Delaying decisions until you know more makes sense, like fixing the structure of a house early and leaving the paint colour for later. My worry is that "deferring" can quietly turn into avoiding a difficult call, so it seems important to say clearly what's decided, what's still open and when it will be decided. We also covered a lot of frameworks very quickly and I didn't absorb all of them, but the point I did take away is that a decision should show up in the design, the build and the testing, not just in the meeting where it was made. I'm still not sure how a PM knows when there's "enough evidence" to launch. I suspect it's mostly judgement, and I'd like to understand that better as the course goes on.
