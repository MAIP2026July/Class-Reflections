# Session 3 Reflection

**Date:** 18 August 2026

"Progress matters more than features" sounds obvious, but this session made me see how easy it is to get wrong. You can keep shipping features and still not help anyone get closer to what they were trying to do. It also made me more careful about the words "user" and "customer." A buyer, an admin and an everyday user of the same product can want completely different things, so I think the first step is being clear about who exactly you're building for. I was a bit sceptical about the talk of "transformation," since in real companies a lot of things get called that when they're really just a faster or cheaper version of what already exists. A small improvement can still be worth doing, but it helps to be honest about how big the change actually is. I liked the point about product layers (a generic core, then an industry layer, then a thin layer of customer-specific changes) and about saying no when one customer wants something that would bend the whole product.

The part I found most relevant was about AI. If a customer can put together something similar over a weekend with an LLM and a few API calls, then knowing how to build it isn't much of an advantage anymore. That left me wondering what makes a product hard to copy: maybe the data, how well it fits into someone's daily work, or trust. I don't have a clear answer yet. The other thing I want to remember is that frameworks are there to help me think, not to think for me. I can see how stacking more and more of them might feel rigorous without actually making the decision any clearer. I also want to read more about Jobs to Be Done, because starting from the progress someone wants to make feels more useful than starting from a list of features.
