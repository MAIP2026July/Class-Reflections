# Session 4 Reflection

**Date:** 22 August 2026

The question that stayed with me from this class was a small change in wording: not "can AI do this?" but "should AI do this?" It's easy to get excited about what the technology can do and forget to ask whether it's actually needed. If a simple rule, a checklist or a basic search can solve the problem, then bringing in a complex model feels like making things harder for no reason. I liked the idea of starting with the simplest option and only moving up to something heavier when there's a real need. It also made me notice how often "let's add AI" is said just because it's the trend right now.

What I found more thought-provoking was how much weight the class put on what happens when the AI is wrong. Average accuracy can look great while one rare mistake does serious damage, so safeguards and a clear way to escalate matter as much as the model itself. The farmer example also stuck with me: a correct warning that arrives too late is of no use to anyone, so timing matters as much as accuracy. I was also glad we talked about "keeping a human in the loop," because I used to think that alone solves the risk. But if the person just keeps clicking approve, it's not really oversight. I don't have a good answer yet on how to design a process where people genuinely stay alert, but I'll be thinking about it for our own product work.
