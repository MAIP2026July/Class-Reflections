# Session 8 Reflection

**Date:** 3 October 2026

What stood out most in this class was how unpredictable AI can be once you start building with it. The professor shared that the same coding tools and the same instructions can work perfectly one day and stall the next, and that you can't always tell if the cause is the model's own randomness or the provider changing something behind the scenes. During development that's just annoying, because you can roll back. In production it's scarier, since you can't check every answer and may never know which customer got a poor one. The insurance example from a classmate made this concrete for me: when a business needs the same number every time, like a premium rating, a rule-based approach may simply be the better choice than an LLM. I also liked the warning about the PM becoming the bottleneck. When AI produces work quickly, it's tempting to keep saying "go ahead," but small wrong assumptions slip in (his example of the tool deciding there must always be three banks) and fixing them later costs more than checking early.

The second half was about how to grow an idea safely: prototype, then pilot, then scale, with a clear go or no-go gate at each step so a team has to earn the next stage. A few things I'll remember: a prototype is not a product, it's fine to automate only part of the work as long as the core output is useful, and if something is still manual, say so openly instead of hiding it. I also noticed that writing down the success criteria upfront protects the team from a PM who keeps changing the rules. Some of it was a bit abstract for me, and I'm still unsure how to size governance so it helps instead of slowing people down. The feedback on our project ideas was a good reminder too: don't build for just one company, describe exactly who your customer is, and look for ways to make the product work for other markets. I'll try that with our own idea this week.
