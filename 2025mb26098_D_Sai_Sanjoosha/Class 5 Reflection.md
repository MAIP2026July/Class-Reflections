Identifying Opportunities for AI Products

This session focused on identifying opportunities where Artificial Intelligence can be used to create meaningful product value. I understood that identifying the right opportunity is one of the most important responsibilities of a product manager. Instead of starting with a technology and searching for ways to use it, we should begin by understanding customer problems and identifying areas where existing solutions are insufficient.
One point that stood out to me was the discussion about not using AI simply because it is a popular or trending technology. Organizations may introduce AI into their products to create a competitive advantage or attract customers, but if it does not improve the actual customer experience, it may not provide sustainable value.
I found this particularly relevant because many organizations are currently exploring AI adoption across different business functions. However, not every process requires an AI-based solution. Some problems can be addressed through traditional automation, process improvements, or simpler technology solutions. The product manager must evaluate these alternatives before deciding on the final approach.
Another important learning was the need to validate the assumptions behind a proposed product. A product idea may appear useful initially, but it should be examined from the customer's perspective. We need to understand whether the problem is significant, whether customers will benefit from the proposed solution, and whether the expected value can be demonstrated.
I also understood the importance of connecting classroom learning with practical project work. Applying the concepts discussed during the sessions to an actual project helps identify gaps and provides an opportunity to test assumptions early rather than waiting until the final stage.
My key takeaway: This session reinforced the idea that successful AI products are driven by customer needs and business value, not by technology alone. As a future manager, I should be able to question whether AI is genuinely required and justify its use through measurable benefits.
