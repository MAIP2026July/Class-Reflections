AI Product Risks, Data Governance and Responsible AI

This class helped me understand the importance of responsible AI development, particularly in relation to data governance, privacy, consent, and security. Before this session, I mainly associated AI product development with identifying business problems, collecting data, and building models. However, the discussion made me realize that the responsible use of data is equally important for the success and sustainability of an AI product.
One of the key topics discussed was the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act and the importance of obtaining appropriate consent before collecting and processing personal information. Organizations need to be transparent about what data they collect, why they collect it, and how it will be used. Improper data collection or misuse of personal information can create legal issues and damage customer trust.
Another concept I found interesting was algorithmic bias. AI systems learn from the data provided to them, and if the data contains biases, the model may produce unfair or discriminatory outcomes. Even when an AI system produces results with high confidence, those results may not necessarily be accurate or fair. Therefore, product managers need to consider the quality, reliability, and representativeness of the data used.
We also discussed data leakage, cybersecurity risks, and the concept of blast radius. I understood that organizations should evaluate the possible impact of a data breach or security incident, including the extent of information exposed and the people affected.
An important point from the session was that product managers do not necessarily have to perform every technical activity themselves, but they remain accountable for ensuring that the right controls and safeguards are in place.
My key takeaway: I learned that building a successful AI product is not just about technical performance. Privacy, security, fairness, consent, and regulatory compliance must be considered from the beginning. Customer trust is an important part of product value, and without responsible data practices, even a technically strong product can fail.
