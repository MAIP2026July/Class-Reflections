AI Model Evaluation, Testing and Continuous Monitoring

This session provided a deeper understanding of how business requirements are translated into AI-based solutions and how the performance of AI models should be evaluated before and after deployment.
One of the most important points I learned was that a product manager must ensure that the team is solving the correct business problem. Sometimes organizations identify a business challenge and immediately try to convert it into a machine learning problem without properly understanding the actual requirement. The product manager plays an important role in defining the problem clearly and determining whether AI or machine learning is the appropriate approach.
We discussed data collection, data quality, model development, and evaluation techniques such as classification and clustering. I understood that model performance should be measured based on the specific business use case. For example, the required accuracy of a classification model depends on the purpose for which it is being used and the consequences of incorrect predictions. There is no single performance threshold that can be applied to every AI product.
The discussion on alpha and beta testing was also useful. A model that performs well during training and testing may not necessarily deliver the same results in a real-world environment. Therefore, testing with actual users and evaluating the system under practical conditions are important before wider deployment.
Another major learning was the importance of continuous monitoring. Unlike traditional software, AI products may experience changes in performance due to changing data patterns, model behaviour, or external dependencies. Monitoring helps organizations identify these issues and take corrective action.
The session also highlighted the importance of understanding different AI techniques and their suitability for specific business applications.
My key takeaway: I realized that launching an AI product is not the final step. Product managers must continuously evaluate its performance, monitor changes, and ensure that it continues to meet customer and business expectations. A successful AI product requires ongoing improvement and not just a successful initial implementation.
