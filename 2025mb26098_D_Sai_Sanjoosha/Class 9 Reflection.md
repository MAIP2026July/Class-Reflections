AI Product Innovation, Experimentation and Validation

This session helped me understand that developing an AI product is not just about identifying a problem and selecting an AI model to solve it. A major part of product management involves experimenting with different approaches and validating whether AI can genuinely deliver value in a particular business context.
One important point discussed was the difference between traditional AI and generative AI. Traditional AI models are generally more predictable when the inputs and other conditions remain the same, whereas generative AI may produce different responses even for similar inputs. This distinction is important when deciding which technology to use for a product because the level of predictability required depends on the business problem being addressed.
Another important learning was that a product manager is not necessarily expected to build the entire technical solution independently. The responsibility is more about understanding the problem, identifying opportunities, guiding the development process and ensuring that the product delivers the expected value. This was a useful perspective because it highlighted the importance of management and decision-making skills alongside technical awareness.
The discussion on experimentation was particularly interesting. Not every AI application or technique is already proven to work for every business problem. Product ideas are often based on hypotheses about how AI could improve an existing process or solve a new problem. These hypotheses need to be tested and evaluated before making commitments to customers. Improvements could be measured through factors such as cost reduction, speed, accuracy or overall user experience.
The session also highlighted the importance of having something unique in a product. Simply offering a product that already exists in the market may not be enough to attract customers. There should be an additional benefit or competitive advantage that gives customers a reason to trust and adopt it. The discussion also distinguished between validating whether a product feature works and determining whether there is sufficient market demand for it.
My key takeaway: An AI product should not be developed just because a particular technology is available. It should be based on a genuine customer problem, supported by experimentation, validated through evidence and differentiated by the value it delivers.
