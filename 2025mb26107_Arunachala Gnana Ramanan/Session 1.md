Topic : Course orientation — what this course is, class protocols, the three pillars of AI in this course

Half the first session was housekeeping, but the housekeeping itself told me a lot about how this course is going to run. No page limits beyond "one page, top to bottom," open use of LLMs as long as we disclose the prompts and the model, and reflections that are graded but explicitly not supposed to be summaries — all of that signals a course built around judgment, not recall. That's a relief and a challenge at the same time; it means I can't hide behind a framework I memorized, I actually have to take a position.
The line that's stayed with me all week is the frame around product management being a "thankless job." His response wasn't to disagree — it was to reframe it the way I'd reframe a rough week with my kids: someone is always going to be unhappy with a decision you made in good faith, and the measure of the job isn't the absence of friction, it's whether things are visibly better than they were a cycle ago. He tied that back to his own story of launching the world's first internet payments product in 1997 — and was refreshingly honest that he didn't personally make the money he expected from it, but he still talks about it with pride decades later. That distinction between reward and payoff is one I want to sit with, because I catch myself measuring my own work purely in terms of appraisal outcomes rather than "did this get meaningfully better."
One thing I'm going to actively try to change: he called out that the best learning in this course comes from participating live, not scanning slides afterward. Given my calendar, that's going to be a real discipline problem for me — something to be honest about rather than pretend I'll manage perfectly.
