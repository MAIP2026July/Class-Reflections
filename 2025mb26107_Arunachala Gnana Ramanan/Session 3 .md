Topic: Recap and self-learnings round; qualifying "user"; mandatory vs. desirable features over time; incremental improvement vs. transformation; product vs. project


The recap format this time — where classmates volunteered their own takeaways before the new material started — was genuinely useful, and a little humbling; hearing Jagannath's point about "progress being more important than features" land so cleanly made me realize I'd only absorbed that idea loosely the first time around. The instructor's insistence that we stop saying "user" or "customer" without qualifying exactly who we mean is a small discipline that I think will save real confusion later in our project documentation.
The part of this session I keep coming back to wasn't even on the slide — the aside about senior citizens being nudged into smartphones and digital-only services they didn't ask for, and whether we're quietly taking away the independence of the generation that built the world we now take for granted. There's no framework for that, and he was honest that he didn't have a tidy answer either. It's the kind of thing I want to actually raise with my own parents rather than file away as a classroom moment.
The caterpillar-vs-butterfly metaphor for transformation vs. incremental improvement is going straight into my vocabulary at work, mostly because I've sat through more than one leadership deck that called a 5% efficiency gain a "transformation." His point that this labeling is often rational — teams deliberately hold back gains so they have something to show next year — was an uncomfortable but honest read of how incentive systems actually shape behavior.
