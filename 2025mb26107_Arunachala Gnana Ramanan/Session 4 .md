Topic: Where and when AI creates real product value; legitimacy, viability, and bounded-learning gates; human-in-the-loop; bias in decision frameworks


This session used three trigger products — a multilingual agricultural advisory tool, a UPI dispute assistant, and Netflix's recommendation engine — to walk through a set of gates before AI even earns a place in a product: is it socially legitimate, does it add real incremental value, is it economically and technically viable, and is there a bounded, monitored learning loop around it. The "nuclear-powered pencil sharpener" line was funny, but the point underneath it is one I don't apply often enough — that just because something is technically buildable doesn't mean it clears the bar of should we build it.
The discussion of dark patterns and switching costs was uncomfortably close to home — I recognized more than one product I use daily in the description of "easy to sign up, deliberately hard to leave." It made me want to go audit our own product's cancellation flow before someone outside the company does it for us in a review.
Naveen's question about bias in his own AI-assisted idea-scoring process, and the answer he got — build a shared rubric with SMEs, but accept that subjectivity shrinks, it never fully disappears — is one I plan to reuse directly. I'd been assuming a scoring model would remove bias from our intake process at work; this session corrected that assumption before I made an expensive mistake acting on it.
