## Personal Reflection

The first session of this course was much more than a simple introduction to AI product management. I initially expected an overview of the syllabus, evaluation structure, and course objectives. While those topics were covered, the session quickly evolved into a deeper discussion about responsibility, decision-making, ownership, and the mindset required to become a successful product manager. This made the class far more engaging and thought-provoking than I anticipated. 【1-8f9b9f】

One idea that immediately stood out to me was the instructor's suggestion that learning should not be viewed as a one-time activity. The recommendation to revisit material multiple times and approach it from different perspectives resonated with me because it reflects my own experiences when learning complex topics. AI and product management are both broad domains that cannot be mastered through a single reading or lecture. The session reminded me that meaningful learning comes from continuous reflection and repeated exposure to ideas. 【1-8f9b9f】

Another important takeaway was the emphasis on maintaining personal reflection notes. Initially, I thought reflections would simply be summaries of each class. However, the instructor clarified that reflections should capture our personal thoughts, disagreements, applications, and insights rather than repeating classroom content. This perspective changed how I approach learning. It encouraged me to engage critically with ideas instead of merely recording them. 【1-8f9b9f】

The discussion around the three dimensions of AI product management was particularly insightful. I learned that AI can be viewed from three different perspectives: using AI to build products, using AI to support product management activities, and embedding AI into products themselves. Before this class, I mostly focused on the third perspective. The session helped me realize that AI influences product management long before a customer interacts with an AI-powered feature. 【1-8f9b9f】

One of the most impactful parts of the class was the discussion about the role of a product manager. Different participants shared their views, describing product managers as coordinators, strategists, enablers, optimizers, and bridges between business and technology. While each viewpoint was valid, the instructor introduced a concept that completely changed my understanding of the role: **a product manager should think like the CEO of the product**. This idea stayed with me because it emphasizes ownership rather than coordination. It means accepting responsibility for outcomes, even when many factors are outside one's direct control. 【1-8f9b9f】

I found this perspective both inspiring and intimidating. Inspiring because it provides a clear sense of ownership, and intimidating because it requires accountability without always having authority. The class highlighted that product managers cannot simply blame engineering, marketing, operations, or external circumstances when things go wrong. They must find ways to navigate challenges, influence stakeholders, and drive results. This was perhaps the most important learning from the session. 【1-8f9b9f】

The discussion around accountability and decision-making also made a strong impression on me. I realized that management is fundamentally about making decisions under uncertainty. Product managers rarely have perfect information. Instead, they evaluate alternatives, make trade-offs, and accept responsibility for the outcomes. The instructor's example of choosing a technically less optimal database because of talent availability clearly demonstrated how practical constraints often shape business decisions. It reminded me that the best technical solution is not always the best business solution. 【1-8f9b9f】

Another valuable insight was understanding the importance of judgment. The instructor explained that management professionals are rewarded not because they are always correct, but because they make better decisions more consistently than others. This simple observation changed how I view management education. The goal is not to memorize frameworks or formulas. Instead, the objective is to improve decision quality and develop sound judgment. 【1-8f9b9f】

I also appreciated the discussion about the challenges of product management. Several participants highlighted the frustrations associated with accountability, conflicting priorities, and limited authority. What resonated with me was the instructor's response that product management can certainly be stressful, but it is also deeply rewarding. The pride associated with creating something meaningful that continues to create value years later was evident in the examples shared during class. This helped me understand why many people remain passionate about product management despite its challenges. 【1-8f9b9f】

One lesson that I found personally relevant was the importance of adaptability and continuous learning. AI is transforming industries at a rapid pace, and resisting change is unlikely to be successful. The session encouraged us to "ride the wave" rather than fear it. As someone interested in technology and AI, this message reinforced my belief that curiosity and continuous learning are essential for long-term growth. 【1-8f9b9f】

The conversation about networking was another takeaway I did not expect. The instructor emphasized that one of the most valuable outcomes of education is the network we build with peers. Looking at the diverse backgrounds of the participants, I realized that learning from classmates can be just as valuable as learning from textbooks or lectures. This encouraged me to participate more actively in discussions and collaborative projects throughout the course. 【1-8f9b9f】

## Key Learnings

- Learning requires multiple passes and continuous reflection.
- Reflection notes should capture personal insights rather than merely summarize content.
- AI influences product management in multiple ways beyond product features.
- A product manager should think like the CEO of the product.
- Ownership and accountability are core responsibilities of product management.
- Decision-making often involves uncertainty and trade-offs.
- Business decisions must balance technical, operational, and strategic factors.
- Good management is largely about improving judgment.
- Product management can be challenging, but it is also highly rewarding.
- Continuous learning and adaptability are critical in the AI era.
- Professional networks are valuable assets for long-term growth.

---

## Personal Takeaway

The biggest lesson I learned from this session is that product management is fundamentally about ownership. I entered the class thinking of product managers as coordinators who connect different teams. I left the session realizing that product managers are responsible for guiding a product's success, making difficult trade-offs, and accepting accountability for outcomes.

The discussion also encouraged me to think beyond technology. Success is not determined by technical expertise alone. It depends on judgment, communication, leadership, and the ability to make decisions under uncertainty. These are skills I hope to develop throughout this course.

Most importantly, I realized that this course is not just about AI products. It is about learning how to think, decide, and lead in environments where change is constant and certainty is rare.

---

## Conclusion

Overall, the first session provided a strong foundation for understanding both AI product management and the mindset required to succeed in the field. It challenged several of my assumptions and introduced a more holistic view of product leadership.

I left the session feeling motivated and curious about the journey ahead. The idea of treating a product manager as the CEO of a product will remain my most important takeaway from this class, and I expect it will shape how I approach future discussions, assignments, and projects throughout the course.
