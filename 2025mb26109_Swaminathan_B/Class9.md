## Personal Reflection

This session was one of the most thought-provoking classes for me because it shifted my attention from building AI solutions to evaluating whether they actually create value. Before attending this class, I often associated success in AI with model accuracy, precision, recall, and other technical metrics. However, the discussion made me realize that good product management goes far beyond technical performance.

A statement that stayed with me throughout the session was that **every product is ultimately a promise of value**. A product may have impressive technology behind it, but customers judge it based on the value they experience. This idea changed the way I think about AI products. Instead of asking whether a model is technically impressive, I should first ask whether it solves a real problem and delivers meaningful value to users.

Another important lesson was the role of experimentation in AI products. I had previously assumed that once a model performs well during testing, its success is almost guaranteed. The class highlighted that AI product development is often built on hypotheses rather than certainty. Product teams must continuously experiment, validate assumptions, and learn from user feedback. This reminded me that innovation comes from testing ideas and adapting rather than assuming we already know the right answer.

I found the discussion on metrics particularly valuable. One concept that stood out was that a high accuracy score does not automatically translate into business success. The banking example used in class clearly showed how a model with excellent accuracy could still create significant financial losses if the cost of wrong decisions is high. This was a powerful reminder that product managers must evaluate AI systems from a business perspective rather than relying solely on technical measurements.

The conversation about economic value helped me understand the importance of connecting AI performance with business outcomes. Earlier, I focused mainly on whether the model was correct or incorrect. Now I realize that every prediction has a cost, a risk, and a business consequence attached to it. Understanding these trade-offs is essential for making responsible product decisions.

The discussion on risk management and tail risks also left a strong impression on me. I learned that average performance can sometimes hide critical problems. A product may appear successful under normal conditions but fail during rare or extreme situations. As a future product manager, I need to think beyond average results and consider what could happen when things go wrong.

Another insight was the importance of resilience. The instructor emphasized that failures and setbacks are inevitable in product development. What matters is the ability to learn, adapt, and recover. This lesson felt relevant not only to AI products but also to personal and professional growth. Success is not determined by avoiding mistakes; it is determined by how effectively we respond to them.

I also found the discussion on A/B testing and experimentation useful. It helped me understand how product improvements should be validated through evidence rather than assumptions. The idea that even experiments can be influenced by bias, changing environments, and external factors made me appreciate the complexity involved in making data-driven decisions.

Towards the end of the session, I reflected on the importance of choosing the right metrics. Metrics influence team behaviour, priorities, and decision-making. If the wrong metrics are selected, people may optimize for numbers instead of actual outcomes. This reinforced the need to design performance measures carefully and ensure they align with long-term product goals.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Every product is a promise of value, not just a collection of features.
- AI product development requires continuous experimentation and validation.
- Business impact matters more than technical accuracy alone.
- Economic value should be considered alongside model performance metrics.
- Tail risks and edge cases can be more important than average results.
- Resilience and adaptability are critical for product success.
- A/B testing helps validate product decisions using evidence.
- Metrics should guide meaningful outcomes rather than encourage superficial optimization.

---

## Conclusion

Overall, this session helped me think more like a product manager and less like someone focused only on technology. I learned that the real challenge is not building an AI model but ensuring that it creates sustainable value while managing risks, costs, and customer expectations. The concepts discussed in this class have given me a broader understanding of AI product management and will influence how I evaluate products, metrics, and business decisions in the future.
