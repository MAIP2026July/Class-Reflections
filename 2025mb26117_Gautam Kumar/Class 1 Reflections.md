### 1. Key Learnings & Takeaways
* **Ownership vs Coordination:** A product manager cannot simply act as an administrative coordinator or backlog organizer. We have to treat ourselves as the overall owner of the product's business and customer outcome.
* **The "Postman" Trap:** If a PM lacks technical depth or refuses to understand architectural trade-offs, they end up just passing messages back and forth between engineering and business stakeholders without creating real value.
* **The Three Pillars of AI:** Distinguishing clearly between:
  1. AI used as a tool to accelerate our internal PM workflow (research, specs, drafting).
  2. AI embedded into the software engineering process.
  3. AI functioning as the core capability delivered to the end customer.
* **Technology is Not Strategy:** AI by itself is just an enabling technology. It only matters if it solves an acute, painful workflow problem that users are willing to pay for or adopt.

### 2. What Resonated Most With Me
What really struck a chord during today's session was Professor Vinaya pointing out that when he asked the room what a PM does, almost everyone gave standard textbook answers—"bridge between business and tech", "roadmap keeper"—and nobody uttered the word **ownership**. 

His analogy of the PM as a mini-CEO who is spending real company capital resonated deeply with my own enterprise experience. In the AI domain especially, where uncertainty is high and token costs or GPU infrastructure can burn cash rapidly, having an owner's mindset means asking hard questions early rather than hiding behind a roadmap. 

I also appreciated his point about learning how to learn. Technology stacks in generative AI are shifting every few months, so holding onto rigid frameworks is less useful than cultivating sound product judgment.

### 3. Constructive Thoughts & What Could Be Explored Further
* The session moved at a very energetic pace. While the orientation set high expectations, it would be wonderful in future sessions if we could spend 10 minutes dissecting one quick, real-world example of an AI product that failed specifically because the PM acted like a "postman" rather than an owner. Seeing what that anti-pattern looks like in practice would make the lesson even sharper.
