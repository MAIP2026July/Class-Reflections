#Class Reflection — 25 July 2026
One thought from the first class that stayed with me was that, in management, there may not always be one single correct answer. Coming from a technical background, I am more used to situations where something either builds or does not build, boots or does not boot, passes a test or fails it. Product decisions are different because two people can take different decisions based on different assumptions and both can still have valid reasoning.

This made me think about my current work. During BSP development or customer support, my natural tendency is to first look for the technically correct solution. But as a lead, I also need to think about effort, customer impact, release schedule, reuse, supportability and business priority. A technically perfect solution may not always be the right product decision.

Another useful point for me was the need to be concise. I usually prefer to give complete technical details so that nothing is misunderstood. But in product management and leadership, too much information can sometimes hide the actual decision. I need to improve in separating the decision, reason, evidence and next action from supporting technical details.

My takeaway from this class is that I should not move away from my technical strength, but I need to add a different layer of thinking on top of it: What decision am I taking, what assumptions am I making, and what outcome am I expecting?
