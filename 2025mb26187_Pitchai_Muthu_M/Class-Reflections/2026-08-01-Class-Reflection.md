#Class Reflection — 1 August 2026

The idea that connected most with me in this class was seeing the product lifecycle as a managed learning loop rather than a straight development flow.

In embedded software, I have often seen a release treated as an end point: development is complete, QA is completed, image is released, and the next activity starts. But when I look at our real work, it is actually a loop. A BSP release goes to internal teams or customers, we receive feedback, hardware combinations expose new behaviours, additional peripherals are requested, validation finds gaps, and those learnings come back into the next release.

So Discover → Define → Strategize → Plan → Build → Launch → Grow → Renew/Retire is not only a product-management diagram for me. I can relate it directly to Android, Yocto or QNX releases.

The project-versus-product thinking also made me reflect. In customer support, one customer may request a particular customization. My engineering instinct may be to solve it. But as a product owner, I should first ask whether this is only for this customer or whether the same requirement can become a reusable capability for other customers/platforms.

My practical takeaway is to start asking two questions after major customer requests or releases: What did we learn? and What part of this learning should become reusable product capability?
