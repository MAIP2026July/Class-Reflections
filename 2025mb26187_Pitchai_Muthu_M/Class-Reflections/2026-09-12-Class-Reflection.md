#Class Reflection — 12 September 2026

This class changed the way I was thinking about both the subject and the exam.

Until this point, I was spending more effort remembering frameworks and terminology. The professor's explanation about case questions made me realize that knowing the name of a framework is only the beginning. In a case, I need to actually take the decision and show how I would implement it.

This is also true in my work. If somebody asks me how I would improve software quality, saying “we should have better validation, automation and monitoring” is correct but not very useful. A stronger answer is to say exactly what I will validate, who owns it, what threshold I will use, what happens when validation fails, and what evidence is required before release.

The quick discussion of AI techniques was useful for another reason. I don't need to become a data scientist, but as someone taking product decisions I should at least identify whether the problem is classification, regression, recommendation, computer vision, generative AI, etc. Otherwise, I may not even ask the right technical questions.

The reflection I am taking forward is: don't hide behind general statements. Whether in an exam, customer meeting or internal review, I should move from “this should be done” to “this is what I will do, for this situation, and this is why.”
