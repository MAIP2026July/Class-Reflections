#Class Reflection — 3 October 2026

For me, this class had two different learnings.

The first one was around automation. The statement “learn before we automate” made a lot of sense to me. In software teams, automation is normally seen as a positive thing. But automating a weak or poorly understood process can simply make the wrong action happen faster and at a larger scale.

I can connect this to build, flash, validation and release activities. Initially, keeping a human checkpoint may look inefficient, but that checkpoint can give us useful learning about exceptions and failure patterns. Once we have enough evidence and confidence, more of the flow can be automated. So I now see Human-in-the-Loop not only as a safety mechanism but also as a temporary learning mechanism while the product matures.

The second reflection came from the midterm discussion. Like many in the class, I also felt that some terminology in the exam was difficult to connect immediately with what I had studied. The discussion made me realize that in real work too, customers, architects or specialists may use terminology I have never seen before. I cannot know every term. What I need is the ability to return to the foundation: What is the actual problem? What decision is needed? What is the objective?

That was probably my strongest learning from the session: technical vocabulary will keep changing, but good problem framing and decision thinking remain useful.
