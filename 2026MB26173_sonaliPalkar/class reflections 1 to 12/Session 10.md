## **MANAGEMENT OF AI PRODUCTS** 

# **Session 10 – Operating AI Products: Cost, Controls and Release Discipline** 

The tenth session highlighted the operational responsibilities involved in managing AI products after the initial idea and prototype stages. One of the strongest messages was that AI usage has a real and growing cost. Product teams cannot assume that model calls or agent activity are insignificant expenses. They need to understand where AI is being used, how frequently it is invoked and whether the selected model provides sufficient value for its cost. 

I learned that choosing an AI model involves trade-offs among cost, accuracy and latency. A more capable frontier model may provide stronger results but can increase operating expenses, while a smaller or less expensive model may be sufficient for simpler tasks. The right choice depends on the use case and customer expectations. Product managers should therefore define acceptable boundaries and decision rules rather than leaving every model or infrastructure choice to ad hoc judgement. 

The discussion on operational controls was also valuable. Teams need repeatable deployment practices, monitoring and alerting so that problems can be detected before they affect customers. Model versions and changes should be tracked, and teams should understand what can be changed independently and what requires escalation. The reference to semantic versioning made me realise that AI products need disciplined change management, while also recognising that software versioning alone may not capture every change in model behaviour. 

Another important learning was that governance must grow with the use of agents and automation. As the number of agents increases, the organisation needs clear visibility into what they can do, what limits apply and when human intervention is required. Product managers also need to consider team capacity and protect people from unrealistic workloads. Strong controls should support reliable delivery without creating unnecessary process. 

My personal takeaway is that launching an AI product is only the beginning. Sustained value depends on managing cost, monitoring performance, controlling changes and preparing teams to respond when something goes wrong. I would apply this by defining operational thresholds and escalation paths early, so that the product remains reliable, economically viable and accountable as it scales.
