## **MANAGEMENT OF AI PRODUCTS** 

# **Session 12 – AI Product Monetisation, Pricing and Ecosystem Strategy** 

The twelfth session helped me connect product design with the commercial decisions required to make an AI product sustainable. I learned that pricing should begin with the value perceived by the customer, rather than simply adding a margin to the cost of development. A product manager needs to understand what each stakeholder is actually trying to achieve, how frequently the solution will be used and how much value it creates. Different stakeholders may value the same product in different ways, so requirements and benefits should be stakeholderspecific. 

The discussion on pricing models was particularly useful. Options can include subscription, usage-based, hybrid, fixed-price or outcome-based approaches. The right model depends on customer usage patterns, willingness to pay, the predictability of demand and the risk shared between provider and customer. For example, a customer may prefer paying for a measurable outcome, while a provider may prefer a timeand-material model because it carries less delivery risk. A fair model must balance customer value with sustainable profitability. 

I also understood that AI product economics require careful model and workflow choices. Cost, accuracy, latency and trust can pull in different directions. A premium model may be more accurate but expensive, while a lower-cost model may be suitable for less critical tasks. The number of requests, prompt size, how tasks are grouped and how the system handles incomplete results can all influence cost and customer experience. These are not purely technical decisions; they affect the product promise and commercial model. 

The session also introduced the value of workflow diagrams and diagramming-as-code tools such as PlantUML and Mermaid. Swimlane diagrams can show responsibilities and parallel activities, helping teams communicate how a product works across stakeholders. Finally, the discussion on ecosystems highlighted that products compete not only as individual offerings but also through the platforms and integrations around them. Compatibility, interoperability, bundling and partnerships can influence adoption and make a product more valuable. 

My personal takeaway is that monetisation must be designed alongside the product, not added at the end. I should understand the value delivered to each stakeholder, select a pricing model that reflects usage and outcomes, and ensure the economics are sustainable. The session reinforced that a strong AI product combines customer value, sound unit economics and a clear position within its wider ecosystem.
