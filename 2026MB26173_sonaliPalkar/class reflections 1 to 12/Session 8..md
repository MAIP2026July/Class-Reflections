## **MANAGEMENT OF AI PRODUCTS** 

# **Session 9 – AI Experimentation, Model Evaluation and Product-Market Fit** 

The ninth session helped me understand why experimentation is central to AI product management. Many AI product ideas begin as hypotheses about whether a particular technique, model or combination of steps can solve a problem better. The product team must test these assumptions rather than treat them as facts. Experimentation helps establish whether a solution works technically and whether the improvement is meaningful to the intended customer segment. 

An important distinction from the session was between validating the product itself and validating its market fit. A team may demonstrate that a feature works, is faster or is more user-friendly, but that alone does not guarantee commercial success. The size and reach of the target market, the competitive alternatives and the way the value is communicated also influence adoption. A technically successful product can still struggle if customers do not perceive enough value compared with other available options. 

The discussion on classification outcomes and the confusion matrix was particularly useful. Accuracy by itself can be misleading because different errors have different business consequences. In a lending example, incorrectly rejecting a creditworthy applicant can mean lost business, while approving an applicant who later defaults can create a financial loss. The product manager must understand the cost of false positives and false negatives and select performance measures that reflect the real business objective. A model with a high accuracy score may still be unsuitable if it makes the more costly type of error too often. 

I also took away that experimentation with AI can be less predictable when the product depends on an external generative AI provider. The underlying model or service may change, which means a test that was successful earlier may produce different results later. Product teams need ongoing evaluation and should not assume that a previously validated result will remain stable indefinitely. 

My personal takeaway is that AI product decisions should be evidence-led and tied to business outcomes. I should define the hypothesis, choose metrics that reflect the cost of errors, test the solution with the intended stakeholders and separately assess whether there is a viable market. The session reinforced that product success is not just model performance; it is the combination of technical effectiveness, customer value and competitive relevance.
