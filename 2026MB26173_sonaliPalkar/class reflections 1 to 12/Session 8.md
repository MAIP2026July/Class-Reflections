## **MANAGEMENT OF AI PRODUCTS** 

# **Session 8 – AI-Led Organisational Reimagination, Fairness and Explainability** 

The eighth session broadened my understanding of AI beyond individual product features. One important idea was that AI should be treated as an enabler of organisational reimagination, not as the end goal. Organisations need to decide where AI can transform an entire activity, where it can support only selected parts of a process and where integration with existing systems or human expertise remains more appropriate. This requires understanding the business problem before selecting the technology. 

I found the discussion on fairness and explainability especially relevant. In areas such as insurance, lending and other regulated decisions, it is not enough for an AI system to produce an output. The organisation may also need to explain why a decision was made to customers, internal stakeholders and regulators. If a decision affects someone’s access to a service or opportunity, the product must be designed to provide an appropriate explanation and a way to question or review the outcome. 

The session also made me think about the difference between building a technically capable solution and designing a usable product workflow. The product manager does not need to personally build every technical component, but must understand how the solution will be used, what information it relies on and whether the output meets the expectations of the people involved. For knowledge-based solutions, for example, retrieving the right source material is only part of the challenge; the system must also provide relevant, current and contextually appropriate information. 

Another learning was the importance of learning through real cases and practical application. Concepts become clearer when they are connected to situations where decisions have consequences. AI product management therefore requires more than familiarity with tools: it requires the ability to question assumptions, understand stakeholder expectations and evaluate the impact of a proposed solution. 

My personal reflection is that responsible AI begins at the problem-definition stage. Fairness, explainability and the ability to justify decisions should not be added only after development. They should influence the product design and workflow from the beginning. Overall, the session reinforced that AI-led transformation is valuable only when it improves organisational outcomes while maintaining trust and accountability.
