#Class Reflection — 18 August 2026

This session changed the way I look at frameworks.
Earlier, whenever I saw frameworks such as JTBD, SWOT, RICE, QFD, RACI or other decision tools, I tended to think that selecting the correct framework itself was important. What I understood from this class is that the framework is only an instrument; the product manager still owns the decision.

This connects strongly with my work. In engineering, we also have processes, SOPs, checklists, release gates and validation templates. They are useful because they improve consistency, but following all of them does not automatically mean we made the right decision. There are situations where the process says everything is okay, but experience tells us that one unresolved technical risk can still create a customer issue.

I also liked the discussion around traceability and evidence. As a team lead, many decisions are made through discussions with development, QA, HQ or customers. After some months it can become difficult to remember why we selected a particular approach. Keeping a small decision record—what we decided, why, based on what evidence, and what assumption we made—can be more useful than depending on memory.

My takeaway is that I should use frameworks to improve my judgment, not replace my judgment. If two frameworks are asking the same question and not changing the decision, using both may only add process overhead.
